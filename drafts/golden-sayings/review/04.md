# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · Sayings LXIX–LXXXIII
Grade: B
The long LXXXI dialogue is cut exactly as the concordance says and reads well; LXXIV matches the Lectio Meditations word for word. LXXXIII does not, and one sentence in LXXI stalls aloud.

## Issues
- `LXXXIII` · fidelity (shared line) · medium
  > No one can rob us of our will
  Marcus quotes this (Meditations XI.36, λῃστὴς προαιρέσεως οὐ γίνεται) and the Lectio Meditations prints "No one can rob us of our free will." The policy is that a shared line matches; "free" is the Meditations' word, not the Greek's, and clashes with this book's "the will".
  Fix: No one can rob us of our free will; no one can be its tyrant. (Editor: the cleaner fix is to change the Meditations to "our will" and keep this line as drafted.)
- `LXXI` · read-aloud · medium
  > From here, out of your mind, throw out, in place of Procrustes and Sciron, grief, fear,
  Three openers before the verb's object arrives; the commas stop the voice four times. Greek ἐντεῦθεν ἐκ τῆς διανοίας ἔκβαλε ἀντὶ Προκρούστου καὶ Σκίρωνος λύπην…
  Fix: Throw out of your own mind, in place of Procrustes and Sciron, grief, fear,

## Best lines
- LXXIII: "Man, you are a god; you have great aims."
- LXXVIII: "Show me a man who is sick and happy, in danger and happy, dying and happy, exiled and happy, in disgrace and happy."
