# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · Sayings CXXXVI–CLVII
Grade: B
The freedom sayings from Book IV are among the most faithful in the draft (the hindered/impeded and will/outside-the-will terms hold exactly). Three phrases misdirect a reader.

## Issues
- `CLIV` · fidelity · medium
  > I ate well for myself
  Now reads as "I ate good food". Greek ἤσθιον ἐμαυτῷ καλῶς: he ate as a man should, decently, and for his own sake.
  Fix: I ate properly, for myself
- `CLVI` · fidelity · low
  > do not give yourself up
  Sounds like surrender to someone; and the next clause already says "surrender completely". Greek μὴ ἀπογνῷς σεαυτοῦ, "do not despair of yourself".
  Fix: do not despair of yourself
- `CLIII` · read-aloud · low
  > so I rejoice day by day in following my own improvement
  "Following" is ambiguous (pursuing or watching). Greek παρακολουθῶν ἐμαυτῷ βελτίονι γινομένῳ, "watching myself become better".
  Fix: so I rejoice day by day in watching myself grow better

## Best lines
- CXXXIX: "But the festival has an end. Leave; go away grateful and self-respecting. Make room for others."
- CLV: "You have flowered sooner than you should, and the winter will scorch you."
