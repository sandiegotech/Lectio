# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · The Hymn of Cleanthes
Grade: B
A plain, faithful prose hymn from the Greek, free of Crossley's diction and line numbers. Its one weak point is the two lines the cached Greek lacks, which the draft supplies from memory.

## Issues
- `stanza 3` · fidelity · medium (unverified, no change proposed)
  > Instead they rush off without sense, each after a different thing: some in a quarrelsome zeal for fame,
  These are lines 26–27 of the standard text, missing from the cached Wikisource copy (it runs straight from line 25 to αὐτοὶ δ' ἐπὶ κερδοσύνας). The drafter rendered them from memory. "Without sense" presupposes the reading ἄνευ νόου; editions also print ἄνευ καλοῦ ("without regard for the noble"). Neither this reviewer nor the drafter can settle it without a full text (von Arnim, SVF I.537, or Thom's edition). Keep as drafted until checked.

## Best lines
- stanza 2: "such is the servant you hold in your unconquerable hands, the two-edged, fiery, ever-living thunderbolt."
- stanza 3: "But you know how to make the odd even, and to bring order to what is without order; and what is unloved is loved by you."
