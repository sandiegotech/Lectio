# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · Sayings CLVIII–CLXXXIX
Grade: B
The Enchiridion sayings are exact and plain; CLXXXIV carries the fixed wording of Cleanthes, Euripides and the Crito lines; the Stobaean maxims (CLXXVIII–CLXXX) check against Schenkl's Gnomologium. Two small things only.

## Issues
- `CLXXXVIII` · form · low
  > proclaimed not by Caesar (for how could he proclaim it?) but by God through reason
  The style sheet admits no brackets in a paragraph; the aside is Epictetus's own, so it stays, set off with the edition's dashes.
  Fix: proclaimed not by Caesar—for how could he proclaim it?—but by God through reason
- `CLXXXVI` · read-aloud · low
  > What do I still care?
  Not idiomatic aloud. Greek τί οὖν ἔτι μοι μέλει;
  Fix: What does it matter to me now?

## Best lines
- CLXXXVI: "I must come like the hour, and like an hour pass."
- CLXXXVIII: "There is no Hades, no Acheron, no Cocytus, no Pyriphlegethon; everything is full of gods and divine powers."
