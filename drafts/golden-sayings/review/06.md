# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · Sayings CX–CXXXV
Grade: B
The Cynic sayings are faithful and forceful, "scout" and "the recall" are carried through, and CXXX keeps the Lectio Meditations' "words of bad omen". One phrase invites the opposite meaning; the rest is form and polish.

## Issues
- `CXV` · fidelity · medium
  > Question the divine.
  Reads as "doubt God". Greek ἀνάκρινον τὸ δαιμόνιον: consult, inquire of the divine, as one consults an oracle before acting.
  Fix: Consult the divine.
- `CXIX` · fidelity · low
  > When he sees that he has kept watch over mankind
  Greek ὅταν ἴδῃς, "when you see": the observer, not the Cynic, sees it; Crossley's "when he knows" was followed.
  Fix: When you see that he has kept watch over mankind
- `CX` · form · low
  > "We will look into it at leisure," he said. "But this much I can tell you: … says to himself, 'I ought to be the manager.' … 'You are the sun. …'"
  The style sheet: after Arrian's "he said" a speech of Epictetus is not quoted (as XXXIV, XXXVI, XC do); CX alone wraps the whole speech in quotation marks and pushes its inner quotations to single marks.
  Fix: "… should be. He said: We will look into it at leisure. But this much I can tell you: …" with the inner quotations in double marks and no closing mark.
- `CXXI` · register · low
  > Men, the philosopher's school is a doctor's office.
  ἄνδρες addressing the class; the style sheet fixes this as "gentlemen".
  Fix: Gentlemen, the philosopher's school is a doctor's office.
- `CXXX` · read-aloud · low
  > Going abroad is this, and a small change. Death is this, a greater change
  "And" breaks the parallel the Greek draws (τοῦτ' ἔστιν ἀποδημία, καὶ μεταβολὴ μικρά· τοῦτο θάνατος, μεταβολὴ μείζων).
  Fix: Going abroad is this: a small change. Death is this: a greater change
- `CXXXIII` · form (extent) · low
  > that you are carrying out the part of a good and noble man not in word but in deed
  Crossley ends at "obedience to God"; the Lectio completes the Greek sentence. Same pattern as CXXIV (which has its note). Editor's call; no change proposed.

## Best lines
- CXI: "This is his house, this is his door, this is the guard at his bedroom, this is his darkness."
- CXXXIV: "I obey, I follow, speaking well of my commander and singing hymns to his works."
