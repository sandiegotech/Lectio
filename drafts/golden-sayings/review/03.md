# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · Sayings LIII–LXVIII
Grade: B
A strong section: the long dialogues (LXIV, LXVIII) run cleanly and the Greek is followed where Crossley emends. One inconsistency of term and one extent question.

## Issues
- `LVIII` · fidelity · low
  > call on the god and beg him
  The same ὁ θεός is "God" two sentences later ("what seems good to God?"); the style sheet fixes ὁ θεός as "God". "The god" makes the second line point at a different being.
  Fix: call on God and beg him
- `LIX` · form (extent) · low
  > Flesh? Far from it. Land? Far from it. Fame? Far from it.
  Crossley skips these three questions (2.8.2) and the concordance records no gap; the style sheet says that where he skipped, the Lectio skips. The Greek is rendered correctly. Editor's call whether the whole-sentence rule covers this; no change proposed.
- `LIII` · read-aloud · low
  > will you make out that it is the man who is saying these things to you, so as to stay ignorant of the power of the divine: that it gives signs to some in this way and to others in that, and that on the greatest and most important matters it gives its sign through the noblest messenger?
  Long, but it is one indignant question in the Greek and it carries if read with the colon as a pause. Kept; noted for the read-aloud pass.

## Best lines
- LX: "You carry God about with you, poor wretch, and you do not know it."
- LXI: "No, but one who dies like a god, who bears sickness like a god."
