# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · Sayings XXVI–LII
Grade: B
Faithful and in voice; the shared lines (Anytus and Meletus, "If this is what pleases God") match the style sheet. One knotted sentence in XXIX and a few small things.

## Issues
- `XXIX` · read-aloud · medium
  > But for me to want, at random, whatever I happen to fancy to come about
  The infinitive chain ("to want … to fancy to come about") cannot be followed aloud. Greek has two parallel clauses: τὸ δ' ὡς ἔτυχέν με βούλεσθαι τὰ δ' ὡς ἔτυχεν δόξαντα γίνεσθαι.
  Fix: But that I should want things at random, and that whatever I happen to fancy should come about
- `XXVII` · register · low
  > "I have lost by it."
  "Lost by it" is a dated idiom. Greek ἐζημίωμαι, "I have suffered loss".
  Fix: "I have suffered a loss."
- `XLI` · read-aloud · low
  > it looks as if you were a slave yourself first
  Tense blurs; Greek αὐτὸς ὑπάρχειν πρότερον ἔοικας δοῦλος (Schenkl, Gnom. 36), "you seem to have been a slave first yourself".
  Fix: you seem to have been a slave yourself first

## Best lines
- XLV: "And as for the last little tunic, which is this poor body, beyond that no one has any power over me."
- XLIII: "If you want a crown at all, take one of roses and put it on. You will look smarter in it."
