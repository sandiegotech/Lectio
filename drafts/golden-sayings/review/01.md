# The Golden Sayings of Epictetus · Sayings I–XXV
Grade: B
Close to the Greek throughout and Crossley's extents kept; one line shared with Marcus does not match the Lectio Meditations, and a few long periods need breaking for the voice.

## Issues
- `VII` · fidelity · medium
  > to do good and to be abused for it
  Marcus quotes the same line of Antisthenes (Meditations VII.36), and the Lectio Meditations prints "It is kingly to do good and to be spoken ill of." Greek κακῶς δ' ἀκούειν has no "for it".
  Fix: It is kingly, Cyrus, to do good and to be spoken ill of.
- `IX` · read-aloud · medium
  > "For what am I? A miserable little man." "This wretched flesh of mine."
  One utterance in the Greek (τί γὰρ εἰμί; ταλαίπωρον ἀνθρωπάριον καὶ τὰ δύστηνά μου σαρκίδια); two quoted pieces read as two speakers.
  Fix: "For what am I? A miserable little man, and this wretched flesh of mine."
- `XVI` · read-aloud · medium
  > Take the man who has studied the way the universe is governed, and has learned that the greatest
  The opening period runs some ninety words before its main verb arrives; aloud, the listener loses "Take the man".
  Fix: Take the man who has studied the way the universe is governed. He has learned that the greatest…
- `XVII` · read-aloud · medium
  > who, once they have recognized their kinship with the gods, and seen that we have these chains fastened on us, … would want to throw them off
  Fifty words hang between "who" and "would want"; the voice cannot carry the suspension.
  Fix: who recognize their kinship with the gods, and see that we have these chains fastened on us, … and then want to throw them off
- `VI` · fidelity · low
  > "I pray to the gods that I may be."
  Greek εὔχομαι δὲ τοῖς θεοῖς; "that I may be" explains the answer into the text.
  Fix: "I pray to the gods."
- `XII` · fidelity · low
  > the thief was better than I am at staying awake
  Greek κρείσσων ἦν, past: he was the better at it that night.
  Fix: the thief was better than I was at staying awake
- `XII` · form (extent) · low
  > for a lamp he became like a wild beast
  Crossley stops at "faithless"; the Lectio completes the Greek sentence (ἀντὶ λύχνου θηριώδης). Defensible under the style sheet's whole-sentence rule, but it runs past Crossley's end. Editor's call; no change proposed.
- `XI` · read-aloud · low
  > had felt something not hard to understand
  Double negative trips aloud; Greek ἔπαθέν τι … οὐκ ἀπίθανον.
  Fix: had felt something natural enough
- `XIV` · read-aloud · low
  > Do you not bathe badly?
  Odd English. Greek οὐ κακῶς λούεσθε; keep the run of "Are you not".
  Fix: Are you not badly off for a bath?
- `XIV` · read-aloud · low
  > what do I still care about anything that can happen?
  Fix: why should I still care about anything that can happen?
- `I` · read-aloud · low
  > and we should sing the greatest and most divine hymn too, that he has given us
  The comma makes "that" read as a relative; it introduces the hymn's content.
  Fix: and we should sing the greatest and most divine hymn too: that he has given us

## Best lines
- XIX: "The teacher is a corpse, and you are corpses."
- VIII: "But I want to be the purple, that small and shining part which makes all the rest look fine and beautiful."
