# The Trial and Death of Socrates · Phaedo · The Final Argument (95a–107b)
Grade: B
The strongest of the four sections, clause by clause. Its fixed terms hold: largeness, admit, withdraw, stand its ground, bring along. Burnet's deletion of γίγνεται at 100d–e is not followed, and two sentences trip when read aloud. Otherwise ready to ship.

## Issues
- `p-027` · fidelity · low · *Phaedo* 100d–e
  > it is by the beautiful that all beautiful things become beautiful … that it is by the beautiful that beautiful things become beautiful
  Burnet brackets γίγνεται both times (the Perseus file marks it `<del>`), so the safe answer is about being: τῷ καλῷ τὰ καλὰ καλά. "Become" brings in a coming-to-be that Burnet's text does not have.
  Fix: all beautiful things are beautiful … beautiful things are beautiful

- `p-002` · read-aloud · low · 95a
  > This argument against the attunement, at any rate, you made astonishingly, beyond what I expected.
  "Made astonishingly" is not English. The Greek has θαυμαστῶς μοι εἶπες ὡς παρὰ δόξαν.
  Fix: you put astonishingly well, beyond what I expected

- `p-068` · read-aloud · low · 103e
  > The odd must surely always get this name that we are now speaking
  "Speak a name" is not English.
  Fix: this name that we are now using
