# The Trial and Death of Socrates · Phaedo · Two Objections (84c–95a)
Grade: B
Faithful and lively: the swan song, the frame and the warning against hatred of argument all hold. To fix: one sentence in Cebes' objection that a reader cannot parse, one bracketed phrase that is translated anyway, and the name of the Eleven.

## Issues
- `p-014` · read-aloud · medium · *Phaedo* 88a
  > he no longer conceded that it does not suffer in its many births, and at the end perish altogether in one of its deaths
  The second verb, "perish", has lost its construction, and the double negative (μὴ οὐ after a negated verb of granting) cannot be followed aloud.
  Fix: he would not go on to concede that it does not suffer in its many births, and does not at the end perish altogether in one of its deaths

- `p-009` · fidelity · low · 86e
  > What was it that troubled you in your turn and makes you disbelieve?
  Burnet brackets [ἀπιστίαν παρέχει]. The Perseus file marks it `<del>`.
  Fix: What was it that troubled you in your turn?

- `p-004` · register (names) · low · 85b
  > for as long as the eleven men of Athens allow it
  Ἀθηναίων … ἄνδρες ἕνδεκα means the Eleven. STYLE §5 and §6 capitalize the Eleven, and the death scene says "the officer of the Eleven".
  Fix: for as long as the Eleven of Athens allow it

- `p-031` · fidelity · low · 90a
  > that the very good and the very bad are few
  The Greek has χρηστοὺς καὶ πονηροὺς σφόδρα ὀλίγους, and σφόδρα may go with "few" ("both very few") rather than with "good" and "bad". Uncertain.
  Fix (if it holds): that the good and the bad are both very few

- `p-102`–`p-103` · read-aloud · low · 94e
  > … and not the kind to lead them and master them, being itself something far more divine than an attunement? / By Zeus, Socrates, I think so.
  Simmias' "I think so" seems to agree that Homer took the soul for an attunement. The Greek (ἀλλ' οὐχ … ; ἔμοιγε δοκεῖ) is just as loose, and the reader settles it from what follows. Jowett avoids the problem by recasting the question with "or rather". Left for the editor.

- `p-009` · fidelity · low · 86d
  > Socrates looked straight at us
  διαβλέψας has no object. "At us" is added.
