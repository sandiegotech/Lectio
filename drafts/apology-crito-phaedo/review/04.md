# The Trial and Death of Socrates · Crito
Grade: B
A clean, well-paced dialogue and a strong speech of the Laws; one line given to the wrong speaker, one lost repetition, and contractions in long turns.

Checked clause by clause against Burnet (43a–54e), Jowett as control. The cached Greek's speaker labels are unreliable (for example {ΚΡ.} at 44e and {ΣΩ.} at 46e, 50e, 52a, 52e); speakers were checked from the sense and against Jowett.

## Issues
- `p-062` / `p-063` · 48b · form · medium
  > CRITO: That is clear too. Someone might well say it, Socrates. That's true.
  In Burnet (and Jowett) ἀληθῆ λέγεις is Socrates' reply, opening his next turn ("And it is true; but still…"). Given to Crito, it has him agree with himself.
  Fix: p-062 ends "…Someone might well say it, Socrates."; p-063 begins "SOCRATES: That's true. But, my dear friend, …"

- `p-033` · 45c · fidelity · medium
  > giving yourself up when you could be saved
  σαυτὸν προδοῦναι, "betray yourself": Crito uses the same verb two sentences later of the sons (προδιδόναι, "betraying your own sons"); "giving yourself up" softens the charge and breaks the repetition.
  Fix: betraying yourself when you could be saved

- `p-035`, `p-051`, `p-053` · 46e–47e · register · low
  > doesn't that seem to you well said … Doesn't it seem … Isn't that well said … isn't it the same … the body, isn't it?
  Contractions in long Socratic turns (230, 121, 51 words); STYLE §2 allows them only in turns of about 25 words.
  Fix: does that not seem / Does it not seem / Is that not well said / is it not the same / is it not?

- `p-034` · 46c · voice · low
  > with more bogeys than it has now
  μορμολύττηται: the noun is fixed as "bogeymen" (STYLE §11, Phaedo 77e); the two passages should match.
  Fix: with more bogeymen than it has now

- `p-096` · 53a · register · low
  > the lame, the blind and the other cripples
  ἀνάπηροι, "the maimed": "cripples" now reads as a slur to many readers.
  Fix: the lame, the blind and the other maimed

- `p-097` · 53d · register · low
  > a leather jerkin
  διφθέρα, a rustic's leather cloak: "jerkin" is a word few readers under sixty carry.
  Fix: a peasant's leather cloak

- `p-002` · 43a · voice · low
  > CRITO: Very early.
  πάνυ μὲν οὖν is fixed as "Very much so" (STYLE §7).
  Fix: none proposed; see second look.

## For the editor
52b: ὅτι μὴ ἅπαξ εἰς Ἰσθμόν ("except once to the Isthmus") is, I believe, bracketed in Burnet's OCT (Athenaeus quotes the passage without it); the cached Greek shows no bracket and the draft prints it. 49a: the draft leaves out ὅπερ καὶ ἄρτι ἐλέγετο, which Burnet also brackets, so the draft is inconsistent if it prints the Isthmus clause. Not applied: decide from the printed page and, if the clause goes, record it in `divergences`.
