# The Trial and Death of Socrates — style sheet

Written before the first section. Every drafter and reviewer reads this first. It agrees with
the Policies of the Lectio *Republic* (`review/2026-11-17/republic/rejected.md`); where it
adds to them, the addition is for these three dialogues only.

## 1. Method

- Draft from Burnet's Greek (`pipeline/.cache/burnet-*-grc.txt`). Jowett is a control on
  meaning and nothing more. Where Jowett softens, expands, skips or explains, follow the Greek.
- Nothing is printed that Plato did not write: no brackets, no parentheses of explanation, no
  "that is", no gloss after a name, no summary, no moral. Parentheses and asides Plato wrote
  stay, set with dashes. A crux goes in `divergences`.
- Nothing Plato wrote is left out. Every clause of the Greek has its clause in the English,
  including the small ones (ὡς ἔπος εἰπεῖν, ἴσως, οἶμαι, the vocatives).
- A formula is rendered the same way every time (section 6).

## 2. Register

Plain, current, never fashionable; readable from fifteen to ninety. American spelling
(defense, honor, judgment, toward). No word that dates the text or brings in an office or a
television courtroom: not "jury", "defendant", "verdict of guilty", "lawyer", "okay", "guys",
"literally", "basically", "absolutely" as an answer, "agenda", "process", "issue" for
problem, "impact" as a verb. Equally no "thee", "ye", "nay", "methinks", "whence", "wherefore",
"O Athenians", "yonder", "forthwith".

- **Contractions** only in short exchanges of dialogue (a turn of about 25 words or fewer),
  and not where the stress falls on the verb ("It is.", "I do."). The Apology is a speech to
  a court and is **uncontracted**, except in the short questions and answers with Meletus
  (24c–28a). Long speeches in the Crito (the Laws) and the Phaedo (every argument, the myth,
  Phaedo's narration) are uncontracted. The death scene is uncontracted.
- Sentences may be long where Plato's are, but each must be sayable in one breath of sense.
  Break a Greek period into two or three English sentences rather than stack clauses. Do not
  add connectives Plato does not have; do not drop his γάρ, οὖν, δέ where the logic needs them.
- "Man" and "men" where the Greek has ἀνήρ; "human being", "person", "people", "anyone" where
  it has ἄνθρωπος or τις. ὦ ἄνθρωπε as an address is "my friend".

## 3. Speakers and layout

Matches the Lectio *Republic*: speech is set **without quotation marks**; each change of
speaker is a new paragraph; a quoted or imagined speech inside a longer speech runs on after
a colon with a capital letter (Perhaps someone might say: Are you not ashamed, Socrates …).

- **Apology.** One speaker. The exchange with Meletus is set as alternating paragraphs with no
  speaker labels, as the Greek has none.
- **Narrated dialogue** (the Phaedo inside the frame): as in the *Republic*, attribution in the
  narration, "said Cebes", "he said", "I said" (Phaedo is "I"). Where the Greek has no ἔφη and
  the speaker is clear from the alternation, no attribution is added.
- **Direct drama** (the whole Crito; the Phaedo's frame at 57a–59c, 88c–89a and 102a): the *Republic* has no direct drama, so here each speech opens with the
  speaker's name in capitals and a colon, as in Jowett's base text: `CRITO: `, `SOCRATES: `,
  `ECHECRATES: `, `PHAEDO: `. Once Phaedo's narration is under way, its paragraphs carry no
  label; the label returns only when Echecrates breaks in, and for Phaedo's first paragraph
  after each break. The last sentence (118a) is addressed to Echecrates from inside the
  narration, like 117b, and takes no label.
- No stage directions, no "PERSONS OF THE DIALOGUE", no scene line.
- Paragraphs follow the sense. A long speech is broken where the Greek turns (εἶεν, ἀλλὰ γάρ,
  a new head of argument), not at a fixed length.

## 4. Punctuation and characters

- Straight apostrophe `'` in contractions and possessives. Possessive of a name in -s: Socrates',
  Meletus', Cebes', Simmias', Anytus'; but Zeus's.
- Quoted **verse**, a quoted **document** (the two sworn charges in the Apology), a quoted
  inscription or proverb, and a word mentioned as a word take typographic single quotes
  `‘ ’`. Verse runs inline as prose, with no line breaks and no slashes. No double quotation
  marks anywhere.
- Dash: spaced em dash ` — `. No semicolon chains of more than two.
- No italics, no markdown, no brackets, no ellipsis.
- Numbers in words. Money and measures stay Greek: drachma, mina, stade.

## 5. Names

Latinized, as in the *Republic*: Socrates, Meletus, Anytus, Lycon, Chaerephon, Crito,
Critobulus, Apollodorus, Aeschines, Antisthenes, Hermogenes, Epigenes, Ctesippus, Menexenus,
Phaedo, Echecrates, Simmias, Cebes, Phaedondes, Euclides, Terpsion, Aristippus, Cleombrotus,
Xanthippe, Evenus, Philolaus, Anaxagoras, Aristophanes, Gorgias of Leontini, Prodicus of Ceos,
Hippias of Elis, Callias son of Hipponicus, Leon of Salamis, Theseus, Aesop, Penelope, Iolaus,
Heracles, Endymion, Harmonia, Cadmus, Odysseus, Homer, Hesiod, Orpheus, Musaeus, Minos,
Rhadamanthus, Aeacus, Triptolemus, Palamedes, Ajax son of Telamon, Sisyphus, Hector, Patroclus,
Thetis, Asclepius, Apollo, Zeus, Hera, Hades, Tartarus, Oceanus, Acheron, Pyriphlegethon,
Cocytus, Styx, the Acherusian lake, Glaucus. Places: Athens, Delphi, Delos, Phlius, Thebes,
Megara, Thessaly, Sparta ("Lacedaemon" only where the Greek says Λακεδαίμων), Crete, Aegina,
Potidaea, Amphipolis, Delium, Sunium, Phasis, the Pillars of Heracles.

- A name is given as the Greek gives it. Achilles is "the son of Thetis" at *Apology* 28c
  because Socrates does not name him. "The son of Sophroniscus" is not turned into "Socrates".
- Institutions keep their names, capitalized, with no gloss: the Council, the Assembly, the
  Eleven, the Thirty, the Prytaneum, the Rotunda (θόλος). πρυτανεύουσα is "presiding",
  πρυτάνεις "the presiding members". Demes: "of Sphettus", "of Cephisia".
- Stay in Greek dress: Prytaneum, Hades, Tartarus, drachma, mina, dithyramb, the Pythian
  priestess. Translated: river names only where Plato himself explains them.

## 7. Oaths and addresses

- μὰ Δία, νὴ Δία, πρὸς Διός: **by Zeus**. νὴ τὸν κύνα: **by the dog**. νὴ τὴν Ἥραν:
  **by Hera**. πρὸς θεῶν: **by the gods**. Ἡράκλεις: **By Heracles**. βαβαί: **Good heavens**.
  "God", "Good lord", "Ye gods", "for heaven's sake" are never used. No oath is added where
  the Greek has none and none is dropped where it has one.
- ὦ ἑταῖρε, ὦ φίλε: "my friend". ὦ φίλε Κρίτων: "my dear Crito". ὦ δαιμόνιε, ὦ μακάριε,
  ὦ θαυμάσιε: "my dear friend", or "you astonish me" where the word has its force.
  ὦ ἄριστε, ὦ βέλτιστε, ὠγαθέ: "my good man", "my good friend"; ὦ ἄριστε ἀνδρῶν "best of men".
  A bare vocative of a name is kept as the name.
- Formulas of assent, the same each time: πάνυ γε "Certainly"; πάνυ μὲν οὖν "Very much so";
  ἀληθῆ λέγεις "That's true"; ἔστι ταῦτα "That is so"; πῶς γὰρ οὔ; "Of course"; ἀνάγκη
  "It must be"; ἔοικεν "So it seems"; φαίνεται "It appears so"; οὐ δῆτα "Of course not";
  ναί "Yes".

## 8. Lines that must be the same wherever they recur

- The old charge (18b, 19b, 23d): "the things in the sky and under the earth", "making the
  weaker argument the stronger".
- The new charge (24b, 26b): "corrupting the young, and not believing in the gods the city
  believes in, but in other, new divine things".
- "I do not think I know what I do not know" (21d, 29b) keeps its shape.
- "It is not living that matters most, but living well" (*Crito* 48b).
- "One must never do wrong" / "nor do wrong in return" (*Crito* 49b–d).
- The body as "prison" (φρουρά 62b is "a kind of guard post", εἱργμός 82e "prison").

## 9. The two endings

The close of the Apology (42a) and the death of Socrates (*Phaedo* 115a–118a) are translated
word for word as nearly as English allows, in short plain sentences, with nothing heightened
and nothing explained. Crito's question about burial, the bath, the officer's tears, the cup,
the weeping, the legs growing cold, the cock for Asclepius: each detail in Plato's order, in
the fewest words. The last sentence keeps Phaedo's own qualifications ("of those we knew in
our time").

## 10. Divergences

Two or three per section, objects `{locus, phrase, older, lectio, note}`. `locus` is the
dialogue and Stephanus reference; `phrase` the Greek; `older` Jowett's words exactly; `lectio`
ours exactly as printed; `note` one or two plain sentences on why.

## 11. Settled in drafting

Terms the sections needed that the table above did not fix. They are now fixed.

- ὦ ἄνδρες among friends in the Phaedo is "gentlemen", as in the Apology.
- Attribution before a speech takes a colon (And Simmias said: What a thing …); attribution
  inside a speech takes commas (By Zeus, Socrates, he said, I was …).
- ἀγανακτεῖν: "resent" of death in the Phaedo's argument; "be upset", "be distressed",
  "be indignant" of a person in the Apology, Crito and death scene.
- ἀπαλλαγή, ἀπαλλάττεσθαι: "parting", "part from", "be rid of"; λύσις stays "release".
- αὐτὸ καθ' αὑτό: "itself by itself". εἰλικρινές: "unmixed". οὐσία: "being" ("reality" at 78d).
- γίγνεσθαι, γένεσις in the argument from opposites: "come to be", "a coming to be".
  ἀναβιώσκεσθαι: "come back to life".
- μέγα, μεῖζον, μέγεθος in the final argument: "large", "larger", "largeness" (Simmias is
  "larger than Socrates"), so the word stays one word. δέχεσθαι "admit"; ὑπομένειν "stand
  its ground"; ὑπεκχωρεῖν "withdraw"; ἐπιέναι "come upon"; ἐπιφέρειν "bring along".
- νοῦς: "mind" in Anaxagoras (97b–99c); "intelligence" or "sense" of a person.
- γυμναστική: "physical training"; μουσική: "music" (both as in the *Republic*).
- κρᾶσις "blending"; ἀναρμοστία "lack of attunement"; ἀντιλογικοί "the disputers",
  ἀντιλογικοὶ λόγοι "arguments for contradiction".
- μορμολύκεια "bogeymen"; ἐπῳδή, ἐπᾴδειν "charm", "sing a charm", "chant like a spell".
- εἱμαρμένη "fate"; ὁ δαίμων of the dead "guardian spirit"; αἰθήρ "ether"; οὐρανός "sky".
- εὐφημία (117e): "reverent silence".
- The last section is labeled "The True Earth and the Death", since "myth" is not printed.
