# The Art of War · Chapter VII
Grade: B
Strong chapter; the famous lines are recognizable and plain. One possible addition at the head.

## Issues
- `p-001` · fidelity · low
  > After that, nothing is harder than the contest of armies.
  莫難於軍爭 has no "after that"; the connective is supplied by the English.
  Fix: Nothing is harder than the contest of armies.

## Best lines
So be swift as the wind and unhurried as the forest. Raid and plunder like fire; stand unmoving like a mountain.
When you surround an army, leave a gap. Do not press a desperate enemy.

Note for the editor: "the generals of all three of your armies" (p-006) departs from the style sheet's 三軍 = "the whole army". It is right here, since p-007 then names 上軍將, the general of the leading army; the style sheet should list this place as the exception.
