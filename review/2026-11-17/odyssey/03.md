# Odyssey · Book III · Nestor at Pylos
Grade: B
Nestor's long speeches are clear, unhurried and true to the Greek, and the sacrifice is exact; the book's last two lines are garbled and partly lost, and one line inverts who ended the war.

## Issues
- `seg-0035` · fidelity · high
  > and soon they came to the wheat-bearing plain, and there, near the end of the road, the powerful horses carried them on.
  The Greek says they reached the plain and there finished their journey, so well did the swift horses carry them (Butler: "completed their journey, so well did their steeds take them"). The rendering leaves them short of the end, and the book's final line (3.497, the sun set and all the roads grew dark) is missing here and does not open Book IV either, so the night falls on no one before they arrive at Sparta.
  Fix: …and soon they came to the wheat-bearing plain, and there they drove on to their journey's end, so well did the swift horses carry them. And the sun went down, and all the roads grew dark.

- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > For nine long years we wove every kind of trap against them, and the gods could barely let it end.
  The Greek (μόγις δ' ἐτέλεσσε Κρονίων) says the son of Kronos barely brought it to fulfillment; "could barely let it end" makes the gods reluctant to stop the war, the reverse of the sense.
  Fix: For nine long years we stitched together every kind of trap against them, and even then the son of Kronos barely brought it to an end.

- `seg-0017` · read-aloud · medium
  > I don't dare even let myself imagine it. Not even if the gods themselves should will it.
  The last fragment attaches to "imagine" and reads as nonsense; the Greek says it would not happen for him, hope as he might, not even if the gods willed it.
  Fix: Old sir, I don't think that will ever come to pass. It's too great a thing to hope for. However I hoped, it would never happen for me, not even if the gods themselves willed it.

- `seg-0010` · fidelity · low
  > or was lost at sea in the waves.
  Drops Amphitrite (ἐν κύμασιν Ἀμφιτρίτης; Butler keeps "the waves of Amphitrite").
  Fix: or was lost at sea in the waves of Amphitrite.

- `seg-0010` · read-aloud · low
  > He was a man born to suffering, his mother bore him for grief.
  Comma splice; the stress falls flat.
  Fix: He was a man born to suffering; his mother bore him for grief.

- `seg-0020` · register · low
  > working away at Agamemnon's wife with his endless flattery.
  "Working away at" is workshop slang; the Greek is θέλγεσκ', he beguiled her.
  Fix: beguiling Agamemnon's wife with his endless flattery.

- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > and a wave of awe ran through them all who watched.
  "Them all who watched" is ungrammatical.
  Fix: and awe took hold of all who saw it.

- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > I'll lay her gold upon her horns and offer her to you.
  "Lay her gold" is unclear; the Greek pours gold around the horns (Butler: "gild her horns").
  Fix: I'll sheathe her horns in gold and offer her to you.

## Best lines
- So she prayed — and made it all come true herself.
- The fool — he never grasped that she would not be moved. The minds of the everlasting gods don't turn so easily.
