# Odyssey · Book V · Calypso, and the Raft
Grade: B
The storm, the raft and the landfall are superb, close and full; one translator's note sits in the text as a segment, and one idiom carries an unwanted sense.

## Issues
- `seg-0033` · form · medium
  > [Trans. note: The veil Ino gives him is her krēdemnon — a woman's head-binding …]
  An editorial note is published as the book's last text segment, in brackets. It breaks the reading and will be printed and recorded as if it were Homer. Butler keeps such matter in footnotes.
  Fix: remove the segment from the text and move the note to the enrichment sidecar (passage note on seg-0022).

- `seg-0006` · register · medium
  > the chattering sea-crows that do their business out on the water.
  "Do their business" now reads as a euphemism for relieving oneself. The Greek (θαλάσσια ἔργα μέμηλεν) means the sea is where they work.
  Fix: the long-tongued sea-crows whose work is on the water.

- `seg-0008` · voice · low
  > and choice hundred-beasts?
  A coinage found nowhere else; Book IV uses "hecatombs" for the same word (seg-0027, 0045).
  Fix: and choice hecatombs?

- `seg-0014` · register · low
  > Go, then, and good luck to you.
  In current English "good luck to you" sounds sarcastic. Calypso's χαῖρε καὶ ἔμπης is a sincere farewell given in spite of everything.
  Fix: Go, then, and I wish you well, even so.

- `seg-0019` · read-aloud · low
  > But I think I can still drive him his fill of trouble yet.
  "Still … yet" doubles up, and "drive him his fill" does not parse.
  Fix: But I think I can still give him his fill of trouble.

- `seg-0020` · read-aloud · low
  > Instead it's a wretched death I'm fated to be taken by.
  The speech ends on a preposition and loses its stress.
  Fix: Instead I am fated to be taken by a wretched death.

- `seg-0027` · register · low
  > looking for a beach that shelves easy
  "Easy" as an adverb is too casual for the passage, and it recurs in seg-0028.
  Fix: looking for a gently shelving beach (and in seg-0028: "a beach that shelved gently").

## Best lines
- willing no longer, lying beside a goddess who willed it.
- As when an octopus is torn from its hole and the pebbles cling thick to its suckers, so the skin was stripped from his strong hands against the rock
