# The Odyssey · Book XX · The Last Night Before the Reckoning
Grade: B
Very strong. "Endure, my heart", the grinding-woman's omen and Theoclymenus's vision all hit hard. One phrase brings a Christian echo into Homer, and the rest is polish.

## Issues
- `seg-0008` · register · medium
  > Let this be the last supper they ever eat.
  "Last supper" brings the Gospels into a pagan poem. The Greek is just "may they dine now for the last time".
  Fix: "Let this be the last meal they ever eat."
- `seg-0010` · form · low
  > and said to Euryclea
  This book spells the name "Euryclea" (also in `seg-0011` and `seg-0012`). XIX and XXI–XXIII use "Eurycleia".
  Fix: "Eurycleia" throughout.
- `seg-0027` · register · low
  > God forbid I should.
  The same monotheist idiom as XVII `seg-0032`. The Greek is "may a god not bring this about".
  Fix: "Heaven forbid I should."
- `seg-0021` · register · low
  > This is no public house.
  Many readers hear "pub". Homer means a public place open to anyone.
  Fix: "This is no common hall, open to all comers."
- `seg-0017` · read-aloud · low
  > to load the cattle and go off to some other country
  You don't "load" cattle to drive them overland. Greek: "to go to another people, cattle and all."
  Fix: "to drive the cattle off to some other country, to strangers."

## Best lines
- `seg-0028` "They were laughing now with jaws that were not their own."
- `seg-0001` "Endure, my heart. You have borne worse than this."
