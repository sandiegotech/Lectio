# The Odyssey · Book XXIII · The Sign of the Bed
Grade: B
The bed test and the reversed shipwreck simile are beautifully done. But Penelope's Helen passage has been silently dropped from her great speech. This is the only real cut in Books XIII–XXIV.

## Issues
- `seg-0019` · fidelity · high
  > There are so many cruel and scheming men in the world. But now you have spoken the secret of our bed
  Seven lines are missing between these sentences (23.218–224). In them Penelope says Argive Helen would never have slept with a foreigner had she known the Greeks would bring her home; a god drove her to it, and from that blindness came all their grief. Ancient critics doubted the passage, but it is in the standard text and in Butler, and it is Penelope's own reason for her caution. If the cut is deliberate, the provenance must say so. Otherwise restore it.
  Fix (insert after "in the world."): "Argive Helen, daughter of Zeus, would never have lain in love with a stranger if she had known that the Greeks would go to war to bring her home again. It was a god who drove her to that shameful thing; she never thought, before, of the blindness that began all our sorrow, hers and ours."
- `seg-0019` · fidelity · low
  > and the one maid my father gave me when I came here, who keeps the door of our room
  This drops her name. Homer gives it: Actoris, daughter of Actor.
  Fix: "and Actoris, the maid my father gave me when I came here, who keeps the door of our room".
- `seg-0018` · fidelity · low
  > There is a secret built into it … That is my secret.
  Homer's word is *sēma*, "sign". It is the same word as in "signs between us" in `seg-0011` and in the book's title. "Secret" breaks the thread.
  Fix: "There is a great sign built into it … That is the sign I give you."
- `seg-0027` · fidelity · low
  > who destroyed his ships and his men, every one but himself
  Homer says Odysseus escaped "with his black ship". He did not escape alone.
  Fix: "who destroyed his ships and their crews, all but Odysseus and his own black ship".
