# Odyssey · Book I · Athena Visits Ithaca
Grade: B
A close, full rendering from the Greek with real lift; a few idioms are carried over that a modern reader cannot parse, and one exclamation is mistranslated.

## Issues
- `seg-0018` · fidelity · medium
  > It's a shameful thing — how badly you need Odysseus home
  The Greek opens with ὢ πόποι, a cry of dismay, not a judgment that the situation is shameful (Butler: "Is that so? ... then you do indeed want Ulysses home again"). As written Athena seems to rebuke Telemachus.
  Fix: Oh, how badly you need Odysseus home, to lay his hands on these brazen suitors!

- `seg-0013` · register · medium
  > for he was a man who got around among people, in his time.
  "Got around" now reads as slang, and to many readers as sexual innuendo. The Greek (ἐπίστροφος ἦν ἀνθρώπων) means he went among men a great deal (Butler: "my father went about much himself").
  Fix: for he traveled widely, in his time, and knew many men.

- `seg-0024` · register · medium
  > a head whose fame fills all of Greece and the heart of Argos.
  "Head" for a beloved person (τοίην κεφαλήν) is a Homeric idiom a modern reader does not carry; read literally it is odd.
  Fix: so dear a man, whose fame spreads through all of Greece and the heart of Argos.

- `seg-0025` · read-aloud · medium
  > why begrudge the loyal singer for lifting our hearts however his spirit moves him?
  "Begrudge ... for" is not idiomatic; the sentence trips.
  Fix: why grudge the loyal singer his right to give us joy however his spirit moves him?

- `seg-0025` · register · low
  > who hands out fortune to working men however he pleases
  "Working men" brings a modern labor sense; ἀλφηστῇσιν is a stock epithet for mortals (bread-eaters). Butler: "mankind".
  Fix: who hands out fortune to mortal men however he pleases.

- `seg-0018` · read-aloud · low
  > He'd come back then from Ephyra
  "He'd" can be heard as "he would", which clashes with the wish just before it.
  Fix: He had just come back from Ephyra then,

- `seg-0019` · voice · low
  > or you may catch a word from Zeus, the kind that so often carries word to men.
  "Word ... word" in one line; the clause reads mechanically.
  Fix: or you may catch a rumor sent by Zeus, the kind that most often brings men news.

- `seg-0022` · fidelity · low
  > Then, godlike as he was, he went straight back to the suitors.
  "As he was" makes the epithet causal; the Greek (ἰσόθεος φώς) only names him.
  Fix: Then at once, a godlike man, he went back among the suitors.

- `seg-0032` · voice · low
  > Telemachus climbed to his own bedroom, built high in the beautiful court, with a clear view all around — and he went up to bed
  Says the same motion twice.
  Fix: Telemachus went to his bedroom, built high in the beautiful court with a clear view all around, his mind churning with thoughts.

- `seg-0014` · voice · low
  > where the bravest of the Argives went in their hollow ships
  Elsewhere the book renders Achaeans/Argives as "Greeks" (seg-0007, 0017, 0019, 0023, 0025, 0029); one stray "Argives" will puzzle a reader who has not met the name.
  Fix: where the bravest of the Greeks went in their hollow ships.

## Best lines
- No man ever really knows his own father.
- every one of them would find a quick death and a bitter wedding.
