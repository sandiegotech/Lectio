# The Odyssey · Book XXIV · Peace in Ithaca
Grade: B
A fine close. The second underworld scene, the orchard recognition (thirteen pears, ten apples, forty figs) and Laertes's "what a day" are all faithful and moving. What remains is one misplaced phrase and several epithets that drift from the earlier books.

## Issues
- `seg-0022` · read-aloud · medium
  > And Athena came close and filled out his limbs, the shepherd of his people, and made him taller
  The phrase in apposition lands on "limbs", so the limbs become the shepherd.
  Fix: "And Athena came close and filled out the limbs of the shepherd of his people, and made him taller".
- `seg-0010` · form · low
  > But when the will of Zeus who holds the storm-cloud at last moved him
  Here the aegis is "storm-cloud" (also in `seg-0037` and `seg-0039`). Book XIII calls it "storm-shield" and Book XXII "aegis". One Greek epithet should have one rendering.
  Fix: "Zeus who holds the aegis" throughout.
- `seg-0037` · form · low
  > Then the bright-eyed goddess Athena came close to him
  Athena's epithet *glaukōpis* is "grey-eyed" in XIII and XVIII but "bright-eyed" in this book.
  Fix: "grey-eyed" throughout.
- `seg-0009` · voice · low
  > It is a shroud for the hero Laertes, against the day death takes him in its long sorrow.
  Amphimedon is quoting the same Greek lines Penelope speaks in XIX `seg-0014`, where the edition has "its long sleep". Homer repeats the lines word for word, and the edition should too.
  Fix: use one rendering in both books, e.g. "against the day when cruel death lays him low."
- `seg-0034` · voice · low
  > he cried back to Odysseus, his words flying
  This is the same "winged words" calque flagged in XIII and XIV. Book XXI meanwhile writes "unwinged".
  Fix: "he called back to Odysseus".

## Best lines
- `seg-0004` "There you lay in the swirling dust, great and greatly fallen, your horsemanship forgotten."
- `seg-0036` "What a day this is for me, dear gods — what joy! My son and my son's son are matching each other in courage."
