# The Odyssey · Book XXI · The Contest of the Bow
Grade: B
A gripping, faithful book. It gets Telemachus's "I would not grieve to see her go" right where Butler gets it backward, and the bow sings like a swallow. Two sentences break at key moments.

## Issues
- `seg-0014` · read-aloud · medium
  > "It is here. The man himself. It is I.
  "It is here" has no referent, so the revelation stumbles at its most important moment. Greek: "He is here, inside — I myself."
  Fix: "He is here — the man himself. It is I."
- `seg-0026` · read-aloud · medium
  > So he spoke, and his words stayed unwinged on her lips: she went and bolted the doors
  "His words … on her lips" mixes up the pronouns. Homer's "her word was wingless" means she made no reply and simply obeyed.
  Fix: "So he spoke, and she answered not a word, but went and bolted the doors of the women's hall."
- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > But Odysseus, the man of many turns, once he had lifted the great bow and looked it over end to end — easily, as a man skilled in the lyre … so without effort Odysseus strung the great bow.
  The opening subject is left hanging, and Odysseus is named twice in the climactic sentence.
  Fix: "But Odysseus, the man of many turns, lifted the great bow and looked it over end to end. And as a man skilled in the lyre and in song easily slips a new string onto its peg, looping the twisted sheep-gut fast at either end, so without effort Odysseus strung the great bow."
- `seg-0029` · register · low
  > girt his sharp sword about him
  "Girt" is leftover archaism.
  Fix: "slung his sharp sword about him".
- `seg-0026` · form · low
  > Then he called the nurse Eurycleia aside
  This spelling is right, but it conflicts with "Euryclea" in XVII and XX (see the XIX report).
  Fix: none here; make the other books match.
