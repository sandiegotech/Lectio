# The Odyssey · Book XIV · In the Hut of Eumaeus
Grade: B
Warm and faithful, keeps the apostrophe to Eumaeus and the eleven goat herds Butler garbles; but a translator's note sits inside the text (and is wrong), and one simile has its epithet on the wrong noun.

## Issues
- `seg-0033` · form · high
  > [Trans. note: This is the only part of the Odyssey where Homer turns … to address a character directly …]
  An editorial note sits in the reading text as a segment, and its claim is false: Homer addresses Eumaeus this way again in Books XV, XVI, XVII and XXII.
  Fix: delete the segment from the text; if the point is wanted, move it to the enrichment sidecar, without the word "only".
- `seg-0016` · fidelity · medium
  > black-hulled and helpless, they were swept past the ship on the swell like sea-crows
  "Black" belongs to the ship ("like sea-crows around the black ship"); here it has slid onto the drowning men.
  Fix: "The men were flung into the sea and tossed on the waves around the black ship like sea-crows, and the god took from them their day of homecoming."
- `seg-0019` · fidelity · medium
  > Instead the storm-winds have snatched him away, and no man knows where.
  Drops "without glory" (Greek *akleiōs*), the word that answers "great glory for his son" just before it.
  Fix: "Instead the storm-winds have snatched him away without glory, and no man knows where."
- `seg-0018` · register · medium
  > led me here, to the steading of a wise man's door
  "Steading" is archaic, and "steading of a … door" garbles the image (Greek: "to the farmstead of a man who knows what is right"). "Steading" comes back in `seg-0030`.
  Fix: "and led me here, to the farm of a man who knows what is right." In `seg-0030`: "would give me a cloak here among the swineherds".
- `seg-0008` · read-aloud · medium
  > I can hardly bring myself to say his name, stranger, he is not here, and yet he loved me and cared for me so well that I call him the master of my heart
  A run of comma splices loses the sense: he shrinks from naming Odysseus even though he is absent.
  Fix: "Even with him gone, stranger, I can hardly bring myself to speak his name, he loved me and cared for me so well. I call him my dear master still, far away as he is."
- `seg-0014` · fidelity · low
  > who above all the gods is angry at cruelty
  This reads as "angrier than all the other gods". The Greek says Zeus "resents wicked deeds most of all".
  Fix: "who is angered most of all by cruel deeds."
- `seg-0027` · register · low
  > nothing but my shield and a bright kilt
  "Kilt" brings a Scot to mind. The Greek is a warrior's belt or loin-guard.
  Fix: "nothing but my shield and a bright war-belt."
- `seg-0026` · read-aloud · low
  > a man the swineherd had bought for himself while his master was away, with his own goods, telling neither his mistress nor old Laertes, paying for him out of the Taphians' hands
  "Bought … paying for him" says the same thing twice, and "out of the Taphians' hands" is awkward.
  Fix: "a man the swineherd had bought from the Taphians with his own goods while his master was away, telling neither his mistress nor old Laertes."
- `seg-0025` · register · low
  > Eat, my strange friend
  Reads as "my odd friend".
  Fix: "Eat, my poor friend".
- `seg-0010` · fidelity · low
  > hoping to wipe the name of Arceisius clean off the earth, out of Ithaca forever
  The Greek is the *line* of Arceisius dying out "nameless" from Ithaca. "Off the earth" and "out of Ithaca" say it twice.
  Fix: "hoping to wipe the line of Arceisius out of Ithaca and leave it without a name."
- `seg-0007` · voice · low
  > spoke to him, his words flying
  The same "winged words" calque as Book XIII.
  Fix: "and spoke to him, and his words had wings:"
