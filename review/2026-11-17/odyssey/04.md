# Odyssey · Book IV · Menelaus and Helen at Sparta
Grade: B
One of the best books so far: Helen's drug, the wooden horse and the Proteus episode are vivid and exact. The fixes are small, but the spelling of names now differs from Books I–II.

## Issues
- whole section · form · medium
  > Antinous … Eurymachus … Icarius … Phronius
  Books I–II spell these Antinoos, Eurymachos, Ikarios, Phronios (and Polybos, Leokritos); this book switches to Latin endings for the same men (and keeps Greek "Aigisthos" alongside Latin "Pisistratus", "Clymenus"). A reader meeting "Antinoos" and "Antinous" will think they are two people.
  Fix: choose one transliteration scheme for the whole edition and apply it everywhere (see SUMMARY).

- `seg-0016` · read-aloud · medium
  > and besides, the Dawn will rise soon enough — there is no harm, then, in mourning whoever has died and met his fate.
  "Then" reads as "therefore", so Pisistratus seems to argue for weeping now, the reverse of his point. The Greek says morning will come, and he does not object to weeping then.
  Fix: and besides, Dawn will come soon enough, and then I won't begrudge us weeping for whoever has died and met his fate.

- `seg-0047` · register · low
  > But by now my crew, waiting in sacred Pylos, must be wearing thin
  The idiom is "patience wearing thin"; a crew does not wear thin. The Greek (ἀνιάζουσιν) says they are growing restless.
  Fix: But by now my crew, waiting in sacred Pylos, must be growing restless

- `seg-0014` · voice · low
  > there's no one in the land to keep the wolves from his door.
  A stock phrase whose usual sense is staving off poverty; the Greek is no one to ward off ruin.
  Fix: there's no one in the land to keep ruin from his door.

- `seg-0041` · fidelity · low
  > so that Agamemnon couldn't pass unseen and seize the first blow.
  The Greek (μνήσαιτο δὲ θούριδος ἀλκῆς) is "and call up his fighting strength"; "seize the first blow" adds a tactic.
  Fix: so that Agamemnon couldn't slip past unseen and rouse his fighting strength.

- `seg-0070` · fidelity · low
  > There the Greeks lay in wait for him.
  The edition renders "Achaeans" as "Greeks" throughout, and here that reads as the Greek army rather than the suitors' crew.
  Fix: There the suitors lay in wait for him.

- `seg-0001` · fidelity · low
  > That son, Megapenthes, was the strong child of a slave woman
  Drops τηλύγετος, that he was born to Menelaus late in life.
  Fix: That son, strong Megapenthes, was born to him late, of a slave woman,
