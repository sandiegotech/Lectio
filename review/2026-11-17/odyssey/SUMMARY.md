## Recurring patterns (XIII–XXIV)

1. **Repeated formulas are rendered differently each time.** Homer repeats lines on purpose, and the edition does not track them. "The iron itself draws a man on" (XVI) becomes "steel within a man's reach…" (XIX). The aegis is "storm-shield" (XIII), "aegis" (XXII) and "storm-cloud" (XXIV). *Glaukōpis* is "grey-eyed", then "bright-eyed". The shroud line differs between XIX and XXIV. "Winged words" appears as "his words flying" (XIII, XIV, XXIV), "winged" elsewhere and "unwinged" (XXI). The nurse is "Euryclea" in XVII and XX but "Eurycleia" in XIX and XXI–XXIII, and "Epeians" alternates with "Epeans". The fix is a short concordance of formulas, names and epithets, applied in one pass.
2. **Leftover archaism and words in senses readers no longer hold.** "Wallet" for a beggar's bag (XIII, XVII, XVIII), "steading" (XIV, XV, XVII), "trumpery", "girt", "cadging", "corn" for grain, "public house". A smaller group goes the other way: "last supper", "God forbid", "mouthing off".
3. **Small fidelity slips at load-bearing words.** Single words are dropped or bent where Homer's point depends on them. Examples: "without glory" (XIV), "lies like truth" (XIX), "sign" rendered "secret" (XXIII), "never lived to" (XVII), and the wrong guard at the side door, copied from Butler's error (XXII). There are also two larger problems. An editorial "Trans. note" sits inside the text in XIV, and its claim is false. Penelope's Helen passage is missing from XXIII without any note.

## Verdict

Books XIII–XXIV are not ready to ship as they stand, but they are close. Each needs only a small fix list, and none needs redoing. The prose is consistently good: plain, rhythmic and unmistakably one voice, much closer to the Greek than Butler, and it keeps nearly every famous line recognizable (Argos, the scar, "Endure, my heart", the bow that sings like a swallow, the head still speaking). Before release, two things must be done. First, fix the two high items: remove the in-text note in XIV, and restore or formally note the Helen lines in XXIII. Second, run one consistency pass over formulas, names and epithets across all 24 books. After that and the medium items, the second half is at A.
