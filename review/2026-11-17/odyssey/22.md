# The Odyssey · Book XXII · The Killing of the Suitors
Grade: B
Relentless and exact. The famous lines all survive: the head still speaking, the self-taught bard, the fish on the shore, "no holy thing to gloat". One guard has been swapped (following Butler's error, not the Greek), and one simile loses its sting.

## Issues
- `seg-0012` · fidelity · medium
  > Odysseus posted Philoetius to guard it, since only one man could come at it at a time.
  The Greek (22.129) has Odysseus post "the noble swineherd" at the side door. Philoetius is Butler's slip, and this edition has copied it.
  Fix: "Odysseus posted the swineherd to guard it, standing close by, for there was only one way in."
- `seg-0027` · fidelity · low
  > and the hawks fall on them and kill, and there is no help and no escape
  This drops the last cruel touch of the simile: "and men delight in the hunt". The Greek birds are vultures, not hawks.
  Fix: "and the vultures fall on them and kill, and there is no help and no escape, and men look on and delight in the chase".
- `seg-0018` · voice · low
  > And the swineherd Eumaeus stood over him and jeered
  Homer addresses Eumaeus directly here ("and you, Eumaeus, swineherd, mocked him"). The edition keeps that device everywhere else and has a note about it in XIV.
  Fix: "And you, Eumaeus, swineherd, stood over him and jeered:".
- `seg-0037` · fidelity · low
  > to card the wool and carry the load of the household
  The Greek is "to bear their slavery". Softening it blurs what the hanging means.
  Fix: "to card the wool and to bear their lot as slaves."
- `seg-0027` · form · low
  > lifted high the aegis that destroys men
  "Aegis" here, but XIII renders the same word "storm-shield". Pick one across the work.
  Fix: "aegis" in both places (it is the recognizable name).

## Best lines
- `seg-0029` "The head was still speaking when it rolled in the dust."
- `seg-0041` "and find a cruel bed where they had thought to roost"
