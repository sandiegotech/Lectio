# The Odyssey · Book XV · Telemachus Sails Home
Grade: B
A strong, faithful book. Eumaeus's life story and the Melampus genealogy are complete and clear. One dangling sentence and a few word choices need fixing.

## Issues
- `seg-0030` · read-aloud · medium
  > Still, it is the cruel belly that drives men to all their wretched troubles, the man who knows want and grief and the long road.
  The second clause hangs from nothing. The Greek is "for the cursed belly's sake men bear hard troubles, any man on whom wandering and pain and grief come."
  Fix: "Still, for the sake of the cursed belly men bear hard troubles — any man on whom wandering comes, and want, and grief."
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > a circuit through Hellas, or down through the Peloponnese
  "Peloponnese" is not in the text. Homer says "through Hellas and the heart of Argos".
  Fix: "a circuit through Hellas and the heart of Argos".
- `seg-0033` · fidelity · low
  > comes in time to take a strange pleasure in his griefs
  "Strange" is commentary. The famous line says plainly that a man takes pleasure even in his griefs afterward.
  Fix: "for a man who has suffered much and wandered far takes pleasure afterward even in his griefs."
- `seg-0027` · register · low
  > press him to stay at the steading
  "Steading" is archaic, and the edition leans on it again in `seg-0048` and in Book XIV.
  Fix: "at the farm" here; "to the farm where his great droves of swine were penned" in `seg-0048`.
- `seg-0035` · register · low
  > a black ship full of trinkets and trumpery
  "Trumpery" is leftover archaism.
  Fix: "a black ship full of trinkets and baubles".
- `seg-0032` · voice · low
  > Strange, then — you must have been only a small child
  For Greek *ō popoi* (surprise), "Strange, then" reads like a non-sequitur.
  Fix: "Think of that — you must have been only a small child".

## Best lines
- `seg-0010` "I too have a gift for you, dear child, to remember the hands of Helen by"
- `seg-0026` "his heart hanging between escape and capture."
