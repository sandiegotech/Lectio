# Odyssey · Book VIII · The Games of the Phaeacians
Grade: A
Excellent: the games, the song of Ares and Aphrodite, and the weeping-woman simile are full, exact and alive. A few small phrasing fixes.

## Issues
- `seg-0040` · read-aloud · low
  > I would show him my goodwill, sorrow as I am.
  Ungrammatical; "sorrow" needs to be a participle (καὶ ἀχνύμενός περ).
  Fix: I would show him my goodwill, grieving as I am.

- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > as they sat at the gods' feast
  The Greek is a rich feast held for the gods (θεῶν ἐν δαιτὶ θαλείῃ), a sacrifice; the rendering suggests the gods themselves were dining.
  Fix: as they sat at a rich feast in honor of the gods.

- `seg-0017` · register · low
  > let those of you who are the best dancers of the Phaeacians make your play
  "Make your play" carries a sports-and-gambling sense; παίσατε means dance, play.
  Fix: let those of you who are the best dancers of the Phaeacians begin the dance

- `seg-0039` · register · low
  > for it was you, girl, who gave me back my life.
  Addressed to a princess, a bare "girl" now sounds curt or dismissive; κούρη is respectful.
  Fix: for it was you, my lady, who gave me back my life.
