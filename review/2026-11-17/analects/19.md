# The Analects · Book XIX · Tsze-chang
Grade: B
The disciples' book reads well, and Tsze-kung's defense of the Master closes it with real eloquence; one broken sentence and one name that will mislead.

## Issues
- `seg-0002` · read-aloud · medium
  > how would I ever get to turning away them?
  Ungrammatical ("turning away them") and chatty ("get to") at the point of the argument (Legge: "What have we to do with the putting away of others?").
  Fix: "If I am unworthy, men will turn me away first — what business have I turning others away?"
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · medium
  > Chau cannot have been as wicked as his name.
  This Chau is the last tyrant of Yin, but the edition uses the same spelling for the Chau dynasty the Master reveres ("I follow Chau"); with no cue, a reader takes it as the dynasty. The same tyrant appears at XVIII.1 ("became a slave to Chau").
  Fix: "The tyrant Chau cannot have been as wicked as his name." (and at XVIII.1: "became a slave to the tyrant Chau").
- `seg-0004` · register · low
  > and then he criticizes
  "Criticizes" is office vocabulary for remonstrating with a prince (Legge: "one may then remonstrate with him").
  Fix: "He wins his prince's trust, and then he speaks against his faults."

## Best lines
- "The faults of a gentleman are eclipses of the sun and moon. When he fails, everyone sees it. When he mends it, everyone looks up again."
- "Living, he is their glory. Dead, he is their grief. How could anyone reach him?"
