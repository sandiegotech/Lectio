# The Analects · Book IX · The Passing Stream
Grade: B
Some of the finest pages in the edition (Yen Yuan's sigh, the stream, the pine); a repeated saying is rendered differently from Book I, and a few lines trip.

## Issues
- `seg-0024` · voice · medium
  > Put loyalty and good faith first. Have no friends who are not your equal in them. And when you are wrong, do not be afraid to mend it.
  This saying repeats I.8 word for word in the source, but the edition renders it differently ("faithfulness and keeping your word" / "your equals" / "change" in Book I) and adds "in them", a gloss. Repeated sayings elsewhere (I.11 / IV.20) are kept identical.
  Fix: use the Book I wording exactly: "Put faithfulness and keeping your word first. Have no friends who are not your equals. And when you are wrong, do not be afraid to change."
- `seg-0029` · read-aloud · medium
  > There are men you can study beside whom you cannot walk the Way with.
  The stacked relatives ("beside whom ... with") trip the ear three times running; "when the rules run out" is also a gloss on "weigh" (Legge: "weigh occurring events along with us").
  Fix: "Some men you can study with, but not walk the Way with. Some you can walk the Way with, but not stand firm with. Some you can stand firm with, but not weigh things with."
- `seg-0030` · fidelity · low
  > He was not thinking of them at all.
  "Them" has no antecedent (the ode says "you"); the source comments on the lack of real longing (Legge: "It is the want of thought about it").
  Fix: "There was no real longing in it. To one who truly longs, what is far?"
- `seg-0011` · read-aloud · low
  > Pretending to ministers when I have none
  "Pretending to ministers" is unidiomatic; and "am I dying by the side of the road?" shifts to an odd present tense (Legge: "shall I die upon the road?").
  Fix: "Pretending I have ministers when I have none ... but will I die by the side of the road?"
- `seg-0002` · form · low
  > the philosopher K’ung
  Curly quotes and apostrophes where most of the edition uses straight ones; see Book VI.
  Fix: normalize to the edition's majority form.

## Best lines
- "I look up at it, and it is higher. I bore into it, and it is harder. I see it before me, and suddenly it is behind."
- "Only when the year turns cold do you learn that the pine and the cypress are the last to fade."
