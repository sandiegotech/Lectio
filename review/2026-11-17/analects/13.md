# The Analects · Book XIII · Setting Names Right
Grade: A
Plain-spoken and exact; the rectification of names runs as a clean chain. One clause in its conclusion is cloudy.

## Issues
- `seg-0003` · read-aloud · medium
  > So the gentleman gives no name that cannot be spoken to the thing
  "Spoken to the thing" is unclear and breaks the clean chain that precedes it (Legge: "the names he uses may be spoken appropriately").
  Fix: "So the gentleman uses no name he cannot rightly speak, and says nothing he cannot carry out."
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · low
  > he knew how to live in a house
  Odd for "managed his household well"; the point is his modest contentment with his means (Legge: "knew the economy of a family well").
  Fix: "that he knew how to keep a household."
- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > Earnest, urging, and warm
  "Urging" as an adjective does not stand on its own.
  Fix: "Earnest, encouraging, and warm — ... With his friends, earnest and encouraging; with his brothers, warm."

## Best lines
- "The gentleman is in harmony without being in unison. The small man is in unison without being in harmony."
- "Pah — peck-measures and rice-baskets. Not worth the counting."
