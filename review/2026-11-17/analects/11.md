# The Analects · Book XI · Men of Former Times
Grade: B
The grief for Yen Hui and the closing scene of wishes are superb; two lines need correcting, one for meaning and one for a gloss written into the text.

## Issues
- `seg-0004` · fidelity · medium
  > No one can find a word to add to what his own parents and brothers say of him.
  The source says no one disputes or differs from his family's praise, not that nothing can be added (Legge: "Other people say nothing of him different from the report of his parents and brothers").
  Fix: "No one says a word against what his own parents and brothers say of him."
- `seg-0005` · fidelity · medium
  > — a flaw in white jade can still be polished away; a flaw in your words, never.
  The content of the ode is the translator's explanation; the source says only that he kept repeating the lines about the white scepter stone.
  Fix: "Nan Yung kept repeating the lines about the white scepter stone. Confucius gave him his elder brother's daughter in marriage."

## Best lines
- "If I do not grieve past measure for this man, for whom should I grieve?"
- "Tien let the last notes thin away, set his lute aside with a clang of strings, and rose."
