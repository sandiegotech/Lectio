# The Analects · Book X · At Home and at Court
Grade: A
The hardest book to make readable, and it reads: court manners and dress are concrete and unfussy. A few phrases to fix.

## Issues
- `seg-0005` · read-aloud · medium
  > his face trembled toward fear
  An unnatural phrase a reader has to stop on (Legge: "His countenance seemed to change, and look apprehensive").
  Fix: "his face changed, as if in fear,"
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > Out of mourning, he wore whatever hung from the girdle.
  "Whatever" reads as indifference; the source says he wore every ornament of the girdle once mourning ended (Legge: "he wore all the appendages of the girdle").
  Fix: "Out of mourning, he wore all the ornaments of the girdle."
- `seg-0008` · register · low
  > nor fish or meat gone off
  "Gone off" is a British colloquialism the American reader does not use for food.
  Fix: "nor fish or meat that had spoiled;"
- `seg-0003` · form · low
  > sleeves spread like a bird’s wings
  Curly quotes and apostrophes where most of the edition uses straight ones; see Book VI.
  Fix: normalize to the edition's majority form.

## Best lines
- "The stable burned down. Coming back from court, the Master said, "Was anyone hurt?" He did not ask about the horses."
- "Only in wine he set no measure — but never drank to confusion."
