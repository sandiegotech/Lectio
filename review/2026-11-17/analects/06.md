# The Analects · Book VI · Knowing, Loving, Delighting
Grade: B
Strong and moving (Hui's poverty, Po-niu's illness); one modern idiom and a typographic split from the rest of the edition.

## Issues
- `seg-0003` · register · medium
  > he does not top up the rich
  "Top up" is current phone-plan slang and jars in the Master's mouth (Legge: "does not add to the wealth of the rich").
  Fix: "a gentleman helps the desperate; he does not add to the rich."
- `seg-0001` · form · low
  > “Yung — you could seat him on a throne.”
  Books VI–X use curly quotes and apostrophes (“ ” ’) where Books I–V and XI–XX use straight ones; the romanized names (Ch’i vs Ch'i) then differ byte for byte across the edition, which can break search and glossary matching.
  Fix: normalize Books VI–X to the edition's majority form (straight " and ').
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · low
  > you could seat him on a throne
  The source says he could "face south", i.e. hold a ruler's seat; "throne" pushes it toward kingship (Legge: "the place of a prince").
  Fix: "Yung — you could give him a ruler's seat."
- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > wanting to arrive, he helps others arrive
  "Arrive" without a destination sounds unfinished; the sense is to get on, to succeed (Legge: "wishing to be enlarged himself").
  Fix: "wanting to get on, he helps others get on."

- "When substance outweighs polish, you get a rustic. When polish outweighs substance, you get a clerk."
