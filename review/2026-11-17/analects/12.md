# The Analects · Book XII · Yen Yuan
Grade: A
Faithful and strong throughout; the wind-and-grass and food-arms-trust sayings land cleanly. One long sentence to break up.

## Issues
- `seg-0006` · read-aloud · medium
  > The man on whom slander that soaks in like water and accusations that sting like a wound in the flesh do not work — call him clear-sighted.
  The subject is buried under two long modifiers before the verb arrives; read aloud, the sentence loses its way.
  Fix: "Slander that soaks in like water, accusations that sting like a wound in the flesh — the man they cannot move, call him clear-sighted. The man they cannot move at all, call him far-sighted."
- `seg-0004` · register · low
  > finds nothing to be sick over
  "Sick over" is colloquial for self-examination (Legge: "discovers nothing wrong").
  Fix: "He looks inward and finds nothing to reproach himself with."
- `seg-0020` · register · low
  > without a doubt in his head
  Chatty idiom in a careful definition (Legge: "without any doubts about himself").
  Fix: "and he lives in the pose without a doubt of himself."

## Best lines
- "The virtue of the gentleman is wind; the virtue of the small man is grass. When the wind crosses the grass, the grass must bend."
- "Death has been with us from the oldest days. But without the people's trust, nothing stands."
