# The Analects · Book XVII · Yang Ho
Grade: B
Vivid and well paced (Yang Ho on the road, the bitter gourd, Tsai Wo's mourning); one line inverts Tsze-lu's objection and one repeated saying drifts from its first rendering.

## Issues
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · medium
  > "So there is nowhere left to go," he said. "Fine. But why must it be Kung-shan?"
  "Fine" reads as grudging consent; Tsze-lu means the opposite, that if there is nowhere to go the Master should simply not go (Legge: "Indeed, you cannot go!").
  Fix: "If there is nowhere to go, then do not go," he said. "Why must it be Kung-shan?"
- `seg-0010` · voice · medium
  > Clever words and a charming face rarely go with human-heartedness.
  The source repeats I.3 word for word; the edition rendered it there as "Clever words and a winning face rarely keep company with human-heartedness." A repeated saying should read the same.
  Fix: "Clever words and a winning face rarely keep company with human-heartedness."
- `seg-0005` · read-aloud · low
  > But don't they also say hard — so hard that grinding cannot thin it?
  The proverb lacks a clear frame and trips (Legge: "if a thing be really hard, it may be ground without being made thin").
  Fix: "But is it not also said: what is truly hard can be ground and not grow thin? What is truly white can be dyed and not turn black?"
- `seg-0007` · register · low
  > They can teach you how to complain.
  "Complain" sounds petty; the sense is voicing grievance rightly (Legge: "regulate feelings of resentment").
  Fix: "They can teach you how to voice a grievance."

## Best lines
- "Does Heaven say anything? The four seasons turn, the hundred creatures are born — and does Heaven say a word?"
- "The village paragon is the thief of virtue."
