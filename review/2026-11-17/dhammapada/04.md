# Dhammapada · Chapter IV. Flowers
Grade: B
A lovely chapter that improves on Müller in places ("disciple in training," "lotus"). Yama is lost, and 51/52 no longer pair.

## Issues
- `seg-0001`, `seg-0002` · fidelity · medium
  > and the realm of Death
  The Pali is yamaloka, Yama's world (Müller keeps "Yama"). The edition drops the proper noun, although it keeps Mara and "the King of Death" elsewhere.
  Fix: "and the realm of Yama, lord of the dead"
- `seg-0008`, `seg-0009` · fidelity · low
  > are the fine words of one who speaks well but does not act.
  In the Pali, v51 turns on fine words being *fruitless* (aphalā) for one who does not act, and v52 on their being fruitful. The edition leaves the fruit out of 51 and then tacks it onto 52 ("and they bear fruit"), so the two verses no longer mirror.
  Fix: v51 "...are fine words that bear no fruit in one who does not act on them." v52 "...are fine words that bear fruit in one who acts on them."
- `seg-0004`, `seg-0005` · fidelity · low
  > his mind elsewhere
  The Pali byāsattamanasa means a mind clinging or entangled, not merely absent.
  Fix: "his mind caught up in them"
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · low
  > A good person reaches every quarter.
  The Pali verb (pavāyati) keeps the scent image: the good person's fragrance blows in every direction. "Reaches" drops the image the verse is built on.
  Fix: "The good person's fragrance spreads in every direction."
- `seg-0012` · form · low
  > the lotus and the jasmine — sweet as all these perfumes are
  The dash is spaced, against the house style.
  Fix: "the lotus and the jasmine—sweet as all these perfumes are"

## Best lines
- "As the bee takes the nectar and flies off without harming the flower, its color, or its scent, so let the sage move through the village."
- "will snap the flower-tipped arrows of Mara and pass beyond the sight of the King of Death."
