# Dhammapada · Chapter XXVI. The True Brahmin
Grade: B
The long refrain holds its rhythm across forty verses, and 394 and 411 are excellent. 396 misreads a Pali idiom, and 395 attaches the man's thinness to his rags.

## Issues
- `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > he is only a man who says "sir" to himself.
  The Pali bhovādī means one who says "bho" ("sir," "friend") *to others*. That form of address marked a Brahmin by birth, so the phrase means a Brahmin in name only. "Says 'sir' to himself" is not what the text says, and readers cannot make sense of it.
  Fix: "he is a Brahmin in name only, one who greets others with 'Sir.'"
- `seg-0013` · fidelity · medium
  > The one who wears cast-off rags, worn thin to the bone
  The Pali (kisaṃ dhamanisanthataṃ) describes the man as lean with his veins showing. As written, "worn thin to the bone" reads as describing the rags.
  Fix: "The one who wears cast-off rags, lean, his veins standing out, who lives alone in the forest and meditates"
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · low
  > you will know the Uncreated, Nirvana.
  The edition takes Müller's gloss into the sentence. The Pali has only akataññū, "knower of the unmade."
  Fix: "you will know the Uncreated."
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · low
  > both teachings—restraint and meditation—
  The Pali dvayesu dhammesu means "two states" (traditionally calm and insight), not two teachings, and the gloss is set between dashes.
  Fix: "When the Brahmin has crossed to the far shore in both states, then for him who has reached this knowledge every bond falls away."

## Best lines
- "Outside you keep yourself clean, but inside you are a tangle of craving."
- "The one who stays gentle among the hostile, peaceful among the violent, and free of clinging among those who cling—him I call a true Brahmin."
