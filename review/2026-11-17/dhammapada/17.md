# Dhammapada · Chapter XVII. Anger
Grade: B
Plain and strong ("The rest merely hold the reins"). 229–230 is tangled and repeats the "Brahman" slip.

## Issues
- `seg-0009` · read-aloud · medium
  > who would dare to blame, worthy as a coin of the purest river-gold?
  The question has no object, and "worthy as a coin" hangs in the air. On top of that, the river's name (Jambu) is dropped and "Brahman" again stands for the god Brahmā (Pali Brahmunā).
  Fix: "But who would dare to blame the one whom the discerning praise day after day—flawless, wise, rich in understanding and in virtue, pure as a coin of Jambu river gold? Even the gods praise such a one; even Brahma praises him."
- `seg-0005` · fidelity · low
  > go to the unchanging place, Nirvana
  The edition takes Müller's parenthetical "(Nirvana)" into the sentence. The Pali has only accutaṃ ṭhānaṃ, "the unchanging place."
  Fix: "go to the unchanging place, and once they have gone there, they suffer no more."

## Best lines
- "him I call a true charioteer. The rest merely hold the reins."
- "Overcome anger by calm, evil by good; overcome the miser by giving, the liar by truth."
