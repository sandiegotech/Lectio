# Dhammapada · Chapter XVIII. Impurity
Grade: B
Vivid and well paced, but the chain of "taints" (mala) breaks at 241, and 254–255 follow Müller's misreading.

## Issues
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · medium
  > Scriptures decay when they go unrecited; houses decay when they go unrepaired
  In the Pali, 241–243 are one chain on mala, "taint" ("non-recitation is the taint of sacred verses..."). The edition says "decay" in 241 and "taint" in 242–243, so the chapter's build to "ignorance is the greatest taint" loses its first step.
  Fix: "Neglect is the taint of scripture; disrepair is the taint of a house; sloth is the taint of beauty; carelessness is the taint of a watchman."
- `seg-0020`, `seg-0021` · fidelity · medium
  > outward acts do not make a holy man.
  The Pali samaṇo natthi bāhire means "there is no true ascetic outside (this teaching)." "Outward acts" is Müller's reading and is not in the text.
  Fix: "There is no path through the sky; there is no true ascetic outside this teaching."
- `seg-0020` · fidelity · low
  > but the Tathagatas, the Buddhas, are free
  The gloss is set into the line as an appositive.
  Fix: "but the Tathagatas are free of all such hindrance."

## Best lines
- "Just as rust, springing from the iron itself, eats away the very iron that bred it, so a transgressor's own deeds lead him down to ruin."
- "We winnow the faults of our neighbors like chaff, but hide our own, as a cheat hides the losing throw from the gambler."
