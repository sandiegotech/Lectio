# Dhammapada · Chapter XI. Old Age
Grade: B
Strong, and the house-builder verses land with their full force. There are two small slips in meaning, a gloss in 154, and spaced dashes.

## Issues
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · medium
  > so the good pass it on, one to another.
  The Pali (santo have sabbhi pavedayanti) says the good *declare this* to the good, meaning the truth that virtue does not decay. "Pass it on" makes it sound as if virtue itself is handed down.
  Fix: "But the virtue of the good never wears out—so the good teach the good."
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · low
  > My mind has reached the unmade, the unconditioned, Nirvana
  The Pali has one term (visaṅkhāragataṃ cittaṃ). The edition makes it three, and "Nirvana" comes from Müller's parenthetical gloss.
  Fix: "My mind has reached the unconditioned; I have come to the end of craving."
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · low
  > sickly, full of cravings
  The Pali bahusaṅkappaṃ means full of many thoughts or plans (Müller: "full of many thoughts").
  Fix: "sickly, full of schemes"
- `seg-0002`, `seg-0004`, `seg-0006`, `seg-0008` · form · low
  > all dressed up — a mass of wounds
  The dashes are spaced, against the closed-up style used through most of the work.
  Fix: close up the dashes (—).

## Best lines
- "House-builder, now you are seen! You shall not build this house again. Your rafters are all broken, your ridgepole shattered."
- "The person who has learned little grows old like an ox: his body grows heavier, but his wisdom does not grow."
