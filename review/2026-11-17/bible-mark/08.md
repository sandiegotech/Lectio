# Mark · Chapter 8 · Who Do You Say I Am
Grade: B
The hinge of the book lands, and the two-stage healing is beautifully plain; the call to discipleship slips into a self-help register.

## Issues
- `seg-0007` · register · medium
  > he has to say no to himself, and pick up his cross
  *aparnēsasthō heauton* (KJV "let him deny himself"). "Say no to himself" reads like advice on willpower, and it loses the verb Mark reuses for Peter's denial (14:30, 72).
  Fix: he must deny himself, and pick up his cross, and follow me. (The edition renders the same verb "disown" at 14:30 and 14:72; whichever word wins, use it in all three places.)
- `seg-0001` · register · medium
  > I'm moved to the depths for these people.
  *splanchnizomai* (KJV "I have compassion on the multitude"); in Jesus' own mouth the phrase is not speakable English (see 1:41, 6:34).
  Fix: My heart goes out to these people.
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > Peter took him aside and started correcting him.
  The verb is *epitiman*, "rebuke" (KJV), the same verb used one line later when Jesus rebukes Peter; "correcting" softens Peter and breaks the echo.
  Fix: Peter took him aside and started rebuking him.
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · low
  > in this unfaithful and sinful generation
  *moichalidi* is "adulterous" (KJV), the prophets' marriage image; "unfaithful" explains the metaphor away.
  Fix: in this adulterous and sinful generation

## Best lines
- "I see people — they look like trees, walking around."
- "You're not thinking God's thoughts — you're thinking human ones."
