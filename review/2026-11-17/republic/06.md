# Republic · Book VI · The Sun and the Divided Line
Grade: B
Faithful, vivid, and closer to the Greek than Jowett throughout; a handful of glosses carried into the text and two small shifts in sense.

## Issues
- `seg-0199` · fidelity · medium
  > (Heraclitus said the sun is put out each evening and kindled new each morning.)
  An editor's explanatory note (inherited from Jowett's text) printed as part of Socrates' speech; Plato has no such sentence.
  Fix: delete the parenthesis; the sentence ends at "they are never lit again." Move the note to enrichment if wanted.
- `seg-0387` · fidelity · medium
  > (Ouranos, the heavens; horatos, the visible.)
  Same problem: a philological gloss spliced into the dialogue.
  Fix: delete the parenthesis; keep "or you'll think I'm playing on the word." and carry the pun in a note.
- `seg-0380` · fidelity · medium
  > Glaucon burst out laughing.
  The Greek (mala geloiōs) says Glaucon spoke very comically, not that he laughed; the line makes him the audience instead of the comic.
  Fix: "And Glaucon, quite comically: Apollo! he said. What an unearthly exaggeration!"
- `seg-0265` · fidelity · medium
  > the most exacting guardians must be philosophers.
  "Exacting" reads as "demanding of others"; the Greek (akribestatous) means guardians in the strictest, most precise sense.
  Fix: "the guardians in the strictest sense must be philosophers."
- `seg-0073` · fidelity · low
  > Not even Momus, he said — the god of faultfinding himself — could find fault with that.
  Gloss explaining Momus inserted into the line.
  Fix: "Not even Momus himself, he said, could find fault with that." (Momus to the glossary.)
- `seg-0144` · fidelity · low
  > is under what people call the necessity of Diomede — no choice at all — to produce
  "no choice at all" is an explanation added to the proverb.
  Fix: "is under what people call the necessity of Diomede to produce whatever they praise."
- `seg-0122` · fidelity · low
  > the bad is more the enemy of what's good than of what's merely middling.
  The Greek contrasts the good with "what is not good," not with the middling.
  Fix: "the bad is more the enemy of the good than of what is not good."
- `seg-0219` · fidelity · low
  > has no leisure to look down into the little wars of men, and fight them
  Plato says "the affairs (business) of men"; "little wars" sharpens the image.
  Fix: "has no leisure to look down on the business of men, and fight with them".
- `seg-0152` · register · low
  > And by the private operators who hang on the crowd
  "Operators" carries a modern slangy sense of schemer.
  Fix: "And by the private men who hang on the crowd and ache to please it?"
- `seg-0407` · register · low
  > and to the last, imaging — the reading of shadows and likenesses.
  "Imaging" now reads as a medical or technical term, and the dash phrase is a gloss.
  Fix: "and to the last, image-thinking."
