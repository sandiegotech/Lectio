# The Republic · Book IV · Justice Found
Grade: B
The argument comes through clean and exact: the four virtues, the dyed wool, the parts of the soul, Leontius. The fixes are mostly consistency across books. The central virtue changes its English name here, and a Homer line "quoted earlier" does not match the earlier quotation.

## Issues
- `seg-0118`, `seg-0176`–`seg-0208`, `seg-0392`–`seg-0393`, `seg-0416` · voice · high
  > Then clearly it is wise, it is brave, it has self-command, and it is just.
  This is the book that defines the four virtues, and it names σωφροσύνη "self-command" (19 times). Book III called the same virtue "self-discipline" (13 times) while laying the groundwork this book cites, and Book V has one "self-command". A reader following the argument across books will not know these are one virtue, so the definition here never connects to Book III's education.
  Fix: choose one term for the whole edition and apply it in Books III–V. "Self-discipline" is plainer for a young reader; "self-command" fits the "stronger than himself" passage (`seg-0184`–`seg-0188`) better. Either works, so long as it is the same everywhere.

- `seg-0365` · voice · medium
  > "He struck his chest and rebuked his own heart,"
  Socrates calls this "the line we quoted earlier", but Book III `seg-0098` renders it 'He struck his chest and called his own heart to heel'. It is also the only verse in the work set in double quotes.
  Fix: reuse the Book III wording exactly, in the edition's single-quote style: 'He struck his chest and called his own heart to heel,'

- `seg-0416` · register · medium
  > He sets his own house in order — literally his own.
  "Literally" is wrong here: the house is figurative, and Socrates means that the "house" is the man himself. Read aloud, it sounds like the modern intensifier.
  Fix: He sets his own house in order, the house that is himself.

- `seg-0086`, `seg-0097`, `seg-0351`, `seg-0364`, `seg-0411`, `seg-0419` · register · low
  > In god's name … By god, no … No, by god … Beautifully put, by god
  The same oath problem as Books I and II: the Greek has Zeus (μὰ Δία) or "the gods" (πρὸς θεῶν, `seg-0086`), and Books III and V say "by Zeus". The lowercase "god" also sits awkwardly beside "Apollo, the god at Delphi" (`seg-0108`).
  Fix: "By Zeus" for each μὰ Δία; `seg-0086` "By the gods, are we going to bring ourselves to legislate any of that?"

- `seg-0061` · form · low
  > "the newest song that hovers about the singers' lips,"
  Double quotes here; every other verse quotation in Books II–V uses single quotes.
  Fix: 'the newest song that hovers about the singers' lips,'

- `seg-0102` · fidelity · low
  > keep telling him he's six feet tall
  The Greek is "four cubits" (τετράπηχυς). Converting to modern units is a small anachronism. Fixing it is optional, since the meaning is the same.
  Fix: keep telling him he's a giant

- `seg-0285` · register · low
  > No way.
  Slang for a plain "not at all" (οὐδαμῶς).
  Fix: Not at all.

- `seg-0006` · register · low
  > with the wheel parked at their elbow
  "Parked" brings in the car.
  Fix: with the wheel set beside them
