# Republic · Book X · Poetry and the Myth of Er
Grade: B
A fine close to the work: the three beds, the ancient quarrel, and the Myth of Er are exact and moving. One passage adds an astronomy Plato never wrote, and one explains a joke inside the text.

## Issues
- `seg-0314` · fidelity · high
  > these are the fixed stars; the seventh, the sun, is brightest; the eighth, the moon, … the second and fifth, Saturn and Mercury, … the third, Venus, … the fourth, Mars, … the sixth, Jupiter
  Plato names none of these; the identifications are Jowett's bracketed editorial notes, here written into Er's vision without marking, and with Roman planet names that no Greek of the period would use. The reader takes them for Plato.
  Fix: drop every identification: "The rim of the largest is spangled; the seventh is brightest; the eighth takes its color from the seventh's light falling on it; the second and fifth are like each other, and yellower than the others; the third carries the whitest light; the fourth is reddish; the sixth is second in whiteness." Put the planet key in enrichment.
- `seg-0096` · fidelity · medium
  > Homer's own companion Creophylus — the name means something like 'son of the Meat-clan,' and people laugh at it — would look even more ridiculous
  The etymology is a gloss written into Glaucon's speech; Plato lets the name carry the joke.
  Fix: "Homer's own companion Creophylus — a laughable name — would look even more ridiculous for his education than for his name". Move the etymology to a note.
- `seg-0137` · voice · low
  > Then tell me, in heaven's name
  The Greek is "by Zeus" (pros Dios), which this book renders that way elsewhere (`seg-0208`, `seg-0236`, `seg-0268`).
  Fix: "Then tell me, by Zeus:".
- `seg-0189` · read-aloud · low
  > set our affairs against the way they have fallen, however reason picks out as best.
  "however reason picks out" doesn't parse on first hearing.
  Fix: "and arrange our affairs to suit the throw, in whatever way reason judges best."
- `seg-0316` · read-aloud · low
  > he too hated the human race because of what he had suffered, and traded into the life of an eagle.
  "Traded into" is not idiomatic.
  Fix: "and exchanged it for the life of an eagle."

- `seg-0318` "And both here, and on the thousand-year journey we have described, may we fare well."
