# The Republic · Book II · The Ring of Gyges
Grade: B
Faithful and often superb (Gyges, the speeches of Glaucon and Adeimantus, the city of pigs); the fixes are the oaths, a handful of loaded word choices, and verse formatting that differs from the rest of the work.

## Issues
- `seg-0162`, `seg-0177`, `seg-0210`, `seg-0245`, `seg-0313`, `seg-0019` · register · medium
  > My god, I said … By god, he said … Then by god … God, no, he said … God forbid … Good lord, my dear Glaucon
  The Greek oaths are by Zeus (μὰ Δία, νὴ Δία), and Books III and V render them "by Zeus". In this book the clash is worse than in Book I because the same pages argue about "the god" (ὁ θεός) as a theological subject; a lowercase "by god" beside "the god is truly good" blurs the two.
  Fix: "By Zeus" for each μὰ/νὴ Δία (`seg-0162`, `seg-0177`, `seg-0210`, `seg-0245`); "Heaven forbid" (`seg-0313`); "Good heavens, my dear Glaucon" (`seg-0019`).

- `seg-0021`, `seg-0028`, `seg-0030`, `seg-0033`, `seg-0035`, `seg-0271`, `seg-0279`, `seg-0309` · form · medium
  > 'harvesting the deep furrow of his mind, / where sound counsel takes root and grows.'
  Verse quotations are set as prose with " / " line separators and straight ASCII quotes. Book I sets its verse in curly quotes with no slashes, and the longest verse quotation here (`seg-0347`, Aeschylus) has no slashes at all. A reader sees three conventions in two books.
  Fix: settle one verse convention for the whole edition (real line breaks, or none) and use curly quotes throughout; apply it here and in `seg-0347`.

- `seg-0031` · register · low
  > Musaeus and his son promise the just even racier goods than these … Others stretch the gods' payroll even longer
  "Racier" now suggests sex, and the Greek (νεανικώτερα) means bolder or more lavish. "Payroll" is office vocabulary laid over μισθούς ("wages", which the next sentence already uses).
  Fix: "even headier rewards than these" … "Others stretch the gods' wages further still".

- `seg-0013` · fidelity · low
  > charmed by your voice like a snake by a piper
  "By a piper" is added; the Greek has only the snake being charmed.
  Fix: charmed by your voice, like a snake, sooner than he should have been.

- `seg-0017` · register · low
  > No one, it would seem, could be made of stuff so adamant that he would hold to justice.
  "Adamant" as an adjective for hardness is archaic, and today it means "insistent", which misleads.
  Fix: No one, it would seem, could be made of such iron that he would hold to justice.

- `seg-0298` · read-aloud · low
  > Then on that side, the god is the last thing in the world to take many shapes.
  "On that side" does not tell the listener which side; the point is change forced from outside.
  Fix: Then from outside, at least, the god is the last thing in the world to be pushed into many shapes.

- `seg-0126` · register · low
  > beds of strewn briony and myrtle
  "Briony" is a word few readers carry, and it is not a sure match for σμίλαξ.
  Fix: beds strewn with bindweed and myrtle
