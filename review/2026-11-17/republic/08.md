# Republic · Book VIII · The Decay of Regimes
Grade: B
A strong, lively book, faithful down to the drone-and-sting imagery and the democratic man, but a translator's note is printed inside the Muses' speech and has to come out.

## Issues
- `seg-0027` · form · high
  > [Trans. note: This is the famous ‘nuptial number,’ the most disputed passage in Plato. … Lectio renders the riddle as a riddle.]
  An editorial note in brackets sits inside the segment, right after the Muses' closing words, so the reader (and any recording) gets it as part of the text.
  Fix: delete the bracketed note from the segment; move it to the enrichment sidecar as a passage note on `seg-0027`.
- `seg-0187` · read-aloud · medium
  > keep injecting their silver into anyone else in the crowd who yields — wounding him with it — and collecting interest, that many-times-multiplied child of the parent sum, they make the drone and the beggar swarm in the city.
  The sentence loses its grammar: "keep injecting … and collecting …, they make" has no joint, and the reader stumbles at "they make".
  Fix: "keep injecting their silver into anyone else in the crowd who gives way, wounding him with it, and collect their interest, that child many times the size of its parent sum. So they make the drone and the beggar swarm in the city."
- `seg-0090` · form · low
  > It does.
  The answer doesn't fit the line before it ("what is honored gets practiced, and what is dishonored gets neglected").
  Fix: "So it is."
- `seg-0215` · register · low
  > carries every breed of constitution in stock … browse the bazaar of constitutions, and pick out whichever style suits him
  "In stock", "browse", and "style" pile up shop talk beyond Plato's single word for the general store.
  Fix: "carries every kind of constitution. Anyone wanting to found a city … should probably go to a democratic city, as to a general market of constitutions, and pick out whichever pattern suits him".
- `seg-0349` · register · low
  > stripping the haves of their property
  "The haves" is a modern political catchphrase.
  Fix: "stripping the propertied of what they own".
- `seg-0219` · fidelity · low
  > strolling the streets like a hero come back from the dead
  "come back from the dead" explains what a hero is; Plato says only "like a hero".
  Fix: "strolling the streets like a hero, as if no one saw and no one cared".
- `seg-0156` · fidelity · low
  > set a blind god, wealth, at the head of his chorus
  ", wealth," is a gloss on the blind god (Plutus).
  Fix: "set a blind god at the head of his chorus".
- `seg-0119` · register · low
  > merely a consumer of whatever was at hand
  "Consumer" brings in modern economics.
  Fix: "merely a spender of whatever was at hand".
