# The Republic · Book III · The Noble Lie
Grade: B
Strong and faithful through the poetry censorship, the doctors and judges, and the myth of the metals. The fixes: two glosses written into the sentence, and a run of modern idioms ("girlfriend", "trigger", "enforcers", "workouts") that date the text.

## Issues
- `seg-0025` · fidelity · medium
  > Cocytus, the river of wailing; Styx, the river of hate
  The glosses on the river names are written into Plato's sentence. The Greek gives only the names (Κωκυτούς τε καὶ Στύγας). The glosses belong in the enrichment sidecar.
  Fix: Cocytus and Styx, 'those under the earth', 'the sapless dead' —

- `seg-0326` · register · medium
  > Then you'll also frown on a Corinthian girlfriend for a man who means to keep his body in shape.
  "Girlfriend" is anachronistic and misleading. A "Corinthian girl" (Κορινθία κόρη) was a byword for a courtesan, and the point is luxury, not dating.
  Fix: Then you'll also frown on a Corinthian mistress for a man who means to keep his body in shape.

- `seg-0414` · register · medium
  > the music weakens his spirit until it's trigger-touchy: flaring at trifles, then guttering out just as fast. Instead of spirited, such men come out short-fused and irritable
  "Trigger-touchy" is a coinage nobody uses, and "trigger" and "short-fused" are current slang. The Greek list is plain: hot-tempered, irritable, full of bad temper.
  Fix: the music weakens his spirit and makes it touchy, quick to flare at trifles and quick to die down. Instead of spirited, such men come out hot-tempered and irritable, brimming with bad temper.

- `seg-0367` · fidelity · low
  > Sharp fellows, he said, these sons of Asclepius — the way you tell it.
  Glaucon repeats Socrates's word: "statesmanlike" (πολιτικούς), picking up "Asclepius the statesman" in `seg-0363`. "Sharp fellows" loses the echo and the joke.
  Fix: Statesmen too, he said, these sons of Asclepius, the way you tell it.

- `seg-0468` · register · low
  > The young men we've been calling guardians until now are better named auxiliaries: enforcers of what the rulers decide.
  "Enforcers" now suggests gang muscle; the Greek is "helpers and supporters of the rulers' decisions".
  Fix: auxiliaries, who help carry out what the rulers decide.

- `seg-0208` · fidelity · low
  > crown him with wool like a holy image
  "Like a holy image" is interpretation added to the sentence. The Greek says only that they crown him with wool.
  Fix: pour myrrh on his head, crown him with a wreath of wool, and escort him to another town.

- `seg-0057` · register · low
  > And they shouldn't be men who crack up laughing, either. When a man surrenders to a violent fit of laughter, it tends to trigger just as violent a swing in him.
  "Crack up" and "trigger" are colloquial and modern for a plain statement about violent laughter bringing a violent reaction.
  Fix: Nor should they be given to laughter. When a man gives way to violent laughter, it usually brings on a violent reaction.

- `seg-0390`, `seg-0416` · register · low
  > Even his workouts and his exertions … a man who works himself hard in the gym and eats like a champion
  "Workouts", "the gym" and "eats like a champion" are fitness-magazine talk.
  Fix: `seg-0390` "Even his exercises and labors"; `seg-0416` "a man who trains hard and feasts well".

- `seg-0300` onward · voice · low
  > After music, our young men get physical training.
  Book II names this half of education "gymnastics" (`seg-0214`, `seg-0216`), and Book III switches to "physical training" without saying so. A reader may take them for two different things.
  Fix: pick one term for the whole edition. "Physical training" reads better; if so, change Book II to match.

- `seg-0188` · fidelity · low
  > but with a small share of imitation in a long stretch of story. Or is that nothing?
  The Greek ἢ οὐδὲν λέγω; is "or am I talking nonsense?" As written, it sounds as if Socrates is dismissing his own point.
  Fix: Or am I talking nonsense?
