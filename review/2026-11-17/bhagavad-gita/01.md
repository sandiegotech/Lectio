# Bhagavad Gita · Chapter I · The Distress of Arjuna
Grade: B
Strong, faithful roll-call and lament. One misidentification (whose heart Bhishma cheers) and an opening sentence with a dangling participle.

## Issues
- `seg-0004` · fidelity · high
  > Then, to lift the old king's heart, the eldest of the Kurus, Bhishma
  In 1.12 Bhishma sounds his conch to cheer Duryodhana (*tasya saṃjanayan harṣaṃ*, "him" = the king who just spoke); "the old king" points the reader to Dhritarashtra, who is not on the field. Arnold's "aged king" is his own slip, not support.
  Fix: Then, to lift Duryodhana's heart, Bhishma, the old grandsire and eldest of the Kurus,
- `seg-0001` · read-aloud · medium
  > On the sacred plain of Kurukshetra, drawn up and hungry for battle — tell me, Sanjaya
  "drawn up and hungry for battle" dangles with no subject until the end of the sentence; the opening line of the poem trips. Sanskrit: *samavetā yuyutsavaḥ māmakāḥ pāṇḍavāś caiva*.
  Fix: On the sacred plain of Kurukshetra my people and the sons of Pandu have gathered, hungry for battle. Tell me, Sanjaya: what did they do?
- `seg-0005` · read-aloud · medium
  > ready to do the bidding in war of the wicked-minded son of Dhritarashtra
  The phrase "in war of" jams two prepositions together and the line loses its stress. Sanskrit *yuddhe priyacikīrṣavaḥ*, "eager to please in battle".
  Fix: eager to serve in this war the evil-minded son of Dhritarashtra.
- `seg-0006` · register · low
  > "Behold, Arjuna," he said
  "Behold" is leftover archaism; *paśya* is plain "look".
  Fix: "Look, Arjuna," he said, "at the Kurus gathered here."
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · low
  > every one of them a great warrior, mounted on his shining chariot.
  *sarva eva mahārathāḥ* is "all of them great chariot-fighters"; "shining" is borrowed from Arnold, not the Sanskrit.
  Fix: every one of them a great chariot-warrior.

## Best lines
- "Gandiva, my great bow, slips from my hand, and my very skin is on fire."
- "Even if they kill me, Krishna, I would not want to kill them — not for the lordship of all three worlds, much less for this earth."
