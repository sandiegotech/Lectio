# Bhagavad Gita · Chapter V · The Renouncing of Works
Grade: B
Faithful and readable; the lotus-leaf and the dog-eater lines are right. One mistranslated term at the close and an unsteady rendering of nirvana.

## Issues
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · medium
  > knowing me for the one who receives all offering and all worship
  5.29 *bhoktāraṃ yajñatapasām*: "the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities". *Tapas* is ascetic discipline, not worship.
  Fix: knowing me as the one who enjoys every sacrifice and every austerity
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · low
  > comes to the peace of Brahman, the great extinction.
  5.24–26 *brahmanirvāṇam*. "Great" is added, and the edition renders this one term differently in II ("peace of God"), here, and VI. Pick one and hold it.
  Fix: comes to the nirvana of Brahman, the stillness of Brahman.

## Best lines
- "Whoever acts laying all his actions on Brahman, having let go of attachment, is untouched by evil — as a lotus leaf is untouched by the water."
- "For the delights that are born of contact with the senses are themselves the wombs of pain"
