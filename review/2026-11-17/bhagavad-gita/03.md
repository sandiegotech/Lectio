# Bhagavad Gita · Chapter III · Action as Sacrifice
Grade: B
Clear and well paced, with a strong close on desire. A few added sentences of commentary and one broken link in the chain of sacrifice.

## Issues
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · medium
  > But these are not finally two.
  Not in 3.3–3.4; Krishna names the two paths (*dvividhā niṣṭhā*) and moves straight to "not by abstaining from action". The unity of the paths is argued later (5.4–5) and should not be pre-announced here.
  Fix: delete the sentence.
- `seg-0005` · fidelity · medium
  > Know that action springs from the sacred order itself, and that the sacred, all-pervading, is forever present in every offering.
  3.15 has a two-step chain: action arises from Brahman (the Veda), Brahman from the Imperishable (*brahmākṣarasamudbhavam*). The Imperishable is dropped, and "the sacred order" blurs the term.
  Fix: Know that action arises from Brahman, and Brahman from the Imperishable; so Brahman, which pervades all things, is forever present in sacrifice.
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · medium
  > let the wise make such work attractive.
  3.26 *joṣayet sarvakarmāṇi*: the wise should lead the ignorant to take joy in all their work, by working themselves. "Make such work attractive" sounds like marketing and loses the example.
  Fix: rather, doing all their own work with discipline, let the wise lead the others to take joy in theirs.
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > is fooling no one but himself; he is a hypocrite.
  3.6 *vimūḍhātmā mithyācāraḥ sa ucyate*, "deluded, he is called a hypocrite"; the first clause is an addition.
  Fix: is deluded; he is rightly called a hypocrite.
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > turns his powers to honest work
  3.7 is "undertakes the yoga of action with the organs of action"; "honest work" moralizes it.
  Fix: sets his organs of action to the discipline of work

- "It is better to do your own duty imperfectly than to do another's duty well."
