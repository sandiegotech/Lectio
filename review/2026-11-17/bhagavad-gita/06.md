# Bhagavad Gita · Chapter VI · The Discipline of Meditation
Grade: B
One of the strongest chapters: the self as friend and enemy, the lamp in a windless place, the torn cloud all land. Fix where "the eternal" stands in for Brahman.

## Issues
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · medium
  > who is without fault and has become one with the eternal. … the boundless happiness of touching the eternal.
  6.27 *brahmabhūtam* ("become Brahman") and 6.28 *brahmasaṃsparśam* ("contact with Brahman"). The edition keeps "Brahman" everywhere else; here it vanishes into a vague abstraction.
  Fix: who is without fault and has become Brahman. … the boundless happiness of contact with Brahman.
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > every desire born of his own scheming
  6.24 *saṅkalpaprabhavān kāmān*, "desires born of intention (fancy)"; "scheming" adds a note of malice.
  Fix: every desire born of his own imagining
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > comes at last to the peace that is the highest of all, the peace that abides in me.
  6.15 *śāntiṃ nirvāṇaparamāṃ matsaṃsthām*: "the peace that culminates in nirvana, that rests in me"; nirvana is dropped.
  Fix: comes at last to the peace whose end is nirvana, the peace that abides in me.

## Best lines
- "Let a man lift himself up by his own self, and never let himself sink down; for the self is the friend of the self, and the self is also its enemy."
- "Failing of both worlds, does he not simply break apart like a torn cloud and come to nothing"
