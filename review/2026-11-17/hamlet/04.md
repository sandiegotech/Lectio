# Hamlet · Act IV
Grade: B
The hard passages are cleared well: Claudius's "murdering piece" speech, the Lamord report, the willow speech. One line reverses its meaning, Claudius's royal "we" is dropped in some places and kept in others, and two verse speeches are re-lineated.

## Issues
- `seg-0235` · fidelity · high
  > and then this 'should' is like a spendthrift's sigh / that eases by hurting.
  The meaning is reversed. The source reads "That hurts by easing": a sigh relieves grief but was thought to drain the heart's blood.
  Fix: that hurts by easing.

- `seg-0235` · read-aloud · medium
  > What we mean to do, / we should do when we mean it; for this 'mean to' changes, … and then this 'should' is like
  The would/should pair is the core of the speech. Swapping "would" for "mean to" while keeping "should" breaks the pairing, and "this 'mean to' changes" stumbles.
  Fix: That we would do, / we should do when we would; for this "would" changes,

- `seg-0009`, `seg-0182`, `seg-0206` · fidelity · medium
  > It would have been so with me, had I been there. … they find me touched by this, I'll give my kingdom, / my crown, my life, and all that I call mine … that I'm made of such flat and dull stuff
  Claudius's royal "we" is turned into "I" here, but kept elsewhere ("all our majesty" in `seg-0011`, "we love ourself" in `seg-0206`). The royal plural is part of how he presents himself, and it is plain modern English, so there is no friction to clear.
  Fix: restore the plural throughout: "It had been so with us, had we been there." / "they find us touched, we will our kingdom give, / our crown, our life, and all that we call ours" / "that we are made of stuff so flat and dull".

- `seg-0098` · fidelity · medium
  > Oh, from this time forth, / let my thoughts be bloody, or be worth nothing!
  The closing couplet of the soliloquy loses its rhyme (forth/worth).
  Fix: O, from this time forth, / my thoughts be bloody, or be nothing worth!

- `seg-0098` · fidelity · low
  > but to find great quarrel in a straw
  "Greatly" (nobly) becomes "great quarrel", which loses the play on "great argument … greatly". The same speech also changes "godlike reason" to "godlike mind" with no need.
  Fix: but greatly to find quarrel in a straw … that capability and godlike reason

- `seg-0133` · fidelity · medium
  > when sorrows come, they come not as single spies, / but in whole battalions.
  The famous line is padded with "as" and "whole".
  Fix: when sorrows come, they come not single spies, / but in battalions.

- `seg-0151` · form · medium
  > That drop of blood in me that stays calm / proclaims me a bastard, cries 'cuckold' to my father,
  The verse is re-lineated; the edition's lines no longer match the source's. Speech after speech keeps the source's line breaks, and this one should too.
  Fix: That drop of blood that's calm proclaims me bastard, / cries "cuckold" to my father, brands the harlot / even here, between the chaste, unsmirched brow / of my true mother.

- `seg-0239` · form · medium
  > Soft—let me think. We'll lay a solemn wager / on your skill—I have it! / When, in the bout, you're hot and dry
  The speech is re-lineated, with 14 source lines spread over 15, so it no longer lines up with the original line for line.
  Fix: keep the source's breaks: "…If this did blast in proof. Soft, let me see. / We'll make a solemn wager on your skills— / I have it! When in your motion you are hot and dry…"

- `seg-0225` · read-aloud · low
  > that it seemed he'd merged and become half-natured / with the brave beast.
  "Half-natured" is not a word a reader knows.
  Fix: as if he'd grown one body, half his nature, / with the brave beast.

- `seg-0172` · voice · low
  > This nothing means more than sense itself.
  The line is padded. The source reads "This nothing's more than matter."
  Fix: This nothing's more than sense.

- `seg-0235` · voice · low
  > But to the heart of the matter:
  The ulcer image runs through the play's disease imagery and is flattened here.
  Fix: But to the quick of the ulcer:

- `seg-0112`, `seg-0114`, `seg-0123`, `seg-0177` · form · low
  > How should I your true love know / from any other one? … and his sandal shoon.
  Ophelia's songs lose the source's indentation and initial capitals, so they read as speech. "Shoon" is left in place, the one truly dead word in the song. In `seg-0123`, "all in the morning betime" becomes "all in the early morning", which breaks the ballad's rhyme with "Valentine".
  Fix: indent and capitalize the song lines as in the source; "And his sandal shoes"; "All in the morning betime" (gloss "betime" in the margin).

- `seg-0244` · voice · low
  > as one unaware of her own peril,
  "Incapable of her own distress" is clear in context.
  Fix: as one incapable of her own distress,

- `seg-0204` · voice · low
  > convert his chains to honors;
  The alliteration of "gyves to graces" is lost.
  Fix: convert his fetters to graces;

## Best lines
- `seg-0133`: "in which gossip, starved for facts, / won't scruple to accuse my own person, / whispering ear to ear" turns a knotty passage into clear verse.
- `seg-0244`: "which coarse-mouthed shepherds give a ruder name, / but our cold maidens call them dead men's fingers." This is exactly the light touch the edition is aiming for.
