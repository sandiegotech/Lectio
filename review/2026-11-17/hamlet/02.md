# Hamlet · Act II
Grade: B
The prose scenes are clear and lively, and the hard passages (Reynaldo, the boy actors, the Pyrrhus speech) are well cleared. But the famous lines keep getting reworded with no need, and several puns are explained away.

## Issues
- `seg-0157` · fidelity · high
  > What a piece of work is a man! … the model of all animals! … Man does not delight me
  Three of the most quoted phrases in the play are altered, though none has any friction. The source reads "What a piece of work is man", "the paragon of animals", and "Man delights not me". "Man delights not me" is echoed in `seg-0159`, so it must match there too.
  Fix: What a piece of work is man! … the paragon of animals! … Man delights not me  (and in `seg-0159`: when I said "Man delights not me"?)

- `seg-0157` · voice · low
  > this fine structure, the earth, seems to me a barren headland … in understanding how like a god
  "This goodly frame, the earth … a sterile promontory … in apprehension" are all current or near-current words, so this is an unneeded rewrite.
  Fix: this goodly frame, the earth, seems to me a sterile promontory … in apprehension how like a god

- `seg-0230` · fidelity · medium
  > Oh, what a rogue and peasant slave I am!
  The famous opening is un-inverted for no reason; an exclamation keeps its inversion naturally in modern English. The same applies to "what an ass I am" in this speech.
  Fix: Oh, what a rogue and peasant slave am I! … Why, what an ass am I!

- `seg-0230` · fidelity · medium
  > I'd have fattened all the region's vultures
  In "region kites", "region" means the sky or air and is not a possessive, and kites are not vultures.
  Fix: I'd have fattened all the region kites  (or: all the kites of the air)

- `seg-0230` · voice · low
  > Get to work, my brain! Hmm—I have heard
  The "Hmm" is added filler.
  Fix: About, my brain! I have heard

- `seg-0130` · fidelity · medium
  > Honestly, we're her private parts.
  "Her privates we" puns on intimate friends or common soldiers and on the genitals. The rewrite keeps only the crude sense and makes Hamlet's "secret parts" redundant.
  Fix: Faith, her privates we.  (or: Honestly, we're her privates.)

- `seg-0210`–`seg-0212` · fidelity · medium
  > "The muffled queen"? / That's good! "Muffled queen" is good.
  Hamlet and Polonius are reacting to an odd word, "mobled", so glossing it in the text makes their exchange pointless.
  Fix: keep "mobled" in all three segments; gloss it in the margin.

- `seg-0072` · fidelity · medium
  > More substance, with less art.
  A famous line, and "matter" in this sense is still current. The same swap in `seg-0110` ("the substance of what you read") breaks the repeated "matter" that Hamlet plays on.
  Fix: More matter, with less art.  /  I mean the matter that you read, my lord.

- `seg-0079` · read-aloud · medium
  > into the madness that now has him raving, / and all of us grieving.
  The last line hangs without a verb. The source reads "And all we wail for", meaning "which we all mourn".
  Fix: into the madness that he raves in now, / and all we mourn for.

- `seg-0073` / `seg-0075` · form · medium
  > "Doubt that the stars are fire, / doubt that the sun does move, / doubt that truth is a liar,
  Hamlet's poem loses its italics and initial capitals, so it no longer looks like a set piece inside the speech. "Doubt that truth is a liar" also changes the line's grammar for no gain. The same loss of italics affects the Pyrrhus and Hecuba speeches (`seg-0205`, `0207`, `0213`), which are a play within the play.
  Fix: keep the source's `_…_` italics for the letter, the poem and the Player's speeches, and capitalize the poem's lines: "Doubt thou the stars are fire, / Doubt that the sun doth move, / Doubt truth to be a liar, / But never doubt I love."

- `seg-0174` · fidelity · low
  > Hercules and his globe and all.
  This writes in a gloss, the allusion to the Globe theatre's sign. The source reads "Hercules and his load too."
  Fix: Hercules and his load too.

- `seg-0183` · register · medium
  > isn't out of his diapers yet.
  "Diapers" is out of period. "Swaddling clothes" is still understood.
  Fix: isn't out of his swaddling clothes yet.

- `seg-0201` · voice · low
  > For look where my cutoff comes.
  "Cutoff" is an odd word. The sense is "the thing that cuts me short".
  Fix: For look where my interruption comes.

- `seg-0021` · fidelity · low
  > by indirect means find directions out.
  The jingle "indirections / directions" is the whole point of the line.
  Fix: by indirections find directions out.

- `seg-0137`, `seg-0141`, `seg-0189`, `seg-0191`, `seg-0097` · voice · low
  > A fine one / shut up in a nutshell / Bah, bah. / on his donkey / Very well.
  None of these needed changing. The source reads "A goodly one", "bounded in a nutshell", "Buzz, buzz" (old news), "on his ass" (from a ballad), and "Excellent well".
  Fix: restore the source wording; "goodly" can become "good".

- `seg-0195` · form · low
  > whom he loved exceedingly well."
  This is a ballad quotation, and the rewrite breaks its meter.
  Fix: The which he loved passing well."  (gloss "passing" in the margin)

## Best lines
- `seg-0168`: "a whole nestful of children, little fledgling hawks, who shriek out their lines at the top of their range". This is a real clearing of "aerie … eyases" that keeps the image.
- `seg-0203`: "by the height of a high-heeled shoe" is exact and light.
