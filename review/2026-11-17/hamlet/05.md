# Hamlet · Act V
Grade: B
The gravedigger scene is excellent, its legal jokes cleared without losing their bite. Most of the ending lands, including "the readiness is all" and "the rest is silence". But two of the play's last famous lines are rewritten, the Osric scene loses its joke, and the gravedigger's malapropism is corrected away.

## Issues
- `seg-0287` · fidelity · high
  > stay away from death's release a while,
  "Absent thee from felicity awhile" is one of the play's great lines. The rewrite replaces it with a gloss ("death's release"). Only "thee" causes any friction.
  Fix: absent yourself from felicity a while,

- `seg-0293` · fidelity · medium
  > and flights of angels sing you to your rest.
  The most quoted farewell in English. This is the one place where the edition's thou-to-you rule should give way.
  Fix: and flights of angels sing thee to thy rest.

- `seg-0003` · fidelity · medium
  > when she willfully sought her own death?
  The Clown says "seeks her own salvation", a malapropism for "damnation". The edition corrects his joke away, yet it keeps his other blunders, *se offendendo* and "argal".
  Fix: when she willfully seeks her own salvation?

- `seg-0165`, `seg-0167`, `seg-0186` · fidelity · medium
  > his description loses nothing in your telling—though I know that to inventory him piece by piece … The relevance, sir? … Why is all this staked, as you call it?
  Hamlet is mocking Osric by out-inflating him. Horatio's next line ("Is it not possible to make oneself understood in plainer speech?") only makes sense if Hamlet's words are absurd. The edition has turned them into plain English, so the joke is gone, and "as you call it" now points at an ordinary word.
  Fix: keep Hamlet's inkhorn words ("his definement suffers no perdition in you … to divide him inventorially … his semblable is his mirror … The concernancy, sir?") and Osric's "imponed", and gloss them in the margin. This is friction the author meant to put there.

- `seg-0230` / `seg-0281` · fidelity · medium
  > and into the cup he'll throw a pearl … Is your pearl here?
  "Union" means a pearl and also a marriage. Hamlet's line forces the poisoned "union" on the man who married his mother. With "pearl" the pun is gone.
  Fix: an union (or "a union") in both places; gloss as "a large pearl" in the margin.

- `seg-0103` · fidelity · medium
  > my sister shall be a ministering angel / when you lie howling.
  The famous line is un-inverted with no need.
  Fix: A ministering angel shall my sister be / when you lie howling.

- `seg-0093` · form · medium
  > O, that the earth which kept the world in awe / should patch a wall to shut out winter's draft.
  The rhymed couplet (awe/flaw) is broken.
  Fix: should patch a wall to expel the winter's flaw.  (gloss "flaw" = gust)

- `seg-0212` · fidelity · low
  > Since no man has any knowledge of what he leaves behind,
  This imports the Folio reading. The base text says "has aught", meaning "owns anything".
  Fix: Since no man owns any of what he leaves, what is it to leave early?

- `seg-0178` · fidelity · low
  > in the reputation given him by those over him,
  This is a mis-gloss. "Them in his meed" means those in his service or pay (or his merit), not his superiors.
  Fix: in the reputation given him by those who serve him, he has no equal.

- `seg-0084` · fidelity · low
  > Quite downcast?
  "Chop-fallen" literally means jawless, and the joke is that the skull has no jaw. The same speech changes "fancy" to "imagination" and then repeats "imagination" two lines later.
  Fix: Quite chapfallen? … of most excellent fancy.

- `seg-0095`, `seg-0144` · form · medium
  > This shows the body / they follow took its own life with a desperate hand. / It was someone of rank. Let's hide ourselves / a while, and watch.
  The verse is re-lineated, with half-lines carried over so the breaks no longer match the source. The same happens in `seg-0144` ("It is dangerous / when the lesser sort come between the thrusts…").
  Fix: keep the source breaks: "…This doth betoken / the corpse they follow did with desperate hand / undo its own life. It was of some estate. / Let's hide a while and watch."

- `seg-0126` · fidelity · low
  > This grave shall have a lasting monument.
  "Living" is current, and it carries Claudius's double meaning (a monument that endures, and Hamlet's life).
  Fix: This grave shall have a living monument.

- `seg-0138`, `seg-0144`, `seg-0095` · voice · low
  > it did me good honest service … of mighty opponents … such cut-short rites
  These are unneeded swaps for "yeoman's service", "mighty opposites" and "maimed rites", all still understood.
  Fix: restore the source phrases.

- `seg-0120` · register · low
  > To show me up by leaping in her grave?
  "Show me up" is British slang for "embarrass". The sense is "outdo".
  Fix: To outface me with leaping in her grave?

- whole act · form · low
  Speaker names in this act keep a trailing period ("FIRST CLOWN.", "HAMLET."), while Acts I–IV drop it.
  Fix: drop the period, to match the rest of the edition.

## Best lines
- `seg-0044`: "Where are his fine distinctions now, his quibbles, his cases, his titles, and his tricks? … and not charge him with assault and battery?" This clears the legal jargon and keeps the speed.
- `seg-0184`: "I knew you'd need a note in the margin before you were through." This is a perfect light touch.
