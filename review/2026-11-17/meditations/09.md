# Meditations · Book IX
Grade: B
One of the strongest books in the edition: the prayers in IX.40, the shameless man in IX.42 and "I have thrown out all trouble" all read naturally. One phrase contradicts its own sentence, and the opening entry's last sentence is very hard to follow.

## Issues
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · medium
  > For he does fight against it who, of his own accord, moves toward what is contrary to truth
  This sentence explains the man who lies *without meaning to*, so "of his own accord" contradicts it. παρ' αὑτόν here is "in spite of himself."
  Fix: For anyone who is carried, in spite of himself, toward what is contrary to truth is fighting against it

- `seg-0001` · read-aloud · medium
  > And when I say universal nature uses them equally, I mean that they happen alike to those produced in continuous succession and to those who come after them, by virtue of a certain original movement of Providence, by which it moved from a certain beginning toward this ordering of things, having conceived certain principles of the things that were to be, and having determined the powers that produce beings and changes and such successions.
  Fifty-eight words with three "certain"s; the reader loses the thread long before the end. The Greek is dense, but it can be broken up.
  Fix: When I say universal nature uses them equally, I mean that they happen alike to everything that comes into being in its turn. This follows from a first movement of Providence. From a beginning, Providence set out to make this ordered world. It conceived the patterns of all that was to be, and it fixed the powers that bring things into being, change them, and make them succeed one another.

- `seg-0002` · read-aloud · low
  > is, as the saying goes, the next best voyage.
  δεύτερος πλοῦς is a Greek proverb for the second-best course; "next best voyage" makes the reader look for a ship.
  Fix: is, as the saying goes, the next best course.

- `seg-0028` · register · low
  > and if chance rules, do not you too be ruled by it.
  "Do not you too be" is archaic word order.
  Fix: and if chance rules, do not let yourself be ruled by chance as well.

- `seg-0042` · form · low
  > Epicurus says: In my illness, my conversation was not about my bodily sufferings, nor, he says, did I talk of such things
  A long first-person quotation with "he says" breaking in, but no quotation marks, so the reader cannot see where Epicurus stops and Marcus resumes ("Do the same, then").
  Fix: Epicurus says: "In my illness my conversation was not about my bodily sufferings," and, he says, "I did not talk of such things to those who visited me…my life went on well and happily." Do the same, then…

## Best lines
- "Today I have escaped all trouble—or rather, I have thrown out all trouble, for it was not outside me but within, in my own judgments."
- "One man prays: How may I manage to sleep with that woman? You pray: How may I not desire to sleep with her?"
