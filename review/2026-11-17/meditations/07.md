# Meditations · Book VII
Grade: B
Many of the most-quoted lines are here and nearly all are handled well ("Look within…keep digging," the emerald, wrestling not dancing); the problems are a truncated entry, a swapped pair in VII.4, and the run of poetic quotations left unmarked.

## Issues
- `seg-0061` · fidelity · medium
  > Only attend to yourself, and resolve to be a good man in everything you do; and remember...
  Lectio copies Long's trailing ellipsis, but the bundled Greek completes the sentence: μέμνησο ἀμφοτέρων, ὅτι καὶ διάφορον ‹ὃ πράσσεις καὶ ἀδιάφορον› ἐφ' οὗ ἡ πρᾶξις. A truncated segment in the flagship reads as a defect.
  Fix: …in everything you do; and remember both things: that what you do matters, and that the material you do it with does not.

- `seg-0004` · fidelity · medium
  > In the one, look at once to the end it is aiming at; in the other, watch carefully for what is really meant.
  Read in order, "the one" is conversation and "the other" is action, which pairs words with an aim and actions with a meaning — backwards. The sense is: in actions, see the aim; in words, see the meaning.
  Fix: In what is done, look at once to the end it aims at; in what is said, watch carefully for what it means.

- `seg-0039` · form · medium
  > To the immortal gods and to us give joy.
  `seg-0039` to `seg-0044` and `seg-0051` to `seg-0053` are fragments of poets Marcus copied out (Long sets them as verse). With no quotation marks or line breaks they read as Marcus's own odd aphorisms, and `seg-0053` ("With food and drink and cunning magic arts, turning the channel's course to escape death.") is not a sentence at all.
  Fix: set each as a quotation, in quotation marks, keeping the verse line breaks where Long has them; e.g. "With food and drink and cunning magic arts / turning the channel's course to escape death." / "The breeze that heaven has sent / we must endure, and toil without complaint."

- `seg-0077` · read-aloud · low
  > So do not tire of receiving what is useful by doing it for others.
  "Doing it" has no clear object; the Greek is "do not tire of being helped in helping."
  Fix: So do not tire of being helped, when it is by helping others that you are helped.

- `seg-0024` · register · low
  > the result is that all comeliness dies away
  "Comeliness" is leftover archaism.
  Fix: the result is that all its beauty dies away

- `seg-0017` · fidelity · low
  > Happiness is a good spirit, or a good thing.
  εὐδαιμονία is literally "having a good daimon"; the line only makes sense as that wordplay, and the reader has no way to see it.
  Fix: Happiness — eudaimonia — means a good spirit within, or a good state of mind.

## Best lines
- "Look within. Within is the fountain of good, and it will keep bubbling up, if you will keep digging."
- "The art of living is more like wrestling than dancing, in that it should stand ready and firm to meet whatever comes at it, sudden and unexpected."
