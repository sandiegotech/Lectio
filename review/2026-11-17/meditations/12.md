# Meditations · Book XII
Grade: B
Nearly ship-ready: the closing entry ("three acts are the whole play") and the sailor rounding the headland are exactly right. There are only two wording fixes and one long sentence to break up.

## Issues
- `seg-0009` · register · medium
  > be like the all-in fighter, not the swordsman
  "All-in fighter" is a British wrestling term; most American readers will not know it. The Greek is the pancratiast, a bare-handed fighter, set against the gladiator.
  Fix: In applying your principles, be like the boxer, not the gladiator: the gladiator drops the sword he uses and is killed, but the boxer always has his fist and needs only to use it.

- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > free from disturbance, nobly, and obedient to the god that is within you.
  τῷ σαυτοῦ δαίμονι ἵλεως is "at peace with (gracious toward) your own daimon," not obedient to it; the book ends its long sentence on harmony, not on submission.
  Fix: free from disturbance, nobly, and at peace with the divinity within you.

- `seg-0003` · read-aloud · low
  > So if you separate from yourself, that is, from your understanding, whatever others do or say, … then you will be able to spend whatever portion of life remains to you
  A single 190-word sentence with a resumptive "if you separate, I say." The Greek is one period too, but aloud it loses the reader by the third "whatever."
  Fix: break it after "speaking the truth;" into a new sentence beginning "Separate from this ruling faculty the things that cling to it…", and keep the conditional for the final clause only: "If you do this, and strive to live only what is really your life, the present, then you will be able to…"

## Best lines
- "So take away your opinion whenever you choose, and like a sailor who has rounded the headland, you will find calm, everything steady, a waveless bay."
- "You are right, but in life three acts are the whole play … So depart contented, for the one who releases you is also contented."
