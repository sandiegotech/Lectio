# Meditations · Book X
Grade: B
Some of the finest writing in the edition (the leaves, the deathbed "schoolmaster," "No longer talk … Be one"), but a translator's footnote has been printed as if Marcus wrote it.

## Issues
- `seg-0009` · form · high
  > "For there, indeed, man's life is easiest: no snow, no raging storm, no rain is there, but always Ocean sends up the gently breathing breezes of the West Wind to refresh mankind."
  This is not in the Meditations. It is Long's footnote on "Islands of the Blessed" (Odyssey 4.565–568), and Long's very next segment is his own note on the Odyssey legend. The bundled Greek goes straight from X.8 to Μῖμος, πόλεμος (X.9). Printing it as text puts Homer in Marcus's mouth.
  Fix: delete `seg-0009`. If the gloss is wanted, it belongs in the enrichment layer as a note on "Islands of the Blessed" in `seg-0008`.

- `seg-0010` · fidelity · low
  > War, amazement, sluggishness, slavery, and the mime-shows of life
  πτοία is fluttering alarm or agitation, not amazement. The Greek list also opens with the farce (Μῖμος), which gives the list its scorn.
  Fix: Farce, war, panic, torpor, slavery: day by day they will wipe out those holy principles of yours

- `seg-0023` · register · low
  > that this or that 'loves'—is wont—to come about?
  "Is wont" is leftover archaism, used to explain the Greek idiom φιλεῖ γίνεσθαι.
  Fix: And is this not why we say that a thing "loves" to happen, meaning that it tends to?

- `seg-0015` · voice · low
  > and a good guiding spirit.
  The inner daimon is "guiding spirit" here and in Book V, "divinity" in Book III, "spirit" in Book II (see the work-wide note). Here the Greek is ἀγαθὸς δαίμων, the root of "happiness" (εὐδαιμονία); one consistent term would let a reader hear that echo.

## Best lines
- "No longer talk at all about the kind of man a good man ought to be. Be one."
- "In a little while you will close your eyes, and the man who carried you to your grave will soon be mourned by another."
