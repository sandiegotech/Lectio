# Meditations · Book III
Grade: B
Faithful and often fine, but it sits very close to Long's syntax, which leaves several sentences stiff, and it inverts the master–servant image in III.3.

## Issues
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · high
  > no longer a slave to this vessel, the body, which is as far inferior to what it serves as a servant is to its master.
  The Greek says the vessel (the body) is as much inferior as the thing that serves it (the mind) is superior; "inferior to what it serves" makes the body the servant and reverses the irony that the better part is the one slaving.
  Fix: no longer a slave to this vessel, the body — a master as far beneath you as you, its servant, are above it.

- `seg-0006` · voice · medium
  > So you, I say, must simply and freely choose the better and hold to it. But the useful is the better. Well then,
  "But the useful is the better" is an imagined objection (Long sets it off with dashes); stated flat, it reads as Marcus contradicting himself before answering.
  Fix: …choose the better and hold to it. "But the better is whatever is useful," someone says. Well then,

- `seg-0006` · read-aloud · medium
  > All these things, even if they seem to fit in only slightly, suddenly gain the upper hand and sweep us away.
  "Fit in only slightly" is opaque; the sense is that they seem to sit comfortably beside the good for a little while.
  Fix: All these things may seem to sit comfortably beside the good for a while; then suddenly they take over and sweep us away.

- `seg-0011` · read-aloud · medium
  > Ask what each thing is, what it is made of, how long it is in its nature to last, this thing that now makes an impression on me, and what virtue I need to meet it
  The trailing "this thing that now makes an impression on me" is Long's word order carried over; it trips aloud and switches person mid-sentence ("Ask…me").
  Fix: Ask of whatever is now making an impression on you: what is it, what is it made of, how long is it by nature meant to last, and what virtue do I need to meet it

- `seg-0004` · read-aloud · low
  > is a kind of priest and servant of the gods, using too the divinity seated within him
  "Using too" is a calque of Long ("using too the [deity]").
  Fix: is a kind of priest and servant of the gods, and makes use as well of the divinity seated within him

- `seg-0004` · read-aloud · low
  > what sort of people they are at home and abroad, by night and by day, and what they are
  "What sort of people they are … and what they are" doubles itself.
  Fix: what sort of people they are at home and in the street, by night and by day, what they do, and whom they keep company with in their impure lives.

- `seg-0016` · voice · low
  > men who have turned themselves into women
  Kept literally here, while Book II rendered the same idea (θηλύτερος) as "weak-willed." Choose one policy for this vocabulary across the work.

- `seg-0004` · voice · low
  The inner daimon is "the divinity seated within" here, "the divinity within you" in III.5, "your divine part" in III.12, but "the spirit within" in II.13 and II.17. The word carries the whole book; fix one English term (Long: daemon; "the divinity within" works best) and use it everywhere.

## Best lines
- "You boarded the ship, you made the voyage, you have reached shore: get off."
- "A man should stand upright on his own, not be propped up by others."
