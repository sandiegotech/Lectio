# Meditations · Book I · What I Learned, and From Whom
Grade: B
Warm, clear and well paced, but it silently drops several concrete details (some of them the less comfortable ones) and gets one relative wrong.

## Issues
- `seg-0017` · fidelity · high
  > not raised too long by my father's mistress
  The Greek is τῇ παλλακῇ τοῦ πάππου, his grandfather's concubine (Long: "my grandfather's concubine"); "father's" is a factual error about Marcus's family.
  Fix: I am grateful that I was not raised any longer by my grandfather's mistress;

- `seg-0016` · fidelity · high
  > He thought of himself as no more than an ordinary citizen
  The clause just before this is dropped: τὸ παῦσαι τὰ περὶ τοὺς ἔρωτας τῶν μειρακίων, that Antoninus put a stop to the love affairs with boys (Long: "had overcome all passion for boys"). Removing it sanitizes the portrait.
  Fix: insert before it: He had put an end to his passion for young men.

- `seg-0016` · fidelity · high
  > and was indifferent to his food and to the cut and color of his clothes.
  Dropped after this: indifference to the good looks of his slaves (οὐ περὶ σωμάτων ὥρας), and the following sentences on his plain clothes from Lorium and Lanuvium and his conduct toward the tax-collector at Tusculum who asked his pardon. The Greek is damaged here, but the edition should carry what can be read, not omit it.
  Fix: …to the cut and color of his clothes, or to the good looks of his slaves. His clothes came from his house at Lorium down on the coast, and mostly from Lanuvium. Think how he treated the tax-collector at Tusculum who asked his pardon; that was his way in everything.

- `seg-0007` · fidelity · medium
  > to write plainly, like the simple letter
  Dropped before this: not to walk around the house in formal dress or do things of that sort (τὸ μὴ ἐν στολίῳ κατ' οἶκον περιπατεῖν). A small, characteristic detail of Rusticus's lesson.
  Fix: …poetry, and fine writing; not to walk around the house in formal dress, or do anything of that kind; to write plainly…

- `seg-0016` · read-aloud · medium
  > never breaking off too soon, satisfied with first impressions.
  As written it says he was satisfied with first impressions, the opposite of the source ("never stopped his investigation through being satisfied with appearances which first present themselves").
  Fix: never breaking off early because first impressions seemed good enough.

- `seg-0010` · fidelity · medium
  > to work the correct form smoothly into my reply, or by confirming the point, or by some other tactful prompt.
  The list loses its parallel ("into my reply, or by confirming") and drops "or by joining in a discussion of the thing itself, not the word" — the point of the lesson.
  Fix: but to slip in the right expression myself — in my answer, or by agreeing with what was said, or by discussing the matter itself rather than the wording, or by some other tactful hint.

- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > rather short on ordinary human warmth.
  ἀστοργότεροι is lack of natural family affection (Long: "paternal affection"), not general warmth; the point is about how patrician families treat their own.
  Fix: tend to be rather lacking in natural affection for their own families.

- `seg-0017` · fidelity · medium
  > and the good sense not to get further in rhetoric and poetry
  Marcus thanks the gods that he did not make more progress (a piece of luck), not for his own good sense; the rewrite moves the credit.
  Fix: and that I did not get further in rhetoric and poetry and the other studies that might have absorbed me, had I felt I was making progress in them.

- `seg-0017` · fidelity · low
  > that I never lost my temper and wronged any of them
  προπεσεῖν is to stumble or slip into offending them; "lost my temper" narrows it.
  Fix: that I never slipped into offending any of them

- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > not to take up fashionable hobbies like breeding fighting quails.
  "Fashionable hobbies" is added gloss and a modern word; the Greek is just "not to keep quails or get excited about such things."
  Fix: and not to keep fighting quails or get worked up about such things.

- `seg-0005` · fidelity · low
  > not to take sides among the racing teams at the Circus or the rival gladiators in the arena
  Greens and Blues are the actual names and are easy to keep; the gladiator types collapse into "rival gladiators."
  Fix: not to back the Greens or the Blues at the races, or the light-shield or heavy-shield fighters in the arena;

- `seg-0017` · fidelity · low
  > including ones for spitting blood and for dizziness.
  Drops the damaged but readable "as at Caieta, as if by an oracle."
  Fix: …for spitting blood and for dizziness, as at Caieta, like an oracle.

- `seg-0016` · register · low
  > nor a courter of popular favor
  "Courter" is awkward, and the source names the means (gifts, ingratiation, flattering the crowd).
  Fix: nor did he court the people with gifts or flattery

- `seg-0016` · register · low
  > balanced and unbeatable
  "Unbeatable" sounds like sport; ἀήττητον is "unconquered."
  Fix: whose soul is complete and unconquerable

- `seg-0017` · form · low
  Book I traditionally closes with the colophon "Among the Quadi, on the Granua" (Long seg-0019; Casaubon puts it at the head of Book II). The Lectio book has no place line at all; check Book II, and keep it at whichever end the edition's base uses.

## Best lines
- "never forced a laugh to cover irritation, and never flew into anger or suspicion."
- "he examined everything calmly, as if he had all the time in the world — orderly, steady, and consistent."
