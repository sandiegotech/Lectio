# Meditations · Book XI
Grade: B
The nine rules for dealing with offenders read with real warmth ("Not so, my child") and the short sayings at the end land; the lines quoted again from Book VII are worded differently, the Socrates exchange is unmarked, and one proper noun follows Long's guess over the bundled Greek.

## Issues
- `seg-0006` · voice · medium
  > "If the gods neglect me and my children, this too has its reason." And again: "We must not chafe and fret at what happens." And: "Reap life's harvest like a ripe ear of wheat."
  These are the same verses Marcus copied in Book VII, which Lectio renders there as "If the gods care nothing for me and my children, there is a reason for it" (VII `seg-0042`), "It is no use being angry at events" (VII `seg-0038`), and "Life must be reaped like ripe ears of grain" (VII `seg-0040`). Marcus is quoting himself on purpose; the reader should recognize them.
  Fix: use the Book VII wording here verbatim: "If the gods care nothing for me and my children, there is a reason for it." … "It is no use being angry at events." … "Life must be reaped like ripe ears of grain."

- `seg-0049` · read-aloud · medium
  > Socrates used to ask: What do you want—the souls of rational beings, or of irrational ones? Of rational ones. Of what kind
  A question-and-answer exchange with no quotation marks; the reader has to work out who answers each time.
  Fix: Socrates used to ask: "What do you want: the souls of rational beings, or of irrational ones?" "Of rational ones." "Rational beings of what kind, sound or unsound?" "Sound." "Then why do you not look for them?" "Because we already have them." "Then why do you fight and quarrel?"

- `seg-0036` · fidelity · medium
  > In the writings of the Ephesians there was this precept
  The bundled Greek reads τῶν Ἐπικουρείων ("the Epicureans"); "Ephesians" is a conjecture that Long prints in square brackets. Lectio drops the brackets and silently chooses against its own original. Either follow the Greek or take the emendation knowingly; at minimum the choice should be deliberate.
  Fix: In the writings of the Epicureans there was this precept

- `seg-0047` · fidelity · low
  > and as for sensual desire, he should keep away from it altogether
  ὄρεξις is desire in general, as in Epictetus's rule to give up desire for now; "sensual" (from Long) narrows it to lust.
  Fix: and as for desire, he should keep away from it altogether

- `seg-0028` · fidelity · low
  > from the leader of the Muses, Apollo
  "Apollo" is a gloss written into the sentence; the Greek gives only the title (Μουσηγέτης).
  Fix: from the leader of the Muses

- `seg-0043` · read-aloud · low
  > To look for a fig in winter is a madman's act; and so is the man who looks for his child
  The comparison pairs an act with a man.
  Fix: To look for a fig in winter is madness; so is looking for your child when that is no longer granted.

## Best lines
- "A man who is honest and good ought to be like a man with a strong smell, so that anyone who comes near him cannot help noticing it whether he wants to or not."
- "Not so, my child; we were made by nature for something else. You will certainly not injure me, but you are injuring yourself, my child."
