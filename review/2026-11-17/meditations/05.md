# Meditations · Book V
Grade: B
The morning-in-bed opening and the "obstacle becomes the road" passage read beautifully; the imagined objector is again hard to follow, and a few phrases misstate the Greek.

## Issues
- `seg-0006` · read-aloud · medium
  > So must a person be one of those who act this way without taking notice of it? Yes. But surely it is necessary to be aware of what one is doing,
  Three voices (objector, Marcus, objector) run together with no marking, so the bare "Yes" and the "But surely" are hard to place. Long separates them with dashes.
  Fix: "So must a person be one of those who do this without noticing it?" Yes. "But surely one has to be aware of what one is doing; they say it is the mark of a social creature to see that it is acting for society, and to want its fellow to see it too." True enough, but you misunderstand what I mean…

- `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > that is why such acts are called right acts, a word that signifies they proceed by the right road.
  The etymology belongs to the Greek word κατορθώσεις ("straight-goings"); said of the English "right acts" it explains nothing.
  Fix: and that is why such acts are called katorthōseis, "straight acts," a word meaning that they go by the straight road.

- `seg-0040` · fidelity · medium
  > give help to all according to your ability and their need;
  κατὰ τὴν ἀξίαν is "according to what they merit" (Long: "their fitness"), not their need.
  Fix: give help to all as far as you can and as they deserve;

- `seg-0008` · read-aloud · medium
  > The nature of nothing whatever brings about anything unsuited to what it governs.
  Tangled and hard to parse aloud.
  Fix: No nature, of any kind, brings about anything that does not suit the thing it governs.

- `seg-0008` · fidelity · low
  > the well-being and good fortune of Zeus, the universe itself.
  "The universe itself" is Long's bracketed gloss, written into the sentence.
  Fix: to the health of the universe, and the well-being and good fortune of Zeus himself.

- `seg-0008` · read-aloud · low
  > disease, or loss of a limb, or loss of something
  "Loss of something" is vague and echoes "loss of a limb."
  Fix: disease, or a maimed body, or the loss of what he had

- `seg-0004` · fidelity · low
  > and that bears me up as I tread on it and put it to so many uses.
  καταχρώμενον is "use up, misuse" (Long: "abuse it"); "put it to uses" drops the note of self-reproach.
  Fix: and that bears me up as I tread on it and misuse it in so many ways.

- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > As a horse runs, a dog tracks game, a bee makes honey,
  The Greek is "a horse that has run, a dog that has tracked, a bee that has made its honey": the point is what each does after finishing.
  Fix: Like a horse when it has run its race, a dog when it has tracked its game, a bee when it has made its honey,

- `seg-0024` · read-aloud · low
  > and of destiny, of which you are how tiny a portion.
  Inverted and unidiomatic.
  Fix: and of destiny, and what a tiny part of it you are.

- `seg-0041` · fidelity · low
  > When you are out there declaiming on the public platform
  The Greek names the Rostra; the proper noun is easy to keep and more vivid.
  Fix: When you are shouting from the Rostra,

- `seg-0032` · form · low
  > Never has he wronged a man in deed or word.
  A line of Homer, set apart as a quotation in Long; here it is unmarked, as is the Hesiod line split across `seg-0035`/`seg-0036`. Quotations should be marked consistently.
  Fix: "He never wronged a man in deed or word." (and set "up to Olympus from the wide-spread earth" inside quotation marks with the preceding half-line)

## Best lines
- "Or was I made for this, to lie under the blankets and keep myself warm?"
- "so that what was a hindrance becomes a help to action, and what blocks the road actually advances us along it."
