# Meditations · Book II
Grade: B
Strong, faithful and readable throughout; a handful of small slips, one of which (leaving "the company of people") can mislead a reader about death.

## Issues
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > As for leaving the company of people, if there are gods, there is nothing to fear
  "Going away from among men" is dying; read cold, "leaving the company of people" sounds like withdrawing from society. The sentence before sets up death, so the link must stay explicit.
  Fix: As for departing from among the living, if there are gods, there is nothing to fear

- `seg-0010` · fidelity · medium
  > with a kind of pain and an unwilling flinch
  λεληθυίας συστολῆς is a hidden, half-noticed contraction (Long: "unconscious contraction"), not an unwilling one; "flinch" is fine, "unwilling" changes the point.
  Fix: with a kind of pain and a flinch he hardly notices

- `seg-0010` · fidelity · low
  > more lacking in self-control, more weak-willed in his wrongdoing
  θηλύτερος is "more womanish" (Long). Replacing it is a defensible editorial choice, but it should be made once, consciously, and applied the same way across the work; "softer" keeps the image without the slur better than "weak-willed," which merely repeats "lacking self-control."
  Fix: more lacking in self-control and softer in his wrongdoing

- `seg-0001` · fidelity · low
  > So none of them can truly harm me, since no one can force me into anything ugly
  "Truly" is added, and "force me into" overstates περιβαλεῖ ("no one can wrap me in what is shameful"). Small, but this is one of the most-quoted passages.
  Fix: None of them can harm me, for no one can involve me in what is ugly

- `seg-0005` · fidelity · low
  > in order to live a calm life, like the life of the gods
  The image is a life that "flows smoothly" (εὔρους, Long: "flows in quiet"); the stream metaphor recurs across the work and is worth keeping.
  Fix: in order to live a smoothly flowing life, like the life of the gods

- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > You wrong yourself, you wrong yourself, my soul
  The Greek is a bitter imperative ("Go on, abuse yourself, my soul"; Long: "Do wrong to thyself"); the indicative loses the sarcasm that the "but soon" clause answers.
  Fix: Go on, wrong yourself, wrong yourself, my soul — but soon you will have no chance left to honor yourself.

- `seg-0017` · form · low
  Long ends the book with the colophon "This in Carnuntum" (and opens it with "Among the Quadi at the Granua"). The bundled Greek omits both, so Lectio follows its source; but the place notes are part of how most readers know the book. Decide once whether to carry them, as a line of their own, for Books I and II.

## Best lines
- "In human life, time is a mere point, the substance is in constant flux, perception is dim … life is a war and a stranger's brief stay, and lasting fame is oblivion."
- "no one loses any life but the one he is living now, and no one lives any life but the one he is losing now."
