# The Epic of Gilgamesh · Tablet II · Bread, Beer, and the Wrestling at the Gate
Grade: B
The shepherds' camp and the fight at the door are excellent; a few lines are explained into the text and the weapon-forging is skipped.

No comparison edition is bundled. Fidelity points below are checked from knowledge of the Standard Babylonian text (with its Old Babylonian sources for Tablet II) and are unverified against a bundled source.

## Issues
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > There is no shame in this. You are what they say you are.
  Enkidu's speech ends with Enlil granting Gilgamesh kingship over the people. These two sentences are added commentary telling the reader how to take the fight. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: cut both sentences; end at "Enlil himself has given you the kingship over the people."
- `seg-0016` · fidelity · medium
  > and set his face toward the cedar and the long road out of the city.
  The tablet does more before the march: Gilgamesh announces the expedition to the assembly and has the craftsmen cast the axes and swords, weapons of enormous weight. The edition moves straight from the elders' warning to departure, losing the forge scene that makes the expedition concrete. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: before the last sentence, add: "He sent for the craftsmen, and they sat down and talked it over, and they cast great axes and great swords, each weighing more than a man can lift, and hung them on the two friends."
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · low
  > Now there will never be quiet in Uruk again.” And the men were glad, in spite of themselves:
  Neither "there will never be quiet" nor "in spite of themselves" is in the source; the crowd rejoices that a match like a god has come. The added line also undercuts what follows. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: “...raised on the milk of wild beasts.” And the men rejoiced: “At last, a man to stand against Gilgamesh..."
- `seg-0009` · fidelity · low
  > That night the bed was laid for the goddess and the bride
  The source names the goddess: the bed is laid for Ishhara. Proper nouns are kept elsewhere.
  Fix: "That night the bed was laid for the goddess Ishhara."
- `seg-0012` · fidelity · low
  > the friend who saves his friend, the companion who will never let the other stand alone.
  Not in this passage. It is an echo of Ninsun's words in Tablet I, added here as commentary.
  Fix: end at "Gilgamesh and Enkidu were one heart." (or cut the gloss after the dash).
- `seg-0013` · fidelity · low
  > There is a crying in my throat and nothing to fight.
  "and nothing to fight" is added and puts a reason in Enkidu's mouth that the text leaves to his slackened arms.
  Fix: "Grief has closed my throat, my friend. My arms have gone slack; my strength has turned to weakness."
- `seg-0005` · read-aloud · low
  > What hard road has you out at this pace?
  Clumsy to say aloud; "has you out" stumbles.
  Fix: "Why are you running so hard?"

## Best lines
- "Whatever a man does, it is wind in the end. But a name — a name stands."
- "the people pressed back against the walls to watch two men try to break each other."
