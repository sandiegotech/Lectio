# The Epic of Gilgamesh · Tablet VIII · The Mourning of Gilgamesh
Grade: B
The lament is the emotional peak of the edition and is very nearly perfect. The funeral half gives up on lines that survive and puts an editor's summary in their place.

No comparison edition is bundled. Fidelity points below are checked from knowledge of the Standard Babylonian text and are unverified against a bundled source.

## Issues
- `seg-0013` · fidelity · high
  > What is certain is the shape of it: a king emptying his storehouse for a friend
  The whole segment is commentary in the narrator's voice: it summarizes, interprets ("From this grief he will not recover"), and narrates events the tablet does not contain ("he does not go home to his bed. He turns his back on Uruk"). It is a gloss presented as the epic.
  Fix: cut seg-0013 entirely. The bracketed note in seg-0014 already tells the reader where the story resumes.
- `seg-0012` · fidelity · medium
  > Only fragments survive on the clay ... The full list cannot be recovered
  A good part of the grave-goods sequence survives: a run of entries in one formula, each naming a netherworld power and a gift (Ishtar, Sin, Ereshkigal, Dumuzi, Namtar and others), ending "may he welcome my friend and walk at his side." The note understates what can be read and drops a ritual whose repetition is its force. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: render the legible entries in the formula, e.g. "For Ereshkigal, queen of the underworld, a flask of lapis lazuli. He showed it to Shamash: 'Let Ereshkigal accept this. Let her welcome my friend and walk at his side.'" Then close with a short bracket: "[Further entries are broken.]"
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > For the dead must be welcomed below, and a man goes better among them when the gods who keep that country have been given their honor.
  An explanation of Mesopotamian funeral practice written into the narration. The formula of the gifts (seg-0012 fix) makes the point itself.
  Fix: cut the sentence, and cut "that each in turn would receive Enkidu kindly ... and not be a stranger there" down to "for the gods of the underworld, so that they would welcome his friend."
- `seg-0012` · voice · low
  > Lectio does not invent it; the broken clay is left broken.
  An editorial note should not talk about the edition's virtues.
  Fix: cut the sentence.
