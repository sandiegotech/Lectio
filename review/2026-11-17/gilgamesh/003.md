# The Epic of Gilgamesh · Tablet III · The Mother's Prayer
Grade: B
Ninsun's prayer is the best thing here; around it the edition invents a few feelings and handles the broken clay two different ways.

No comparison edition is bundled. Fidelity points below are checked from knowledge of the Standard Babylonian text and are unverified against a bundled source.

## Issues
- `seg-0010` · fidelity · medium
  > She did not weep where they could see her, for she was a goddess and the mother of the king; but her heart had said what a mother's heart says.
  The tablet says only that she put out the censer and came down from the roof. The hidden tears and the explanation are invented. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: "When she had finished her prayer, she put out the censer and came down from the roof."
- `seg-0013`, `seg-0015` · form · medium
  > After this much is lost — some lines broken away from the clay, so that we cannot follow every word.
  The gap is reported twice, in two voices: once in the narrator's voice inside the story (seg-0013, again at the end of seg-0014: "and here the tablet breaks off"), and once as a bracketed note (seg-0015). The reader cannot tell which is story and which is apparatus. Pick one convention for the whole work: the bracketed editorial note.
  Fix: seg-0013 → "[Some lines are lost here. When the tablet can be read again, the heroes are making their last preparations, and Gilgamesh is leaving instructions for the city while he is away.]" In seg-0014, end at "...turned their faces to the Cedar Forest and the long road." Keep seg-0015 as is.
- `seg-0009` · fidelity · low
  > the south wind and the north, the east wind and the west, and every storm wind there is.
  The source has Shamash raise thirteen named winds against Humbaba (blast, counterblast, hurricane, whirlwind and the rest) so his face darkens and Gilgamesh's weapons can reach him. "Every storm wind there is" flattens a famous catalogue, and "let the winds be the weapons of my son" changes its point. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: "...the south wind and the north, the east wind and the west, the blast and the counterblast, the hurricane, the storm, the gale, the whirlwind: thirteen winds. Let them rise against Humbaba and darken his face, so that the weapons of Gilgamesh can reach him."
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · low
  > the kind the temple-women wear, the sign of those who belong to the god
  A gloss explaining the token, added into the sentence.
  Fix: "And she laid a token on Enkidu's neck, and she made him her son and the brother of the king."
- `seg-0012` · voice · low
  > And the holy women answered her, and they did what was done at such a time.
  Vague filler standing in for a broken passage; it says nothing a reader can picture.
  Fix: cut the sentence, or mark the break: "[The lines that follow are broken.]"
- `seg-0014` · fidelity · low
  > as men say a thing twice when they mean it
  Commentary on the repetition, added to the text. The repetition can speak for itself.
  Fix: "And once more the elders gave their charge, the same words they had spoken at the start:"

## Best lines
- "Then she spoke to the sun-god, and her prayer was also a complaint."
- "When you cross the sky by day, keep your eye on him. When you go down at night, do not let go of him."
