# The Epic of Gilgamesh · Tablet X · The Ale-Wife, the Boatman, and the Waters of Death
Grade: B
Close to ship-ready, and Uta-napishti's speech on death is the edition at its best. It needs a few cuts where the narration explains the boat.

No comparison edition is bundled. Fidelity points below are checked from knowledge of the Standard Babylonian text and are unverified against a bundled source.

## Issues
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · medium
  > the crew without which the boat cannot cross the deadly water, the ones whose touch the Waters of Death cannot harm.
  An explanation of the Stone Ones added to the narration, telling the reader the consequence before Ur-shanabi does (seg-0010). The source simply has Gilgamesh smash them.
  Fix: "He came on Ur-shanabi, and on the Stone Ones. And a blind fury came up in Gilgamesh."
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > And Siduri said to him: "Why are your cheeks so hollow, your face so cast down?
  In the SB, Siduri's "why are your cheeks hollow" comes after Gilgamesh names himself, as "If you are Gilgamesh..." (seg-0004). Here she asks it twice, once before he says who he is, which blurs the three-fold pattern (Siduri, Ur-shanabi, Uta-napishti). Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: "And Siduri said to him: "Who are you? Tell me your name." And Gilgamesh answered her..."
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · low
  > And so it was done: each pole driven once into the deadly water and then thrown away, never used twice, so that his hand never touched it.
  Explanatory gloss. Ur-shanabi's count ("a first pole, a second, a third...") already shows the method.
  Fix: "And so it was done." (or cut the sentence and go straight to "When the three hundred poles were spent...")
- `seg-0009` · fidelity · low
  > And Gilgamesh answered him as he had answered the ale-wife — who he was, and what he had done
  The SB repeats the full exchange word for word with Ur-shanabi; reporting it secondhand breaks the triple repetition the tablet is built on. Summarizing once is acceptable, but then seg-0013 should not repeat it in full either. Pick one: keep both repetitions in brief, or give the second one in direct speech.
  Fix: give Ur-shanabi's question and Gilgamesh's opening line in direct speech ("Why are your cheeks so hollow...?" "How should my cheeks not be hollow... My friend Enkidu, whom I loved, has turned to clay.") and summarize the rest.

- all segments · form · low
  > "This is surely a killer.
  Tablets X and XI use straight quotation marks ("), while Tablets I–IX use curly quotes (“ ”).
  Fix: normalize to curly quotes throughout.
