# The Epic of Gilgamesh · Tablet VII · The Doom of Enkidu
Grade: B
The House of Dust and the curses are rendered with real weight. The council dream slips between voices, Shamhat's blessing turns one line around, and Gilgamesh's answer to the door speech is missing.

No comparison edition is bundled. Fidelity points below are checked from knowledge of the Standard Babylonian text (with the Hittite prose summary for the opening dream) and are unverified against a bundled source.

## Issues
- `seg-0001` · form · medium
  > And this was the dream he told. Anu and Enlil and Ea, and Shamash of the bright sky, sat down together to judge. Because the two of us killed Humbaba
  The dream starts in the narrator's third person and then, with no quotation mark, switches to Enkidu's "the two of us". The reader cannot tell who is speaking. Keep the dream inside Enkidu's speech.
  Fix: "...I dreamed it in the night. Anu and Enlil and Ea, and Shamash of the bright sky, sat down together to judge. Because the two of us killed Humbaba..." and close the quotation after "...the dream broke off there.” (changing "the dream broke off" to "and then I woke").
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · medium
  > let you be honored, and let you be the mother of a great household.
  The SB blessing ends the other way: for Shamhat's sake a wife, a mother of seven, will be left. The blessing is a courtesan's glory, not domestic honor, and the edition reverses it. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: "Let the wife, the mother of seven, be left for your sake."
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · medium
  > He tore the door down in his heart, though he had built it up with his hands, and he wept.
  The sentence is invented. In the SB, Gilgamesh hears the door speech and weeps, and answers his friend: the gods have left grief to the living, and he will pray to them and have a statue of Enkidu made in gold. That reply is dropped, so Gilgamesh says nothing until Tablet VIII. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: replace the sentence with: "Gilgamesh heard what his friend said, and his tears ran down. 'My friend,' he said, 'the gods have given you a mind wide and deep, and yet you speak like this. The dream is hard, but the grief of it is left to the one who lives. I will go to the great gods and pray for you. I will make an image of you in gold.'"
- `seg-0008`, `seg-0010` · form · low
  > He held me down under him —” and here the words break in the telling, and a few are lost.
  The note about the broken clay is again written in the narrator's voice, and here it follows a closing quotation mark mid-speech. Tablet VII's last segment uses the bracketed note; use the same here.
  Fix: "He held me down under him. [A few lines are lost.]" and in seg-0010: "She lifted her head, and she looked at me. [The rest of the dream is lost.]"
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · low
  > his face was like the face of the storm-bird
  The source names Anzu. Proper nouns are kept elsewhere (Irkalla, Belet-seri).
  Fix: "his face was like the face of the Anzu bird"
- `seg-0007` · voice · low
  > with as much weight as he had cursed ... with the same breath that had cursed her.
  The same framing comes at both ends of the speech; the second adds nothing.
  Fix: end at "...the mother of seven, be left for your sake.”" and cut the closing sentence.
