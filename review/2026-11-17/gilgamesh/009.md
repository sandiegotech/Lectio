# The Epic of Gilgamesh · Tablet IX · The Road of the Sun
Grade: B
Mashu, the Scorpion-people and the jewel garden are all here and well paced. The decision to seek Uta-napishti has been rewritten as narrator's reflection, and the twelve-hour litany is less exact in its wording than the source.

No comparison edition is bundled. Fidelity points below are checked from knowledge of the Standard Babylonian text and are unverified against a bundled source.

## Issues
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · medium
  > All the while one thought worked in him and would not let him go. Enkidu had died, and the body of his friend had gone back to clay.
  In the SB, Gilgamesh's resolve comes right after his fear of death, in his own words: he will take the road to Uta-napishti, son of Ubar-Tutu, and go quickly. The edition moves it past the lion episode and the gap, adds the narrator's reasoning ("If any living being knew how a man escapes death, it was he"), and borrows "gone back to clay" from Tablet X. The patronymic is dropped. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: move the resolve into seg-0001, after "so I roam the open land": "I will take the road to Uta-napishti, the son of Ubar-Tutu. I will go quickly." Cut seg-0003 down to: "He set his face to the road, however long it was, and went."
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · low
  > “I am going to die,” he said to himself, “the same as Enkidu died
  The source asks it as a question ("Shall I not die too, like Enkidu?"), and the question is the line people know. The added "He had come to the road that has no turning..." is not in the source.
  Fix: "“I too must die. Shall I not be like Enkidu?” he said to himself. “Grief has come into my heart. I am afraid of death, and so I roam the open land.”" Cut the last sentence.
- `seg-0010` · voice · low
  > At four double-hours the dark was thick, and there was no light: nothing before, nothing behind.
  The source repeats one formula word for word at each double-hour. The edition varies it ("he could see nothing before him", "nothing before, nothing behind", "the same thick dark"), which weakens the litany.
  Fix: use one wording throughout: "At N double-hours the dark was thick, and there was no light: it would not let him see ahead or behind."
- `seg-0008` · read-aloud · low
  > “My heart is heavy,” Gilgamesh had said
  The past perfect, needed only because the note in seg-0007 says "he has won them over", makes the reader double back. Let the speech stand in order after the gap.
  Fix: "Gilgamesh said: “My heart is heavy..." and in the seg-0007 bracket cut "When the text returns, he has won them over."
- `seg-0014` · read-aloud · low
  > and the lapis lay blue as bramble.
  Bramble is not blue; the simile does not land.
  Fix: "and lapis lazuli grew where brambles would be."

## Best lines
- "Its two peaks lean up against the floor of heaven; its roots reach down to the underworld below."
- "At nine double-hours — he felt the north wind on his face."
