# The Epic of Gilgamesh · Tablet I · The Coming of Enkidu
Grade: B
Strong, plain, and alive; one prologue passage is missing and the first dream loses its key image.

No comparison edition is bundled. Fidelity points below are checked from knowledge of the Standard Babylonian text and are unverified against a bundled source.

## Issues
- `seg-0002` → `seg-0003` · fidelity · medium
  > Read aloud how Gilgamesh suffered, and all that he came through.
  The SB prologue goes on (I 29–44) with the hymn to the king: surpassing all kings, wild bull of Uruk who goes at the front, who opened mountain passes, dug wells, crossed the ocean to Uta-napishti and restored the shrines, ending "who can say like Gilgamesh, 'It is I who am king'?" The edition jumps from the tablet-box straight to "Two parts of him were god", so the hero is never praised before he is judged. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: insert a short paragraph after seg-0002: "He surpassed all other kings, tall and splendid. He was the wild bull of Uruk, who goes in front as a leader should and behind as the one his brothers trust. He opened the passes through the mountains, dug wells on the high slopes, crossed the ocean to the edge of the sunrise, and searched the world for life, until by his own strength he came to Uta-napishti the Faraway. He restored the shrines the Flood had ruined. Who among all the kings can match him? Who else can say, as Gilgamesh can, 'I am the king'?"
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · medium
  > and the open ground of Ishtar's temple — three square miles and a half
  The SB gives the temple precinct as half a *sar*. Dropping the half leaves the reader with sums that do not add up (1+1+1+? = 3½). "Square mile" for *sar* is also a silent conversion, but tolerable. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: "and half a square mile for Ishtar's temple — three square miles and a half, and all of it is Uruk."
- `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > I leaned my forehead against it, and they helped me, and I lifted it at last
  In the SB the core of the dream is that he loved the rock like a wife, caressed and embraced it. Ninsun's reading in seg-0015 ("the way a man leans on his wife") answers an image the dream never gave, and the axe dream repeats a love line the rock dream lacks. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: "I loved it the way a man loves his wife; I held it close. Then I lifted it, and carried it to you — and you made it my equal." In seg-0015: "You loved him as a man loves his wife, and held him close, and he will never leave you."
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · low
  > hard as the war-god himself
  The SB names Ninurta ("knit strong by Ninurta"). Proper nouns are kept elsewhere (Anu, Ea, Enlil).
  Fix: "knit hard as the war-god Ninurta."
- `seg-0012` · fidelity · low
  > “You have grown wise, Enkidu. You have become like a god.
  The SB reads *damqāta*, "you are handsome / fine", not "wise"; the wisdom has just been given in narration. Unverified against a bundled source.
  Fix: "You are beautiful, Enkidu. You are like a god."
- `seg-0016` · voice · low
  > So he read his dreams.
  Ninsun reads the dreams; Gilgamesh only tells them. The SB closes with the dreams being told (Shamhat recounting them to Enkidu). As written the line is confusing.
  Fix: "So Gilgamesh told his dreams." (or "And Shamhat told Enkidu the dreams of Gilgamesh.")

## Best lines
- "a fighter, born in silence, hard as the war-god himself"
- "His strength is like a rock fallen from the sky."
