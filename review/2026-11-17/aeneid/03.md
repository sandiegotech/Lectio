# Aeneid · Book III · The Wanderings, and the Death of Anchises
Grade: B
Clean, vivid voyaging prose, but one passage reverses Helenus's warning and the opening sentence misplaces "undeserving". The rest is polish.

## Issues
- `seg-0035` · fidelity · high
  > But Helenus had warned us to hold a course between Scylla and Charybdis, a hair's breadth either way from death; we resolved to turn back.
  This is inverted: Helenus warned them not to run between Scylla and Charybdis, which is why they turn back (Dryden: "nor dare to Scylla run").
  Fix: "But Helenus had warned us not to steer between Scylla and Charybdis, where either way runs a hair's breadth from death; so we resolved to turn back."
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · high
  > to bring down the kingdom of Troy and Priam's throne — undeserving as it was
  This reads as if Priam's throne was unworthy. "Gentem immeritam" means Priam's people did not deserve their fate.
  Fix: "When the gods had seen fit to bring down the power of Asia and Priam's people, who had not deserved it,"
- `seg-0029` · fidelity · medium
  > the holy power of Pallas Athena, who first welcomed us with the sound of her name
  This is invented: "Palladis armisonae, quae prima accepit ovantis" is Pallas of the clashing arms, who first received them as they cheered. It also adds the Greek name "Athena".
  Fix: "the holy power of Pallas of the clashing arms, who was the first to receive us in our joy,"
- `seg-0017` · read-aloud · medium
  > Or has any fortune kind enough found you again, worthy of you — Hector's Andromache, still wife to Pyrrhus?
  The clauses are knotted, and the second question reads as a statement.
  Fix: "Or has a fortune worthy of you found you again? Hector's Andromache, are you still the wife of Pyrrhus?"
- `seg-0014` · read-aloud · medium
  > and left a sickening stench behind in the screaming
  The phrase is garbled ("tum vox taetrum dira inter odorem": a dreadful cry amid the stench).
  Fix: "and through the sickening stench came their dreadful screams."
- `seg-0027` · register · medium
  > Live happily, you whose fortune is already made and behind you.
  "Fortune is made" reads as money. Aeneas means their destiny is fulfilled.
  Fix: "Live happily, you whose destiny is already complete."
- `seg-0022` · fidelity · medium
  > a body of wolves and dolphins joined to a belly of howling dogs
  The anatomy is scrambled. Virgil gives her dolphins' tails joined to a belly ringed with wolves ("delphinum caudas utero commissa luporum").
  Fix: "below, a sea-monster of huge size, with dolphins' tails joined to a belly ringed with wolves."
- `seg-0036` · form · medium
  > a god drove me at last onto your shores.
  Aeneas's narration (Books II–III) ends here without a closing quotation mark, and Book III never reopens the quote. The speeches inside the narration again use double quotation marks.
  Fix: close the quote here; open Book III with a quotation mark; use single quotation marks for the speeches inside the narration.
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · low
  > once ruled by fierce Lycurgus — an old friend to Troy, our gods joined to theirs
  It is the land, not Lycurgus, that was Troy's old friend ("hospitium antiquum Troiae").
  Fix: "once ruled by fierce Lycurgus, and long a friend to Troy, its gods allied with ours while our fortune lasted."
- `seg-0001` · read-aloud · low
  > Spring had barely begun — the first warmth, my father Anchises giving the word to trust ourselves to fate and the sea.
  A sentence fragment.
  Fix: "Spring had barely begun when my father Anchises gave the word to trust our sails to fate."
- `seg-0013` · fidelity · low
  > the Strophades — "the Turning Islands," the Greeks call them
  Virgil says only that they bear a Greek name, and the translation of that name is a gloss added to the sentence. In seg-0018, "Polyxena" is also supplied where Virgil writes only "Priam's virgin daughter".
  Fix: "the Strophades, as the Greeks name them,"; in seg-0018, "Happiest of all was the virgin daughter of Priam".
- `seg-0010` · voice · low
  > The sacred images of my gods, the household gods of Troy — the Penates — that I had carried
  Naming them three times stacks gloss on gloss.
  Fix: "The sacred images of the Trojan household gods, which I had carried out of the burning city,"
- `seg-0033` · register · low
  > and I wish to god I had stayed poor at home.
  A modern oath, as in Book II.
  Fix: "and I wish my fortune had kept me there."
- `seg-0020` · register · low
  > rolls the wheel of fortune so
  "Wheel of fortune" adds an image and calls up the game show. "Volvitque vices" means he turns the course of events.
  Fix: "and turns events in their order."
- `seg-0035` · voice · low
  > taking the deserving stranger who had earned it aboard
  The phrase says the same thing twice.
  Fix: "taking aboard the stranger, who had earned it,"

## Best lines

- "one Troy in two hearts."
