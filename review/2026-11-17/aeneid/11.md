# The Aeneid · Book XI · The Death of Pallas, the Council, and Camilla
Grade: B
The funeral of Pallas, the council debate and Camilla's last ride all land. A handful of clauses carry the wrong subject or add a moral the Latin does not make, and this book alone uses curly quotation marks.

## Issues
- `seg-0004` · fidelity · medium
  > while we walk back to him in this useless funeral pomp, owing nothing more to anyone but the powers below, and carry him his son, a dead thing that can pay no other debt.
  The debt has moved to the wrong subject. In the Latin, the dead *young man* owes nothing now to the gods above. As written, the mourners owe it, and the sentence muddles.
  Fix: while we walk sadly behind his son in this useless honor — a dead boy who owes nothing now to any god above.

- `seg-0025` · fidelity · medium
  > How many did you stretch on the earth as you died?
  Camilla is not dying yet. The Latin *quot morientia corpora fundis* asks how many dying bodies she lays low.
  Fix: How many dying men did you stretch on the earth?

- `seg-0013` · fidelity · medium
  > The gods had backed the better cause; the fresh graves and the undried tears all said so.
  This invents a moral verdict. Virgil has the anger of the gods and the fresh graves before Latinus's eyes warning him that Aeneas comes by fate.
  Fix: The anger of the gods and the fresh graves before his eyes told him so.

- `seg-0017` · read-aloud · medium
  > The man's a fraud, and his pretended dread of crossing me is the same fraud — don't waste your shaking on it, Drances
  The jump from "you" to "the man" makes it sound like Aeneas is meant. The Latin is aimed at Drances, who pretends to fear Turnus so the charge stings more.
  Fix: And you, the artful fraud, pretend to tremble at my anger to make your charge sting the more. Don't be afraid, Drances; a soul like yours isn't worth this hand.

- `seg-0027` · fidelity · low
  > snapped the head off his own spear
  The Latin *summam hastae ipsius* means Venulus's spear, not Tarchon's.
  Fix: snapped the point off Venulus's own spear

- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > all the while shaking, his spear balanced and certain.
  This reads as if Arruns trembles. In the Latin he shakes his sure spear.
  Fix: all the while shaking his sure spear, relentless.

- `seg-0014` · register · low
  > and fill the cliffs with their crying — God, the cruelty of it —
  "God" as an oath, in a pagan Greek's mouth, is anachronistic.
  Fix: — and oh, the cruel punishment of my men! —

- `seg-0015` · read-aloud · low
  > I wish, Latins, we had settled this before — and not be holding a council now
  The verb does not agree.
  Fix: I wish, Latins, we had settled this before, rather than holding a council now

- `seg-0026` · register · low
  > where the helmet ends and the gorget begins
  "Gorget" is archaic. The Latin says between breastplate and helmet.
  Fix: where the helmet meets the breastplate

- `seg-0018` · register · low
  > he shan't pay the gods' price for this with his blood
  Fix: he won't pay the gods' price for this with his blood

- `seg-0001` · form · low
  > “The hardest of it is done, men.
  This is the only book in VII–XII set with curly quotes and apostrophes. VII–X and XII use straight ones. Normalize to the house style.
  Fix: normalize punctuation across the edition.

## Best lines
