# The Aeneid · Book X · The Council of the Gods, the Death of Pallas, and the End of Mezentius
Grade: B
The deaths of Pallas, Lausus and Mezentius are rendered with real force. The book still needs fixes: one roster of names is scrambled, one parent is wrong, one simile is changed, and Juno's speech gains a sneer Virgil never wrote.

## Issues
- `seg-0005` · fidelity · high
  > he was a big-boned, hard-sinewed youth, as strong as his brother Mnestheus or as Aeneas's own father.
  The second comparison is wrong. Acmon is "no less than his father Clytius" (*nec Clytio genitore minor*), not Anchises.
  Fix: a big-boned, hard-sinewed youth, no less than his father Clytius or his brother Mnestheus.

- `seg-0005` · fidelity · medium
  > Asius and the brave brothers of Sarpedon's blood, both the Assaraci, young Haemon who was ready to die young, Clarus and Thymoetes, Tibris and Castor of Lycia.
  The roster is scrambled. There is no Haemon here; the name is Thaemon. "Ready to die young" is invented. Thymbris is the old one, and the Lycians are Sarpedon's brothers Clarus and Thaemon.
  Fix: Asius son of Imbrasus and Thymoetes son of Hicetaon, the two Assaraci, and old Thymbris with Castor, in the front rank; and with them both brothers of Sarpedon, Clarus and Thaemon, from high Lycia.

- `seg-0003` · fidelity · medium
  > You were allowed to pull your son out of the fight — to draw the coward back from danger and leave a hollow phantom in his place.
  "Coward" is added. Juno says Venus can snatch Aeneas from Greek hands and put fog and empty wind where a man should be.
  Fix: You could snatch Aeneas out of the hands of the Greeks, and set fog and empty wind where the man had stood.

- `seg-0010` · fidelity · medium
  > from the towers they let their spears fly with doubled force, like cranes against a dark southern storm, blackening the whole sky as they came on.
  The simile is changed. Virgil compares the Trojans' *shout* to the cry of Strymonian cranes flying before the south wind. Nothing about spears blackening the sky.
  Fix: from the towers they let their spears fly; and their shout was like the cry of Strymonian cranes under black clouds, calling across the sky as they fly before the south wind.

- `seg-0023` · fidelity · medium
  > No good mother will bury you; no birds of the air will wait for your bones. You'll be left for the carrion
  This contradicts itself (no birds, then carrion) and drops the fathers' tomb. The Latin: no mother will bury him or weigh him down with his fathers' tomb, and he will be left to the wild birds.
  Fix: No loving mother will lay you in the earth or weigh your limbs down with your fathers' tomb. You'll be left to the wild birds — or the flood will carry you under, and hungry fish will lick your wounds.

- `seg-0017` · fidelity · medium
  > the Fates laid hold of the son and dragged him to the war, to die under Evander's spear.
  Evander is not on the field. *telis Evandri* means Pallas's weapons.
  Fix: to die under the spear of Evander's son.

- `seg-0004` · read-aloud · medium
  > Whether it's the camp's defenders who are held in by fate or the Trojans' own blunders, I'll favor neither.
  This is garbled. The Latin says: whether the camp is besieged because fate favors the Italians, or through Troy's error and false warnings.
  Fix: Whether the camp lies besieged because fate favors the Italians, or through Troy's own blunder and misleading oracles, I'll favor neither.

- `seg-0031` · read-aloud · medium
  > I'll dress you, Lausus, in the armor I strip from this Trojan pirate's body; you'll be the trophy of Aeneas.
  "Trophy of Aeneas" reads as Aeneas's trophy. Mezentius means Lausus will be the trophy *over* Aeneas.
  Fix: you yourself, Lausus, dressed in his spoils, will stand as my trophy over Aeneas.

- `seg-0036` · fidelity · low
  > I don't fear death, and I spared none of your gods.
  The Latin *nec divum parcimus ulli* means he has regard for no god. "Your gods" narrows it.
  Fix: I don't fear death, and I respect no god.

- `seg-0005` · fidelity · low
  > like a gem set in gold against dark jet.
  Jet is invented. Virgil has a gem set in gold, or ivory inlaid in boxwood.
  Fix: like a gem set in yellow gold, or ivory inlaid in boxwood.

- `seg-0016` · register · low
  > rolling from his car to spurn the ground
  "Car" for chariot is left over from older English (it occurs again in `seg-0024`, "pitched him from the car").
  Fix: rolling from his chariot to beat the ground with his heels

- `seg-0007` · read-aloud · low
  > First Massicus, riding the Tiger over the foaming sea
  Readers will not know the Tiger is a ship.
  Fix: First Massicus, cutting the sea in the bronze-beaked Tiger

## Best lines
- "Each man has his own short day. The span of life is brief and never given back. But to stretch your fame by what you do — that is the work of courage." (`seg-0020`)
- Blind, the minds of men, blind to fate and to the lot that is coming, and blind to all restraint when good fortune lifts them up. (`seg-0021`)
