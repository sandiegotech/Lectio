# The Aeneid · Book VIII · Evander, and the Shield of Aeneas
Grade: B
A strong book: the Cacus story and the shield both carry well. Two speakers are confused, there is one invented prophecy and one invented line, and there are a few tangles.

## Issues
- `seg-0009` · fidelity · high
  > Aeneas had been watching the man's face and his eyes all the while he spoke, the whole of him.
  The subject is wrong. In the Latin (*ille os oculosque loquentis … lustrabat*), Evander studies Aeneas while Aeneas speaks.
  Fix: Evander had been watching his face and his eyes all the while he spoke, taking in the whole of him.

- `seg-0014` · fidelity · high
  > and Evander between young Pallas and the old king led the way, easing the road with talk.
  This splits Evander and "the old king" into two people. In the Latin, the king, bent with age, walks with Aeneas and his son Pallas on either side.
  Fix: and the old king walked with Aeneas beside him and his son at his other side, easing the road with talk.

- `seg-0004` · fidelity · medium
  > Here is my great house. From here my waters will one day wash the walls of mighty Rome.
  The Rome prophecy is added. The Latin *celsis caput urbibus exit* says the river's source rises among lofty cities.
  Fix: Here is my great house; my source rises among lofty cities.

- `seg-0016` · fidelity · medium
  > sword and fire ready to destroy my people — to conquer Venus a second time, in conquering them again.
  The last clause is invented. Virgil has cities sharpening the sword behind shut gates "against me and to destroy my people".
  Fix: what walls, behind shut gates, are sharpening the sword against me and to destroy my people.

- `seg-0016` · read-aloud · medium
  > But Venus, frightened for her son — for the new war, and the rising threats, were no idle fear —
  The parenthesis turns the war into "fear", and the verb does not agree.
  Fix: But Venus, frightened for her son, and not without cause, with the Laurentine threats and the hard uprising,

- `seg-0023` · read-aloud · medium
  > to carry the news to Ascanius and his father's plans.
  As written, the news goes to "his father's plans".
  Fix: to carry Ascanius word of how things stood and of his father.

- `seg-0030` · read-aloud · low
  > calling the beaten home with all his garment into his blue lap
  "With all his garment" is clumsy.
  Fix: opening the folds of his robe and calling the beaten home into his blue lap and his sheltering streams.

- `seg-0012` · register · low
  > his dead-out eyes
  Fix: his dead eyes

## Best lines
- "Dare, my guest, to think nothing of wealth; shape yourself, as he did, into a man worthy of a god" (`seg-0015`)
