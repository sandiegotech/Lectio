# The Aeneid · Book IX · Turnus at the Walls, and the Night of Nisus and Euryalus
Grade: B
The Nisus and Euryalus episode is the best writing in this half. The book does need some fixes: a few clauses are garbled or self-contradictory, three lines of the belt's history are cut, and the speeches are more colloquial than in Books VII and VIII.

## Issues
- `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > But Euryalus couldn't resist Rhamnes's gear — the gold studs of the sword-belt;
  The belt's history (Aen. 9.360–364) is dropped. Rich Caedicus once sent it to Remulus of Tibur as a guest-gift, Remulus left it to his grandson, and the Rutulians took it in war. That history is what makes the stolen belt matter.
  Fix: Euryalus took Rhamnes's medallions and his gold-studded belt, a guest-gift rich Caedicus once sent to Remulus of Tibur, which Remulus left to his grandson, and the Rutulians later took in war; he slung it over his shoulder…

- `seg-0031` · fidelity · medium
  > not with a spear, no spear would have stopped a man that size, but a whirling lance, a great javelin
  This contradicts itself ("not a spear … but a javelin"). Virgil sets an ordinary javelin against the *phalarica*, a heavy siege missile.
  Fix: no ordinary javelin would have taken that man's life; it was a great siege-spear that came hurtling in, roaring like a thunderbolt.

- `seg-0031` · read-aloud · medium
  > word reached him that the enemy was hot with fresh slaughter and had thrown his own men open to the gates.
  This does not make sense. The Latin says the enemy was hot with new killing and had thrown their gates open.
  Fix: word reached him that the enemy, hot with fresh slaughter, had thrown their gates wide open.

- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > "No," said Ascanius — "my whole hope hangs on my father's safe return.
  *Immo* here means "more than that", not a refusal. "No" makes Ascanius seem to contradict Aletes.
  Fix: "More than that," said Ascanius, "my whole hope hangs on my father's safe return.

- `seg-0015` · fidelity · low
  > in the half-light of the early dawn, Euryalus's stolen helmet betrayed him, the moon catching it
  The Latin is *sublustri noctis in umbra*, the dim shadow of the night. Dawn clashes with the moonlight in the same sentence.
  Fix: in the dim shadow of the night, Euryalus's stolen helmet betrayed him, the moon catching it

- `seg-0001` · register · medium
  > the turning day has handed you for free.
  "For free" is shop talk. It also stands for the whole book's speech register, which is heavy with contractions ("you'd", "he's pushed", "Let's go — now", "There's no fear"). The book has about 110 contractions, against about 5 in VII and none in VIII. Iris, Turnus and Nisus sound chattier here than the same speakers do in the books around this one.
  Fix: the turning day has brought you unasked. Settle one speech register for the whole work (see SUMMARY).

- `seg-0024` · fidelity · low
  > sent to Troy against the rules, lightly armed
  The Latin is *vetitis armis*, arms forbidden him because he was slave-born. "Against the rules" is flat and vague.
  Fix: sent to Troy in arms forbidden to one of his birth, lightly armed

- `seg-0029` · register · low
  > when the rainy Kids are rising
  Most readers will not know "the Kids" is a constellation.
  Fix: when the rainy stars of the Kids are rising

- `seg-0033` · fidelity · low
  > took the head clean off, helmet and all, at sword's length.
  The Latin *longe* means the head flew far off, helmet and all. It does not mean "at sword's length".
  Fix: took the head off with one stroke and sent it flying far, helmet and all.

## Best lines

- all he was guilty of was loving his luckless friend too much. (`seg-0017`)
