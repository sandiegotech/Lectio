# Aeneid · Book V · The Games for Anchises, and the Loss of Palinurus
Grade: B
Lively and well paced: the games read like sport and the Palinurus ending lands. Fix a famous maxim that has been blunted, one harsh addition to Anchises's words, and several knotted phrases.

## Issues
- `seg-0035` · fidelity · medium
  > Leave the cowards and the women behind; carry only the chosen, only the bravest hearts, to Italy.
  Anchises says only "lectos iuvenes, fortissima corda, defer in Italiam". "Cowards and women" is a sharpening the text does not make (Dryden: "The chosen youth, and those who nobly dare").
  Fix: "Carry only the chosen young men, the bravest hearts, to Italy."
- `seg-0010` · fidelity · medium
  > they believed they could win, and so they nearly did.
  "Possunt, quia posse videntur" is one of the poem's maxims, and the added "nearly" breaks it.
  Fix: "they could win, because they believed they could."
- `seg-0010` · read-aloud · medium
  > Sergestus pulled into the lead near the rock, though only by a length — not a whole length
  The phrase contradicts itself.
  Fix: "Sergestus pulled into the lead near the rock, though not by a whole length: his stern was still lapped by the Sea-Dragon's prow."
- `seg-0023` · fidelity · medium
  > "Eryx, I give you this in Dares' place — a better life for yours.
  This is muddled. The offering is a better life paid in place of Dares' death ("meliorem animam pro morte Daretis"). The same speech also drops "victor".
  Fix: "Eryx, I pay you this better life in place of Dares' death. Here, the victor, I lay down the gloves and my art."
- `seg-0004` · read-aloud · medium
  > a contest for the strong, who fight bare-fisted with the leather gloves
  "Bare-fisted" and "gloves" contradict each other.
  Fix: "a contest for the strong, who fight with rawhide gloves;"
- `seg-0029` · register · medium
  > "Poor us — that the Greeks didn't kill us under the walls of Troy
  "Poor us" sounds chatty for "O miserae", and it is repeated.
  Fix: "We poor women, whom the Greeks did not kill under the walls of Troy... We poor women, whom fate has kept alive for this."
- `seg-0016` · fidelity · low
  > and Diores third, undone by two men's bad luck.
  This is invented. Diores is not undone, because the falls raise him to third.
  Fix: "and Diores took third."
- `seg-0021` · register · low
  > if good Aeneas approves and my sponsor Acestes agrees
  "Sponsor" now sounds commercial. "Auctor" means the man who urged him on.
  Fix: "and Acestes, who urged me into this, agrees"
- `seg-0024` · voice · low
  > Aeneas did not pretend it had not happened; but he took it for a good sign.
  An awkward rendering of "nec maximus omen abnuit Aeneas".
  Fix: "Great Aeneas did not refuse the omen; he took it as a good sign."
- `seg-0012` · fidelity · low
  > so heavy that two servants could barely lift it between them
  This drops the names Virgil gives ("Phegeus Sagarisque").
  Fix: "so heavy that the servants Phegeus and Sagaris could barely lift it between them"
- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > So far the games for Anchises went well and holy.
  The phrase is awkward.
  Fix: "So far the games for Anchises had kept their holy course."
- `seg-0040` · read-aloud · low
  > To you alone he came, bringing a doomed sleep, an innocent man.
  The appositive dangles.
  Fix: "To you alone he came, bringing deadly sleep to a man who had done no wrong."

## Best lines
- "Palinurus... you trusted a calm sky and a quiet sea too far. Now you'll lie naked on some shore that has no name."
- "let us go where fate drags us and drags us back. Whatever comes, the way through it is to bear it."
