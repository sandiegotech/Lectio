# Aeneid · Book II · The Fall of Troy
Grade: B
A gripping, faithful sack of Troy that keeps nearly every famous line. The fixes are a few misreadings, two modern echoes, and the quote nesting of Aeneas's long speech.

## Issues
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > If all you Greeks are the same to you, and it's enough that I'm one of them
  The line is garbled, because "you Greeks" addresses the Greeks. Sinon says "if you hold all Greeks in one rank" ("si omnes uno ordine habetis Achivos").
  Fix: "If all Greeks are the same to you, and it's enough that I'm one of them,"
- `seg-0036` · fidelity · medium
  > Remember to tell him about his grim son Neoptolemus, and all my ugly deeds.
  The point is "degeneremque Neoptolemum", a son unworthy of his father, and "grim" loses the sneer.
  Fix: "Remember to tell him of my sorry deeds, and how his son Neoptolemus has fallen from his line."
- `seg-0006` · fidelity · medium
  > Is this what Ulysses is known for?
  "Sic notus Ulixes?" means "Is that how well you know Ulysses?" The rendering points the question the wrong way.
  Fix: "Is that all you know of Ulysses?"
- `seg-0004` · register · medium
  > We thought they were gone — gone with the wind, bound for Mycenae.
  "Gone with the wind" calls up the film title at a tragic moment.
  Fix: "We thought they had gone, sailing with the wind for Mycenae."
- `seg-0021` · register · medium
  > God, the sight of him — how changed he was
  A modern oath in a pagan mouth. "Ei mihi, qualis erat" is a cry of grief.
  Fix: "Ah, the sight of him, how changed he was"
- `seg-0007` · read-aloud · medium
  > and Troy would be standing still, and you, high tower of Priam, would still be standing.
  The phrase repeats itself, and "standing still" can be heard as "motionless".
  Fix: "and Troy would be standing now, and you, high citadel of Priam, would remain."
- `seg-0002` · form · medium
  > "The grief you ask me to bring back to life, my queen
  Books II and III are one quoted speech, but the speeches inside it also use double quotation marks, so the reader loses track of who is speaking, and the paragraphs after the first do not reopen the quote.
  Fix: use single quotation marks for speeches inside Aeneas's narration, and open each new paragraph of the narration with a quotation mark (or drop the outer quotation marks for Books II–III by editorial rule).
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > with the craft of Pallas Athena guiding the work
  Virgil writes "Palladis", and in this same book he also calls her Minerva (seg-0004, seg-0016, seg-0018). "Athena" is the Greek name, added by the edition. Same in seg-0015 and seg-0039.
  Fix: "with the craft of Pallas guiding the work"; likewise "Pallas" in seg-0015 and seg-0039.
- `seg-0023` · fidelity · low
  > with the fire god towering over it
  This drops the proper noun ("Volcano superante").
  Fix: "with Vulcan towering over it"
- `seg-0045` · read-aloud · low
  > climb up onto my neck. I'll carry you on my own shoulders
  "Neck" is a literal rendering that sounds odd, and the next clause repeats it.
  Fix: "climb onto my back. I'll carry you on my own shoulders;"
- `seg-0049` · voice · low
  > I gave it up, lifted my father onto my back
  "Cessi" is a yielding to fate, but "gave it up" reads as despair. It is the last beat of the book.
  Fix: "I yielded, lifted my father onto my shoulders,"

## Best lines
- "The one safety for the beaten is to hope for none."
- "Three times I tried to throw my arms around her neck; three times the ghost I grasped at slipped through my hands like a light wind, like a dream that takes wing."
