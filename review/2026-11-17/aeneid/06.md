# Aeneid · Book VI · The Descent to the Underworld
Grade: B
A grave, beautiful underworld: the pageant of heroes and the Marcellus lament both work. One line reverses the Sibyl's meaning, and there are a few ungrammatical phrases and modern slips.

## Issues
- `seg-0004` · fidelity · high
  > "Are you slow? Until you are, the great mouths of the awestruck house will not gape open."
  The logic is inverted: "until you are [slow]". The Sibyl means the doors will not open until he prays ("cessas in vota precesque... neque enim ante dehiscent").
  Fix: "Are you slow to pray? The great mouths of this awestruck house will not open until you do."
- `seg-0002` · read-aloud · medium
  > On its doors he carved the death of Androgeos; then the Athenians made to pay the price each year
  The sentence is ungrammatical because a verb is missing.
  Fix: "On its doors he carved the death of Androgeos, and then the Athenians, made to pay for it each year with seven of their sons; the dread urn stood there with the lots already drawn."
- `seg-0009` · read-aloud · medium
  > the brave man had attached himself to Aeneas, a no lesser lord.
  Ungrammatical.
  Fix: "the brave man had joined Aeneas, following no lesser lord."
- `seg-0031` · register · medium
  > Each of us suffers his own spirit-death
  "Spirit-death" is a coinage that no reader can unpack. "Quisque suos patimur manis" means each of us suffers his own ghostly punishment.
  Fix: "Each of us suffers the punishment that is his own;"
- `seg-0035` · register · low
  > The father-in-law sweeping down from the Alpine wall and the fortress of Monaco
  "Monaco" is a modern anachronism for "arce Monoeci".
  Fix: "from the Alpine ramparts and the citadel of Monoecus"
- `seg-0020` · register · low
  > With the guard out cold, Aeneas seized the entrance
  Slang. The Latin has the guard sunk in sleep ("custode sepulto").
  Fix: "With the guard buried in sleep, Aeneas seized the entrance"
- `seg-0008` · register · low
  > to see twice the black depths of hell
  "Hell" carries a Christian sense. The edition says "Tartarus" elsewhere in this book (seg-0016, seg-0024).
  Fix: "to see twice the black pit of Tartarus"
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · low
  > pitying the great love of the princess Ariadne ... guiding Theseus' blind feet with a thread
  Virgil names neither Ariadne nor Theseus ("reginae", "caeca... vestigia"), so both names are glosses added by the edition, and "blind" appears twice.
  Fix: "pitying the great love of the princess, untangled the maze's tricks and turns, guiding the hero's blind steps with a thread."
- `seg-0036` · voice · low
  > your art is to rule the nations with your power. Remember it. These will be your skills
  "Art" and then "skills" says the same thing twice, and it slackens the most quoted lines of the book.
  Fix: "You, Roman, remember: rule the nations with your power. These will be your arts: to set the ways of peace, to spare the conquered, and to war down the proud."
- `seg-0005` · register · low
  > And he made an end of speaking.
  A leftover archaism.
  Fix: "And he fell silent."

## Best lines
- "On they went, dim figures under the lonely night, through the dark, through the empty halls of Dis and his kingdom of nothing."
- "Poor boy — if only you could break the bitter grip of fate. You will be Marcellus."
