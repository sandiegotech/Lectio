# Confessions · Book VIII · Take It and Read
Grade: B
Close and alive through the whole conversion; a handful of small slips: one dropped cry, one sharpened number, one word in the wrong register.

## Issues
- `seg-0027` · register · medium
  > those old girlfriends of mine
  "Girlfriends" makes the line glib and dating-app current; the source is "my ancient mistresses" (antiquae amicae), old loves that still have a hold on him.
  Fix: those old loves of mine

- `seg-0010` · fidelity · low
  > How high you are in the highest, and how deep in the deepest!
  The exclamation that opens the sentence ("Woe is me!", ei mihi) is dropped, and it carries the sigh that this praise grows out of.
  Fix: Oh, how high you are in the highest, and how deep in the deepest!

- `seg-0018` · fidelity · low
  > Twelve years and more of my life had run out since my nineteenth year
  The source hedges ("many of my years (some twelve)", forte duodecim). This makes the figure firm and then adds to it.
  Fix: Many years of my life, perhaps twelve, had run out since my nineteenth year

- `seg-0003` · register · low
  > crowded with fallacies and frauds after the elements of this world
  The Pauline tag (Col 2:8) is carried over word for word and means nothing to a reader today.
  Fix: crowded with fallacies and frauds, built on the elements of this world

- `seg-0021` · read-aloud · low
  > because the moment I truly willed it, I would will it indeed
  The repeated "will it" trips when read aloud, and "indeed" is filler.
  Fix: because the moment I willed it, I would will it completely

- `seg-0030` · register · low
  > not in beds and indecencies
  "Beds" gives cubilibus literally and makes the most famous verse in the book stumble.
  Fix: not in sexual excess and indecency

- `seg-0012` · register · low
  > he seemed to me not so much brave as lucky
  "Lucky" sounds off-hand for felicior ("blessed"), which is about grace, not chance.
  Fix: he seemed to me not so much brave as blessed

- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > Go deaf to those unclean members of yours upon the earth
  "Go deaf to" is an odd phrase. The sense is "stop your ears against."
  Fix: Shut your ears to those unclean members of yours upon the earth

- `seg-0004` · form · low
  > Anubis the barking dog-god, and monster gods of every breed, weapons leveled against Neptune
  This is a quotation of Virgil (Aen. 8.698–700), set as verse in the source, but here it runs on as prose in its own segment.
  Fix: set it as three short lines, with breaks after "dog-god," and "breed,".
