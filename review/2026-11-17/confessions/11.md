# Confessions · Book XI · Time and Eternity
Grade: B
Exact and lucid all through the argument about time. The one real fault is that Augustine's key term, *distentio*, is rendered three different ways, so the reader loses the thread the book is built on.

## Issues
- `seg-0030` · fidelity · medium
  > I see, then, that time is some kind of stretching-out.
  The term (*distentio*) recurs in seg-0033 ("a stretching-out"), in seg-0039 ("my life is a stretching-apart … not by distension but by intention"), and in seg-0041 ("without any stretching-apart of your action"). The conclusion of the book, that the mind's stretching is also the soul's scattering, only lands if the reader hears it as the same word each time.
  Fix: settle on one rendering and use it in all four places, e.g. "a stretching-out" in 0030/0033, then in 0039: "my life is a stretching-out, pulled many ways at once … moving not by stretching-out but by straining-forward", and in 0041: "without any stretching-out of your action".

- `seg-0017` · read-aloud · low
  > we cannot say that time is, except because it is tending toward is not
  "Tending toward is not" is a deliberate oddity that reads as a typo when spoken.
  Fix: we cannot truly say that time is, except in the sense that it is tending not to be.

- `seg-0039` · register · low
  > My thoughts, the innermost guts of my soul
  "Guts" for *viscera* is coarse here, as it is in Book IX.
  Fix: My thoughts, the inmost depths of my soul

- `seg-0040` · read-aloud · low
  > in the mold of me that is your truth
  The phrase is knotted and the listener cannot parse it.
  Fix: in your truth, the mold that shapes me

- `seg-0034` · register · low
  > Hold on, my mind, and press hard.
  "Hold on" now means "wait a moment," which is the opposite of *insiste*.
  Fix: Keep at it, my mind, and press hard.
