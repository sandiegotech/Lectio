# Confessions · Book IV · The Death of a Friend
Grade: B
The grief passage and the "beautiful and fitting" argument are among the best in the edition. A few small repairs, plus the work-wide quotation style.

## Issues
- whole section · form · low
  > the people called ‘elect’ and ‘holy,’
  This book alone uses curly single quotes and curly apostrophes (’). Books I, III and VII use straight double quotes, and II, V and VI use straight single quotes. Pick one convention for the work.
  Fix: normalize to the house style (straight double quotes, matching Books I, III and VII) across the whole edition.

- `seg-0012` · read-aloud · medium
  > and I had been left to myself an unhappy place, where I could not stay and from which I could not get away.
  The grammar does not parse ("left to myself an unhappy place"). The Latin is *ego mihi remanseram infelix locus*, "I had remained, to myself, an unhappy place."
  Fix: "and I had become to myself an unhappy place, where I could not stay and from which I could not get away."

- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > isn’t it breaking faith with you, trusting in shams, feeding the winds?
  The Latin is *fornicatur abs te*. Elsewhere the edition keeps the sexual image ("I went whoring away from you", Book I), so softening it here breaks a thread that runs through the whole work.
  Fix: "isn't it whoring away from you, trusting in shams, feeding the winds?"

- `seg-0028` · read-aloud · low
  > And what good did it do me that at about twenty, when a certain work of Aristotle fell into my hands — they call it the Ten Categories, and at the bare name of it I would stand open-mouthed, ... pronounced it with their cheeks bursting with pride — I read it alone, and understood it?
  A 50-word dashed insertion separates the question from its answer.
  Fix: "When I was about twenty, a certain work of Aristotle fell into my hands. They call it the Ten Categories, and at the bare name of it I used to stand open-mouthed, ... And what good did it do me that I read it alone, and understood it?"

- `seg-0031` · read-aloud · low
  > I was going ugly, sacrilegiously wrong
  "Going ugly" is not idiomatic.
  Fix: "I was going so foully, so sacrilegiously wrong".

- `seg-0016` · read-aloud · low
  > It will not drop you where it descends, but will stand with you
  "It" has no clear antecedent; the Latin plural (*non te deponent quo descendunt*) refers to the fleeting parts of the self just mentioned.
  Fix: "They will not drag you down where they descend, but will stand with you, and hold, ..."

## Best lines

- "He went away — and look: he is here." (seg-0019)
