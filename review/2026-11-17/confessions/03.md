# Confessions · Book III · The Cauldron of Carthage
Grade: B
Exact, vivid, and true to the Latin, including the hard passage on justice and custom; it needs only three read-aloud repairs.

## Issues
- `seg-0020` · read-aloud · medium
  > I confess to you, Lord, what I remember of it — and I have told it often: that answer of yours through my wide-awake mother — that she was not rattled by my false reading, plausible as it was, and saw in an instant what had to be seen, which I certainly had not seen until she said it — even then that moved me more than the dream itself, ...
  Three dashed insertions before the main verb arrives, so the ear loses the subject. "Rattled" is also too casual for the moment.
  Fix: "I confess to you, Lord, as best I remember it, and I have told it often: that answer of yours through my mother, wide awake as she was, moved me even then more than the dream itself. She was not thrown by my false reading, plausible as it was; she saw at once what had to be seen, which I certainly had not seen until she said it. In the dream, the joy this devout woman would have so long afterward was foretold so long in advance, to console her in her present anguish."

- `seg-0021` · read-aloud · low
  > Go now, leave me — and as you live, it cannot be that the son of these tears should be lost.
  *Ita vivas* is an oath ("as surely as you live"); "and as you live" reads like a condition. This is one of the famous lines, and it should land cleanly.
  Fix: "Go now, leave me. As surely as you live, it cannot be that the son of these tears should be lost."

- `seg-0016` · read-aloud · low
  > Iniquity lies to itself — whether by corrupting and perverting its own nature ... or men stand guilty of raging against you ... when, in private pride, some single false thing is loved in the part.
  The list changes subject partway through ("Iniquity ... or men ..."), and "loved in the part" is opaque.
  Fix: "Iniquity lies to itself, when men corrupt and pervert their own nature ...; or they stand guilty of raging against you ..." and "when private pride takes one part for the whole and loves some single falsehood there."
