# The Divine Comedy · Paradiso: Canto VI
Grade: B
Justinian's eagle history moves with real momentum, but the speech has lost its quotation marks and two doctrinal lines read wrong.

## Issues
- `seg-0001` · form · medium
  > After Constantine turned the eagle back
  The whole canto is Justinian's speech; Longfellow opens and closes it with quotation marks, the Lectio has none, so a reader meeting the canto cold takes it for Dante's narration.
  Fix: open the speech with a quotation mark at seg-0001 and close it at seg-0048.
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · medium
  > that any contradiction is both false and true.
  Source: in every contradiction one side is false and the other true; as written the line says the opposite.
  Fix: "the way you yourself see / that of two contradictory claims, one is false and one is true."
- `seg-0030` · fidelity · medium
  > the glory of avenging his own wrath.
  "his own" points to the third Caesar; the wrath is God's (the living Justice's), avenged by the Crucifixion.
  Fix: "the glory of taking vengeance for its wrath."
- `seg-0039` · read-aloud · medium
  > and when our longings lean and rise toward that, / bent that way,
  "lean and rise … bent that way" doubles and blurs the image; source: when desires mount there, swerving so.
  Fix: "and when desires climb toward such ends, / and swerve that way, the rays of the true love / must climb less brightly upward."
- `seg-0040` · read-aloud · low
  > of what we earn against what we deserved,
  "earn" and "deserved" mean nearly the same, so the contrast vanishes; source: our wages against our merit.
  Fix: "of our reward against what we deserved,"
- `seg-0020` · form · low
  > the Isere saw, the Saone, the Seine,
  Diacritics dropped (Isère, Saône; also "Provencals" in seg-0044 for Provençals).
  Fix: restore the accents.

## Best lines
- "who had handed back / twelve for every ten that he was given."
- "begging his bread a crust at a time, / much as it praises him, it would praise him more."
