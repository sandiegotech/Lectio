# The Divine Comedy · Purgatorio, Canto III
Grade: B
Manfred's speech is excellent; two misreadings (the "quia" line and the thousand steps) need fixing.

## Issues
- `seg-0023` · fidelity · high
  > That people was still as far from us — / a thousand of our steps, I'd say — / as a good arm could throw a stone,
  Dante says that after they had walked a thousand steps the crowd was still a stone's throw off ("dopo i nostri mille passi"); here the thousand steps become the distance, which contradicts the stone's throw.
  Fix: Even after we had gone a thousand steps, / that people was still as far from us / as a good arm can throw a stone,

- `seg-0013` · fidelity · medium
  > Be content with the because, humankind.
  "Quia" here is the scholastic "that" (the fact), set against the "why"; "the because" says the opposite and is also unidiomatic.
  Fix: Be content, humankind, with knowing that it is.

- `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > and you have watched men long without end —
  Source is desire "without fruit" (in vain); "without end" changes the point, and the line reads as "watched men for a long time".
  Fix: and you have seen men long for it in vain —

- `seg-0029` · register · low
  > the leader of that lucky flock
  "Lucky" for fortunata again (see Canto II); too casual.
  Fix: the leader of that blessed flock

## Best lines
> But Infinite Goodness has arms so wide / that it takes in whatever turns to it.
> Now the rain washes them, the wind shifts them, / beyond the kingdom, almost on the Verde, / where he carried them with the candles snuffed out.
