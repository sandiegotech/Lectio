## Recurring patterns (Inferno)

1. **One word or phrase that misleads a modern reader.** Nearly every medium issue is a single literal or ambiguous choice that sends the reader the wrong way: "tomb" for the ditch (019), "pane of leaded glass" for a mirror (023), "clawed" for scratched (013), "lying on down" heard as lying down (024), "drew his back" for the muzzle (025), "alive alone" (012), "there is no help for it" for repenting in vain (011), and "his threat" for the threat of shame (017). Each is a one-line fix.
2. **Register tics, both directions.** A modern interjection "God," stands in for "Ahi/Ahimè" (016, 018), with "Damn it" (017), "lucky you" (016), "is good with me" (019), "specialist" (027), "shut down" (006) and "sluttish"/"slut" (013, 018). Leftover archaism sits beside them: "Behold" (017), "yonder" (030), "the while" (020), "shift" (023).
3. **Small glosses folded into the verse.** "And her thread" (012), "faint, hoarse" (001), "dumb and grazing" padding the Ulysses line (026), and the English translation added to "Vexilla regis" (034), which also pushes the last tercets of the Inferno a line out of step (034, seg-0041 to seg-0044). This is the same habit the Purgatorio and Paradiso reviewers flag, at lower frequency.

## Verdict (Inferno)

The Inferno is ready to ship after a light pass. It has no high-severity errors and no canto needs redoing. Twenty-three of thirty-four cantos are A. The voice is one voice from Canto I to XXXIV: plain, physical and well paced. It moves easily between Dante's high style (Francesca, Ulysses, Ugolino) and his comic and obscene registers (the Malebranche, Master Adam and Sinon), and the famous lines all survive intact ("Abandon every hope, you who enter here", "Love, which absolves no loved one from loving", "You were not made / to live like beasts", "Then fasting did what grief had failed to do", "to see again the stars"). The eleven mediums are each a one-line fix. After those, sweep for the "God," interjection and the few archaisms, and re-cut the Latin opening and the final tercets of Canto XXXIV.

## Recurring patterns (Purgatorio)

1. **Glosses that break the verse.** When the edition explains something inside the text, it often adds a line and the tercets slip. The Latin plus English for "In exitu Israel" (036) and "Beati pacifici" (051), the padded "throwing money away" line (056), and the bracketed Occitan prose inserted as three verse segments (060) all knock segments off the tercets, split sentences, and move segment ids out of step with Longfellow, which matters for Compare and the alignment spine.
2. **Single lines misread.** These are rare but real, and they are where the high and medium issues sit: the thousand steps taken as the distance (037), Giovanna turned into someone addressed (039), the "quia" reversed (037), "ermafrodito" (060), worldly goods that "bring no joy" (051), "for a better life" taken from Longfellow and not from "per morir meglio" (060), and the "sacra fame" quotation in its Virgil form, not as Statius reads it (056). Each is a one-line fix.
3. **No settled policy on Latin and closing lines.** Liturgical Latin is sometimes kept bare (049, 059, 063, 064, 067), sometimes rendered in English (046, 047, 053), and sometimes both (036, 051). The canto's closing single line is sometimes its own segment and sometimes folded into the last tercet (047, 058, 067). Small slips of register also recur: "lucky" for *fortunate* (036, 037), "let slide", "What is it with you", "gram", "embargo".

## Verdict (Purgatorio)

This is a strong modern Purgatorio. Its voice is plain, steady and often beautiful. Famous lines survive ("O empty shadows", "Siena made me, Maremma unmade me", "the sweet new style", "I crown and mitre you", "the ancient flame"), and the hard doctrinal cantos (XVI–XVIII, XXV) stay followable without being flattened. It is not ready to ship as is, but it is close. It needs one pass to fix the two high misreadings and the twenty mediums, to re-break the tercets in 036, 051, 056 and 060 so that segments line up again, to move the Arnaut translation in 060 out of the verse into a note, and to settle one rule each for Latin and for the closing line. No canto needs to be redone.

## Recurring patterns (Paradiso)

1. **Tercets slipping out of step.** In several cantos the segments drift one line off the source stanzas, so each segment ends mid-sentence and Compare and the spine pair the wrong lines. The worst case is XXX (097, seg-0012 to the end). It also happens in VII (074), VIII (075), XIX (086) and XXXII (099). The closing single line is handled differently from canto to canto: folded into the last tercet (084), split in two (087, 088), or led into by a stranded "by" (100, the last line of the poem). Other form slips: Justinian's whole speech in VI has no quotation marks (073), straight and curly quotes are mixed across cantos (072), and VII has stray leading spaces (074).
2. **Clauses turned around in dense passages.** Almost every medium fidelity issue is one inverted clause, where subject and object or before and after are swapped. Examples: "any contradiction is both false and true" (073), the stair "no one comes back down without first climbing it" (077), Beatrice "left out from among" the sights memory lost (081), the Uccellatoio prophecy pinned on Montemalo (082), the load that "throws the ship overboard" (083), "the truth … will bear witness" for the vengeance bearing witness (084), Mary following "the One it sprang from" (090), God's mirror reversed (093), and "perfect outside of it" (100). Each is a one-line fix, but each says the opposite of Dante.
3. **Small glosses and register slips.** Glosses slip into the line: "the Moon" (069), "Peter the Lombard" (077), "Felix — 'happy'" (079), "stain red" (076), "gold border" (083). Words carry the wrong modern sense: "sinister concerns" (079), "Orient" (078), "unpack" (078), "under protest" (071), "the way I see it" (094), "kicked his shoes off" (078). A few archaisms are left over: "wont" (081), "whence" (091), "knew not" (080), "joy" used as a verb (086). One cross-canto echo is broken: Thomas's "where a man grows fat" in X is quoted back as "where the sheep grow fat" in XI (078).

Comparison-edition note: in `longfellow-1867`, section 100 carries Longfellow's appendix (six sonnets, seg-0050–0063) after the last line of Canto XXXIII. That is a recipe issue in the Longfellow bundle, not the Lectio edition, but it puts apparatus into the final canto's Compare view.

## Verdict (Paradiso)

The Paradiso is the best-sounding third of the edition. Its plain, unhurried voice suits the long doctrinal cantos, and the great moments land: "And in his will is our peace", "how salt is the taste / of another man's bread", "The little threshing-floor that makes us so fierce", "my place, my place, my place", the river of light, the white Rose, and "the Love that moves the sun and the other stars". There are no high-severity errors and no canto needs redoing, but it is not ready to ship as is. It needs one pass to fix the 42 mediums, chiefly the inverted clauses in pattern 2. It also needs a structural pass: re-cut the drifted tercets in 074, 075 and especially 097, set one rule for the closing line, and put quotation marks back around Justinian (073). After that pass it is shippable.
