# The Divine Comedy · Purgatorio, Canto XXII
Grade: B
Statius's story of his conversion is the heart of the canto and reads beautifully, but a padded line breaks the tercets for eight segments and the Virgil quotation misses Dante's reading.

## Issues
- `seg-0012` · form · medium
  > The opposite vice — / throwing money away — is what I'm paying for,
  An explanatory line is added (the source has only "this excess"), which pushes every tercet from seg-0012 to seg-0019 one line late: sentences split across segments and seg-0019 is a stray single line in mid-canto. From here on every segment sits one behind Longfellow's (modern seg-0020 = Longfellow seg-0019).
  Fix: Then know this: greed was set too far from me, / and this excess of mine is what thousands / of turning moons have punished. (then re-break 0013–0019 on the tercets and drop the orphan)

- `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > 'To what, O cursed hunger for gold, / do you not drive the appetites of men?'
  This is Virgil's original line, but Dante has Statius read it as "Why, O holy hunger for gold, do you not govern the appetite of mortals?", a call to measure, which is what teaches him that spending too can be a sin. As given, the quotation no longer supports the confession.
  Fix: 'Why, O holy hunger for gold, / do you not rule the appetite of men?' —

- `seg-0034` · fidelity · low
  > tell me if they're damned, and in what ditch."
  "Ditch" suggests the pits of lower Hell; the source asks in which quarter (vico).
  Fix: tell me if they're damned, and in what quarter."

- `seg-0002` · register · low
  > "Beati," and the word "sitio" — no more.
  Bare Latin with nothing to tell the reader it is the beatitude of those who thirst for justice; elsewhere the edition uses English.
  Fix: "Blessed," and "they who thirst" — and no more.

## Best lines
> You did as one who walks the road at night / and carries the lamp behind him — no help to himself, / but he makes the ones who follow wise —
> Through you I became a poet; through you, a Christian.
