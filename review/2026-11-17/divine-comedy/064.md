# The Divine Comedy · Purgatorio, Canto XXX
Grade: A
Beatrice's arrival and Virgil's vanishing are rendered with full force; the triple "Virgil" and "the ancient flame" survive intact. Small polish only.

## Issues
- `seg-0012` · voice · low
  > had not been brought to stand and shake before her, / had not stood trembling in her presence —
  Two lines say the same thing; the doubling reads as filler, not emphasis.
  Fix: had not stood trembling, overcome, / in her presence —

- `seg-0029` · fidelity · low
  > As the snow, among the living rafters of beams
  "Of beams" is redundant and muddles Dante's metaphor (the living rafters are the trees).
  Fix: As the snow, among the living rafters

- `seg-0006` · register · low
  > a hundred, ad vocem tanti senis —
  Dante's Latin is kept, which is defensible, but this phrase sits mid-sentence and stops a reader cold; "at the voice of so great an elder" is all it says.
  Fix: a hundred, at the voice of so great an elder —

## Best lines
> But Virgil had left us empty of himself, / Virgil, the gentlest of all fathers, / Virgil, to whom I gave myself to be saved.
> I know the signs of the ancient flame.
