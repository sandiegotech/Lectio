# The Divine Comedy · Paradiso: Canto VII
Grade: B
The redemption argument is clear and well-voiced, but the tercets slip out of step in the last third and a few lines need cleanup.

## Issues
- `seg-0040` · form · medium
  > unless the Son of God had humbled himself
  From seg-0039 the stanzas drift: "to take on flesh" spills into seg-0041, each later segment carries the head of the next tercet, and seg-0043 runs to four lines. Segments no longer match the source tercets, which breaks Compare and the spine.
  Fix: re-cut seg-0039–0043 to the source tercets (seg-0040 ends "to take on flesh and be born into the world"; seg-0041 = "Now, to fill … as it is to me"; seg-0042 = "You are saying: 'I see the air … and last only a little while —'"; seg-0043 = "'and yet these things were created.' … secure against corruption.").
- `seg-0030` · read-aloud · medium
  > note this with care, / and the finest reasoning
  The aside has lost its verb for the second half; source: "if you note it with the subtlest care."
  Fix: "nor could it win them back — if you weigh this / with your finest care — by any road"
- `seg-0002` · form · low
  > that soul sang — the one on whom a double light
  Every continuation line in this canto begins with a stray leading space, unlike Cantos I–VI.
  Fix: strip the leading spaces.

## Best lines
- "that would make a man happy in the heart of fire"
- "but your own life the Supreme Goodness breathes / into you directly, and so enamors it / of himself that ever after it longs for him."
