# The Divine Comedy · Paradiso: Canto IX
Grade: B
Cunizza and Folco both come through with character, and the closing invective bites; one detail is reversed and a few stanzas are padded past their three lines.

## Issues
- `seg-0033` · fidelity · medium
  > as long as it became these graying locks —
  Source: "as long as it suited my hair," i.e. while he was young; "graying" turns it into the opposite.
  Fix: "as long as it still suited my young years —"
- `seg-0024` · form · low
  > but in the dark below, the shade grows black / as the mind inside it darkens with its grief.
  The tercet has become four lines with "in the dark" and "inside it … with its grief" added; source: down below the shade darkens outwardly as the mind is sad.
  Fix: "Up here, gladness is gained as brightness, / as down there it shows as a smile; but below, / the shade grows dark outside as the mind grows sad."
- `seg-0040` · form · low
  > she was raised up before all other souls, / the first that Christ's great triumph carried off.
  Four lines, and the last line repeats "before all other souls."
  Fix: "Into this heaven, where the cone of shadow / cast by your world comes to its point, she was raised / first of all the souls in Christ's triumph."
- `seg-0048` · fidelity · low
  > this whoring of the faith.
  Source is "adultery" (the Church as bride betrayed); "whoring" is coarser and shifts the figure.
  Fix: "will soon be free of this adultery."
- `seg-0016` · fidelity · low
  > will stain red the water
  "red" spells out what Dante leaves as "will change the water."
  Fix: "will change the color of the water that bathes Vicenza,"

## Best lines
- "are left to rot, and only the Decretals / get studied — you can see it in their worn-out margins."
- "Yet here we do not repent — we smile, / not at the sin, which never comes back to mind,"
