# The Divine Comedy · Paradiso: Canto I
Grade: B
Strong, faithful opening; a handful of knotted clauses where the Italian syntax was carried over without being resolved.

## Issues
- `seg-0002` · read-aloud · medium
  > no one coming back down can know how to tell, or can tell at all
  The doubled "can … tell" trips; source is simply "neither knows how nor is able to retell."
  Fix: "and seen things that no one coming back down / knows how to tell, or has the power to."
- `seg-0005` · read-aloud · medium
  > as you ask of the one you give the beloved laurel
  The relative clause tangles; source: such a vessel as your beloved laurel requires of the one who receives it.
  Fix: "as you require before you give your beloved laurel."
- `seg-0018` · fidelity · medium
  > I made my own, / and fixed my eyes on the sun past all our power
  "made my own" has no object, and "past all our power" sharpens "oltre nostr'uso" (beyond our custom).
  Fix: "so her act, poured through my eyes into my mind, / became my own, and I fixed my eyes / on the sun beyond our custom —"
- `seg-0022` · fidelity · medium
  > drawn up from there above
  Source: he fixed his eyes on her, having drawn them away from up there (the sun). The Lectio reverses the direction.
  Fix: "fixed mine on her, drawn back down from the sun;"
- `seg-0024` · fidelity · low
  > To say in words what it is to pass beyond the human
  Dante's coinage "trasumanar" is the famous word here; the paraphrase is clear but loses the single-word shock.
  Fix: "To pass beyond the human cannot be told / in words; let the example serve …"

## Best lines
- "A great flame follows a little spark"
- "lightning, fleeing the place it belongs to, / never ran as you do, climbing home."
