# The Divine Comedy · Purgatorio, Canto XXVI
Grade: B
Guinizelli and Arnaut are handled with warmth, but two lines misread the sin and Dante's phrase, and the Occitan translation is inserted as bracketed apparatus inside the verse.

## Issues
- `seg-0028` · fidelity · medium
  > Our own offense was of both kinds at once;
  "Ermafrodito" means their sin was with the other sex (as against the Sodomites); "of both kinds at once" suggests they committed both sins.
  Fix: Our own sin was with the other sex;

- `seg-0025` · fidelity · medium
  > "Blessed are you," said the one who had questioned me first, / starting again, "who for a better life / take on a cargo of our borderlands.
  The source is "per morir meglio", to die better, and what Dante takes on board is experience of these regions; "for a better life" follows Longfellow, not Dante, and "a cargo of our borderlands" is opaque.
  Fix: "Blessed are you," said the one who had questioned me first, / starting again, "who take on board, to die better, / the knowledge of our borderlands.

- `seg-0050` · form · medium
  > [The Occitan, in plain English: "Your courteous question
  A bracketed prose gloss runs as three extra verse segments (0050–0052) inside the canto: editorial apparatus in the text, and it pushes the segment count past Longfellow's (53 vs 51). Keeping Arnaut's Occitan is right, as Dante did.
  Fix: Remove seg-0050–0052 from the verse and carry the English as a note (enrichment sidecar or footnote), as Longfellow does.

- `seg-0039` · fidelity · low
  > was a better craftsman of the mother tongue.
  "Il miglior fabbro" is a phrase readers know as "the better smith"; "craftsman" loses it.
  Fix: was the better smith of the mother tongue.

## Best lines
> which, as long as this modern way of writing lasts, / will make even their ink precious forever.
> the way a fish goes down through water.
