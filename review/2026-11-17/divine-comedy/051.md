# The Divine Comedy · Purgatorio, Canto XVII
Grade: B
The visions of wrath and Virgil's map of love are clear and well argued; one doctrinal line is skewed and two tercets swell to four lines.

## Issues
- `seg-0045` · fidelity · medium
  > There is another good that brings no joy;
  Dante's point is that worldly goods do not make us happy (blessed), not that they give no pleasure; the next tercet's "too far" only makes sense if they do please.
  Fix: There is another good that does not make us happy;

- `seg-0023` · form · medium
  > "Beati pacifici — blessed are they / who are free of evil anger."
  The tercet runs to four lines, and the English gloss inside the quotation is added (Dante's own gloss is only "who are without evil anger"). Same Latin-plus-English habit as Canto II.
  Fix: felt near me something move, as of wings, / and a fanning across my face, and a voice: "Blessed / are the peacemakers, who are free of evil anger!"

- `seg-0031` · form · low
  > either love of nature or love of the mind; / and this you know.
  "And this you know" is set as a fourth line, breaking the tercet.
  Fix: Join it to the third line: either love of nature or of the mind; and this you know.

## Best lines
> for he who sees the need and waits to be asked / is already leaning, in malice, toward no.
> Our feet may rest — let your speech not rest.
