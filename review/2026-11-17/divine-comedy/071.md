# The Divine Comedy · Paradiso: Canto IV
Grade: B
The doctrinal argument reads cleanly and the opening dilemma is vivid; two clauses misstate Beatrice's point and one is padded.

## Issues
- `seg-0013` · fidelity · medium
  > to give a sign / of the heaven that stands the lowest of them all
  Source: a sign of the celestial rank "that has least ascent," i.e. the lowest degree of bliss; saying "the lowest heaven" undercuts her point that they do not live in this sphere.
  Fix: "but to give a sign / of the rank of glory that rises least high."
- `seg-0023` · fidelity · medium
  > is a matter for faith to wrestle with — a hard / belief, not a heresy that stains the soul.
  Source: it is "an argument of faith, not of heretical wickedness" (the seeming injustice is itself occasion for faith). "wrestle with" and "a hard belief" are explanation added to the text.
  Fix: "is an argument for faith, / and not for heresy and sin."
- `seg-0019` · fidelity · low
  > and worth more than a quick dismissal grants.
  Padding; Longfellow and the Italian end at "not to be derided."
  Fix: "than the words sound, and may well hold / a meaning that is not to be mocked."
- `seg-0010` · register · low
  > Not the Seraph most in-Godded of them all
  The hyphenated coinage trips the ear; the rest of the edition does not imitate Dante's neologisms this way.
  Fix: "Not the Seraph most deeply drawn into God"
- `seg-0034` · register · low
  > men do under protest
  "Under protest" is a legal-procedural idiom; source is "against their liking."
  Fix: "men do against their will"

## Best lines
- "In that Truth it rests, like a wild beast in its den, / the moment it arrives — and it can arrive;"
- "for he appears to mean just what he says."
