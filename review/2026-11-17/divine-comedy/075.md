# The Divine Comedy · Paradiso: Canto VIII
Grade: B
Charles Martel's voice is warm and the argument on inherited nature lands, but the last third drifts off its tercets and a few sentences tangle.

## Issues
- `seg-0039` · form · medium
  > as no part of a city?" "Yes," I said,
  From here to the end each segment carries the opening of the next source tercet ("and here I ask no reason why" belongs to seg-0039; seg-0042 ends mid-name "and one the man"; seg-0049 ends "and crown"). Segments no longer match the source stanzas, which breaks Compare and the spine.
  Fix: re-cut seg-0039–0050 to the source tercets, as Longfellow divides them.
- `seg-0041` · fidelity · medium
  > "No — not if your own master writes it true."
  In the source Charles answers his own question; set in fresh quotation marks after "he went on," it reads as Dante's reply.
  Fix: keep it inside Charles's speech: "…for offices that differ? / No — not if your master Aristotle writes it true." (or simply drop the quote break: "…for offices that differ? No, / not if your own master writes it true.")
- `seg-0022` · read-aloud · medium
  > flashed upon / my brow whose waters are the Danube's
  The relative clause attaches to "my brow"; source: the crown of the land the Danube waters after it leaves the German borders.
  Fix: "Already on my brow there flashed the crown / of the land the Danube waters, once it leaves / the German borders far behind;"
- `seg-0049` · read-aloud · medium
  > and crown / a man fit only for the pulpit king;
  "crown … king" split across a line and a segment, with "king" stranded at the end, makes the sentence hard to parse aloud.
  Fix: "…one born to buckle on the sword, / and make a king of one who was born to preach;"
- `seg-0043` · fidelity · low
  > The wheeling heavens, a seal pressed into mortal wax,
  The heavens are the seal that presses the wax, not something pressed into it.
  Fix: "The wheeling heavens, the seal for mortal wax,"
