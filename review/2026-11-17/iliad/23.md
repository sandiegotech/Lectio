# The Iliad · Book XXIII · The Funeral Games for Patroclus
Grade: B
The ghost of Patroclus, the pyre and the chariot race are excellent. But a translator's note has been left in the text, and the register slips into modern profanity and sports talk more here than anywhere else in the edition.

## Issues
- `seg-0051` · form · high
  > [Trans. note: Achilles ends the games by handing Agamemnon a prize he never has to throw for — a deliberate gesture of honor …]
  This is editorial commentary printed as a segment of the poem. Nothing in the source corresponds to it.
  Fix: delete the segment from the text. If wanted, move it to the enrichment sidecar as a passage note.
- `seg-0045` · register · medium
  > "Damn it — the goddess fouled my running."
  The profanity is anachronistic. The source has only an exclamation of dismay (Butler: "Alas, the goddess has spoiled my running").
  Fix: "Curse it — the goddess fouled my running."
- `seg-0028` · register · medium
  > To hell with you — we Greeks were fools to call you a man of sense.
  "To hell" brings in a Christian afterlife. The source says "go to ruin" or "be gone".
  Fix: Go to ruin — we Greeks were fools to call you a man of sense.
- `seg-0046` · read-aloud · medium
  > and yet they call him green old age
  The idiom is garbled. The source says his old age is a vigorous ("green") one.
  Fix: and yet they say his is a green old age.
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · low
  > I had killed another child — by an unlucky accident, a quarrel over the dice
  The victim's name is dropped (Butler: "the son of Amphidamas"). seg-0021 likewise drops "son of Anchises" after Echepolus.
  Fix: I had killed the son of Amphidamas — by an unlucky accident … / Echepolus, son of Anchises, had given the mare …
- `seg-0013` · register · low
  > there is a time to mourn, and a time to have done with mourning
  The cadence is from Ecclesiastes. The source says men can have their fill even of lament.
  Fix: men can have their fill even of grieving.
- `seg-0043` · register · low
  > straining for the win and the tripod
  Sports-page "win" recurs in seg-0044 ("The win is with you both") and seg-0045 ("strained for the win").
  Fix: straining for victory and the tripod / The victory is yours, both of you / strained to win.
- `seg-0022` · register · low
  > Hug it close as you wheel.
  The idiom is too colloquial for Nestor. "Car" for chariot also recurs throughout the race (seg-0020, seg-0022, seg-0025, seg-0027, seg-0033, seg-0034, seg-0035, seg-0037).
  Fix: Keep close to it as you wheel. Use "chariot" throughout.
- `seg-0027` · register · low
  > this is reckless driving!
  The source is literal, but in a modern ear it sounds like a traffic ticket.
  Fix: you're driving like a madman!

## Best lines
- seg-0009: "There it is, then — even in the house of the dead something remains, a soul and a phantom, but no mind in it at all."
- seg-0038: "and his anger melted — as the dew softens the standing grain when the fields bristle with the ripening harvest."
