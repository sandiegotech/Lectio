# The Iliad · Book V · The Day of Diomedes
Grade: B
A thrilling, faithful aristeia, with the god-wounding scenes at full strength. One invented closing line and a few words that carry the wrong sense today.

## Issues
- `seg-0058` · fidelity · medium
  > And so the gods withdrew from the field.
  This segment has no source. Book V ends with Hera and Athena returning to the house of Zeus (Butler seg-0068, Pope's last lines). The idea is Book VI's opening line, which the edition already renders there ("Now the gods had left the field..."). It is an added summary and a doubled beat.
  Fix: delete the segment, or if segment ids must stand, leave it empty per pipeline practice and flag it for the human gate.

- `seg-0033` · register · medium
  > at the round bucklers and the light hide targets
  "Targets" (an old word for small shields) is carried over from Butler; today's reader hears archery targets.
  Fix: "at the round shields and the light, fluttering hide shields"

- `seg-0043` · register · low
  > a rich man from Paesus who owned much corn land
  "Corn" in the British sense; an American reader sees maize.
  Fix: "who owned much grain land"

- `seg-0030` · register · low
  > Reckless man, brute, who did not shrink from his sins
  "Sins" brings in a Christian frame. The Greek says he did not care about doing wrong.
  Fix: "Reckless man, brute, who thought nothing of doing wrong"

- `seg-0012` · fidelity · low
  > the blood came spurting out through the rings of his shirt
  "Rings" suggests chain mail, which is anachronistic. The Greek is the "plaited/twisted tunic".
  Fix: "the blood came spurting up through the woven tunic"

## Best lines

- "As fig juice curdles white milk, liquid one moment and thickened in a moment under the stirring hand, so swiftly he healed furious Ares."
