# The Iliad · Book II · The Dream and the Ships
Grade: B
Close and complete, with the Catalogue kept whole down to the repeated names. Thersites's portrait trips aloud, and one word is too slangy.

## Issues
- `seg-0018` · read-aloud · medium
  > a man with no measure to his tongue, his head full of words, reckless ones, set against the kings, in no good order, anything he thought would make the Greeks laugh.
  Five tacked-on phrases with no main verb after the first clause. Read aloud, it loses the thread (Butler: "a man of many words, and those unseemly; ... who cared not what he said, so that he might set the Achaeans in a laugh").
  Fix: "Thersites kept on, a man with no measure to his tongue. His head was full of words, reckless and disorderly, for picking fights with kings — anything he thought would make the Greeks laugh."

- `seg-0021` · register · low
  > that foul-spoken blowhard
  "Blowhard" is American slang. The Greek is "word-flinging abuser".
  Fix: "that foul-mouthed slanderer"

- `seg-0001` · fidelity · low
  > Now the other gods and the charioteers of the plain slept the night through
  The Greek is "the other gods and men, the chariot-fighters". "Men" drops out and "of the plain" is added.
  Fix: "Now the other gods and the men who fought from chariots slept the night through"
