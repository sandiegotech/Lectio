# The Iliad · Book XXI · The Battle at the River
Grade: B
Very strong. Lycaon's death and the river's revolt are close and powerful. Only a few casual idioms and one awkward phrase.

## Issues
- `seg-0025` · register · low
  > So you run, Far-Shooter, and hand Poseidon the win outright
  "The win" is sports-page usage. The same slip is in XVII.
  Fix: and hand Poseidon the victory outright
- `seg-0026` · register · low
  > turned on Artemis in anger and let her have it
  "Let her have it" is too casual next to Hera's formal rebuke.
  Fix: turned on Artemis in anger and rebuked her
- `seg-0014` · read-aloud · low
  > my own dear mother, who lied me to sleep with promises
  The coinage trips. The source says she beguiled him with lies.
  Fix: my own dear mother, who soothed me with lies
- `seg-0030` · read-aloud · low
  > If I run from mighty Achilles where the others are running, the rout, he'll catch me all the same
  The appositive "the rout" stalls the sentence.
  Fix: If I run from mighty Achilles where the others are running in rout, he'll catch me all the same
- `seg-0029` · register · low
  > saw monstrous Achilles below
  "Monstrous" now suggests wickedness. The source means huge or gigantic.
  Fix: saw giant Achilles below
- `seg-0013` · form · low
  > clearing the débris from the ditch
  The accented spelling is out of step with the edition's plain American spelling.
  Fix: clearing the debris from the ditch
