# The Iliad · Book XIII · Poseidon at the Ships
Grade: B
Strong, close and vivid throughout; one gloss written into the text, and a few small register and read-aloud fixes.

## Issues
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · medium
  > the proud Hippemolgi who milk their mares, and the Abians, the justest men alive, who live on milk and shed no blood
  "Who milk their mares" explains the name, and "shed no blood" is invented. The milk-drinking also belongs to the Hippemolgi, not the Abians (Butler: "the noble Hippemolgi, who live on milk, and the Abians, justest of mankind").
  Fix: the proud Hippemolgi, who live on milk, and the Abians, the justest men alive.
- `seg-0017` · register · low
  > Meriones, his sturdy aide
  "Aide" sounds like a political staffer. It recurs in seg-0025 ("him and his aide") and seg-0038. The house word for *therapon* elsewhere is squire or companion.
  Fix: Meriones, his sturdy squire (and the same in seg-0025 and seg-0038).
- `seg-0038` · read-aloud · medium
  > bound the hand up in a twist of well-spun wool, a sling the aide of that shepherd of the people carried
  The relative clause is inverted and stacks two titles, so it trips when read aloud.
  Fix: bound the hand up in a sling of well-spun wool, which his squire carried for him, shepherd of the people.
- `seg-0030` · register · low
  > Strange man, come stand and face me yourself
  "Strange man" (for *daimonie*) means nothing to a modern ear. Ajax uses the same address in seg-0047 ("You strange man, come closer").
  Fix: Fool, come stand and face me yourself / seg-0047: Madman, come closer.
- `seg-0010` · read-aloud · low
  > I would not pick a quarrel with a man who hung back from the war because he was weak — but you, I am furious with.
  The inverted ending loses its stress.
  Fix: … because he was weak — but with you I am furious.
- `seg-0045` · register · low
  > Paris, you misery, fine to look at, woman-mad, smooth talker
  "You misery" is not idiomatic English, and "smooth talker" is a little slangy for *seducer*.
  Fix: Paris, curse of a man, fine to look at, woman-mad, seducer.

## Best lines
- seg-0029: "Even on his way down to the strong gatekeeper, Hades, his heart will be glad: I have given him an escort."
- seg-0039: "A man can take his fill of all things — of sleep, of love, of sweet song and the faultless dance … But the Trojans are insatiable for battle."
