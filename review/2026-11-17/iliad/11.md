# The Iliad · Book XI · The Day of Agamemnon
Grade: B
A long, crowded book carried with momentum and good similes. Nestor's cattle-raid is whole and readable. One taunt reads as its opposite.

## Issues
- `seg-0032` · read-aloud · medium
  > Far-famed Odysseus, never filled with cunning or with toil
  The Greek means "insatiable of tricks and toil". "Never filled with cunning" will be heard as "never cunning", the reverse of the taunt.
  Fix: "Far-famed Odysseus, who can never get enough of tricks and toil"

- `seg-0026` · register · low
  > Here comes great Hector, rolling down on us — we're for it.
  "We're for it" is British slang. The Greek is "this disaster is rolling down on us two."
  Fix: "Here comes great Hector — disaster rolling down on us."

- `seg-0018` · form · low
  > their bellies grey with dust
  British spelling. The edition uses "gray" everywhere else.
  Fix: "their bellies gray with dust"

## Best lines
- "So he fell there and slept the sleep of bronze, poor man, killed defending his own people, far from the bride he had wed and never had joy of"
- "That is the man I was among my people, when I was a man."
