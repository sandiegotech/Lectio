# The Iliad · Book VI · Hector and Andromache
Grade: B
The best-loved book is handled with real care; Glaucus's leaves and the farewell at the gate both land. Fix Helen's kinship line and the formula that chills Hector's tenderness.

## Issues
- `seg-0025` · fidelity · medium
  > Brother — brother of a hateful bitch of a woman, who brings on this horror.
  Hector is Helen's brother-in-law (the Greek is "brother-in-law of me, a scheming, chilling bitch"). As written, Hector is the brother of some third woman, and Helen's self-accusation is lost on first hearing.
  Fix: "Brother — my husband's brother, brother to me, a scheming, hateful bitch who brought on all this horror —"

- `seg-0033` · voice · low
  > You strange woman — don't grieve too much for me in your heart.
  The edition renders Homer's *daimonie* as "You strange man/woman" every time (Zeus to Hera, Hector to Paris twice, Andromache to Hector, here). In a scolding it works. In Hector's gentlest moment, just after "he stroked her with his hand", it reads cold.
  Fix: "My poor love — don't grieve too much for me in your heart."

- `seg-0015` · read-aloud · low
  > That is the blood and the line I claim to be from.
  A famous line ends on a dangling preposition and loses its stress.
  Fix: "That is my line, and that is the blood I am proud to claim."

## Best lines
- "The generations of men are like the leaves of the trees."
- "With that he placed his son in the arms of his wife, and she took him to her sweet-smelling breast, smiling through her tears."
