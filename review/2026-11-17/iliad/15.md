# The Iliad · Book XV · The Battle at the Ships
Grade: B
Close and forceful, and the great set pieces hold. One clause reverses who is fighting whom; the rest is polish.

## Issues
- `seg-0032` · fidelity · high
  > The Greeks held firm against the Trojans' attack, and though the Trojans were the fewer, they could not drive the Greeks back from the ships
  The subject is reversed. The Greeks could not drive the Trojans, who were fewer, back from the ships. As written, the next clause ("the Trojans could not break the Greek line") just repeats this one.
  Fix: The Greeks held firm against the Trojans' attack, but though the Trojans were the fewer, the Greeks could not drive them back from the ships.
- `seg-0046` · fidelity · medium
  > so the Greeks were thrown into godlike panic by Hector and father Zeus
  "Godlike panic" mistakes the adverb, which means "wondrously" or "terribly" (Butler: "the Achaeans were utterly panic-stricken").
  Fix: so the Greeks were thrown into a wild, unearthly panic by Hector and father Zeus.
- `seg-0046` · fidelity · low
  > From that poor father came a far better son
  "Poor" reads as "pitiable", but the source means "worse" or "baser". The point is the contrast in worth.
  Fix: From that far meaner father came a far better son.
- `seg-0014` · register · low
  > not try to frighten me as if I were some nobody. Let him save his bullying for the sons and daughters he fathered
  "Some nobody" and "bullying" are a shade too casual in Poseidon's mouth.
  Fix: as if I were a coward. Let him keep his threats for the sons and daughters he fathered.
- `seg-0036` · register · low
  > rattling the empty car
  "Car" for chariot again (see XIV).
  Fix: rattling the empty chariot.
