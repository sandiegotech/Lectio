# The Iliad · Book XXII · The Death of Hector
Grade: B
A great book, carried with full force: Priam's plea, the chase, the scales and Andromache's lament. One speech scrambles its grammatical person, and one patronymic misleads.

## Issues
- `seg-0017` · read-aloud · medium
  > You missed. So godlike Achilles did not know my fate from Zeus after all — though you spoke as if he had. You silver-tongued trickster
  Hector switches from "you" to "godlike Achilles … he" and back within three sentences, so the listener loses who is meant. The source addresses Achilles throughout (Butler: "You have missed your aim, Achilles, peer of the gods").
  Fix: You missed, godlike Achilles. So you did not know my fate from Zeus after all — though you spoke as if you did. You silver-tongued trickster
- `seg-0021` · fidelity · medium
  > Not if Priam, son of Dardanus, told them to give your weight in gold
  Priam is Laomedon's son. Book XX gives the line, and the source has *Dardanides*, "of the line of Dardanus". The same rendering is in XIII seg-0027 and repeatedly in XXIV.
  Fix: Not if Priam, of the line of Dardanus, told them to give your weight in gold
- `seg-0002` · register · low
  > when many another man would still be biting the dust before he reached Ilium
  This is the literal image, but in modern English it reads as a cowboy cliché. It also appears in XIX seg-0005.
  Fix: when many another man would still have gone down into the dust before he reached Ilium

## Best lines
- seg-0005: "There's no whispering with him now, soft and intimate, from oak tree or rock, the way a young man and a girl whisper to each other"
- seg-0028: "poor woman, she did not know how far he was now from any bath, how Athena had brought him down beneath the hands of Achilles."
