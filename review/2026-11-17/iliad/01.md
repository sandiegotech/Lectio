# The Iliad · Book I · The Quarrel
Grade: B
Strong, fast, recognizably Homer; one real cut (Thetis and Briareus) and a handful of clauses that drift.

## Issues
- `seg-0033` · fidelity · high
  > Remind him: you once saved him, alone among the gods, when the others wanted to bind him.
  The whole Briareus episode is gone: Hera, Poseidon and Athena as the would-be binders, Thetis summoning the hundred-handed Briareus (Aegaeon to men), the gods' fear at his side (Butler seg-0036). Achilles' retelling to Thetis is also collapsed into a summary dash-list ("the priest, the plague, the quarrel, Briseis carried off") and his opening "You know it; why tell you what you know?" is dropped.
  Fix: restore in full: "...when the others wanted to put him in chains: Hera, Poseidon, and Pallas Athena. You came, goddess, and freed him from the chains; you quickly called the hundred-handed one to high Olympus, the one the gods call Briareus and men Aegaeon, stronger even than his father. He sat down beside the son of Cronus, glorying in his strength, and the blessed gods were afraid and did not bind him. Remind him of that now..." and give Achilles' retelling at least in compressed speech, opening "You know. Why tell you what you already know?"

- `seg-0026` · fidelity · high
  > A scepter-bearing king, whom Zeus has glorified, never shares in the common honor.
  Inverts the sense: Nestor says such a king has never had an *ordinary* share of honor, i.e. his honor is above the rest (Butler: "no man who ... wields a sceptre has like honour with Agamemnon"). "Never shares in the common honor" reads as if he lacks it.
  Fix: "A king who holds the scepter, to whom Zeus has given glory, has never had an ordinary share of honor."

- `seg-0026` · fidelity · medium
  > And I was of their company, and they listened to my advice.
  Drops Nestor's boast that he came from far-off Pylos at their summons, fought on his own, and that "no man living now on earth could fight them" (Butler: "Not a man now living could withstand them"). Theseus son of Aegeus is also missing from the list (a line many editors bracket, but Butler keeps it).
  Fix: "I came from Pylos, far off, because they summoned me, and I fought on my own account. No one alive on earth today could fight those men. And they listened to my advice and heeded my word."

- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > Maybe a vow, or a sacrifice, will buy us back from ruin.
  Achilles asks whether Apollo blames them for a vow or a sacrifice left unpaid, and whether the savor of lambs and goats might move him to lift the plague; the line turns the question into a remedy and drops the lambs and goats.
  Fix: "whether he blames us for a vow or a sacrifice, and whether the smoke of lambs and choice goats might move him to keep ruin from us."

- `seg-0027` · fidelity · medium
  > You gave her; take her back.
  Imperative where the Greek is a bitter statement: you (Greeks) who gave her are taking her away (Butler: "those that take were those also that gave").
  Fix: "You gave her, and now you take her away."

- `seg-0018` · voice · medium
  > You shameless, scheming greed —
  Addressing a man as an abstraction ("greed") does not land aloud; Homer has "you clothed in shamelessness, mind set on gain."
  Fix: "You, wrapped in shamelessness, always out for gain —"

- `seg-0047` · read-aloud · medium
  > and no one's hunger went unsatisfied at the shared feast — not for the gorgeous lyre Apollo held, nor for the Muses
  The "not for" construction attaches the lyre to hunger and garbles the line. "Gorgeous" is also a touch fashionable.
  Fix: "and no one went without a share of the feast, or of the beautiful lyre Apollo held, or of the Muses, who sang in turn with their lovely voices."

- `seg-0013` · read-aloud · low
  > while I'm alive and have my eyes
  Awkward for "while I live and look upon the earth" (Butler: "while I yet live to look upon the face of the earth").
  Fix: "while I live and see the light of day"

- `seg-0017` · fidelity · low
  > Don't try it, clever as you are, Achilles
  The Greek is "brave as you are, godlike Achilles" (Butler: "valiant though you be"); "clever" shifts the taunt.
  Fix: "Don't try it, Achilles, brave and godlike as you are"

- `seg-0018` · fidelity · low
  > and I go back to my ships with some small thing
  Drops "small but dear" — the point is that what little he gets, he values.
  Fix: "and I go back to my ships with some small thing I'm glad of"

- `seg-0015` · read-aloud · low
  > you've never spoken or carried out a single kind word
  "Carried out a word" trips.
  Fix: "you've never once said a good word, or made one come true."

- `seg-0044` · register · low
  > lay my untouchable hands on you
  "Untouchable" now suggests immunity; the sense is hands no one can resist.
  Fix: "lay my irresistible hands on you"

- `seg-0012` · fidelity · low
  > who had guided the Greek ships to Troy by his prophecy
  Drops that the gift of prophecy was Apollo's, and "dear to Zeus" in Calchas's address to Achilles.
  Fix: "by the gift of prophecy Phoebus Apollo had given him" and "Achilles, dear to Zeus, you tell me..."

## Best lines
- "and he came on like nightfall."
- "A king who feeds on his own people"
