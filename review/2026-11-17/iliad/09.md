# The Iliad · Book IX · The Embassy to Achilles
Grade: B
The great speeches are whole and alive, including Odysseus's quiet omission of "I am the greater king" and Phoenix's full story. A few phrasings need fixing: one archaic greeting, one tangled line, mismatched quote marks.

## Issues
- `seg-0019` · read-aloud · medium
  > I hate that man like the gates of death who says one thing and hides another in his heart.
  The relative clause lands on "death", so the most quoted line of the speech trips.
  Fix: "I hate like the gates of death the man who hides one thing in his heart and says another."

- `seg-0014` · register · medium
  > “All hail — and welcome. You must come on some pressing need.
  "All hail" is stage archaism. Achilles' greeting is warm and plain: "Welcome, you come as friends; there must be great need."
  Fix: “Welcome, both of you. You come as friends — there must be some great need.

- `seg-0018` · form · low
  > ‘My son, strength is a thing Athena and Hera will give you ... honor you the more.'
  Inner quotations open with a curly ‘ and close with a straight '. The same happens in seg-0037 (‘Zeus the wide-thunderer ... over her,' he said, ‘and her people have taken heart.').
  Fix: close with ’ in both places.

- `seg-0009` · read-aloud · low
  > than the one I have carried in me since the day — yes, royal as you are — that you went into the hut of Achilles
  The parenthesis reads as sarcasm. The Greek is a plain respectful address ("Zeus-born").
  Fix: "since the day, my king, when you went into the hut of Achilles"

- `seg-0016` · register · low
  > and the chine of a hog rich with fat
  "Chine" again (also Book VII), a word few readers carry.
  Fix: "and the fatty back of a hog"

## Best lines
- "But a man's life cannot be raided back, cannot be seized or bought, once it has crossed the barrier of his teeth."
- "A man who longs for the horror of war among his own people is a man without clan, without law, without a hearth."
