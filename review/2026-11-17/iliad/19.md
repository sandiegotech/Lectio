# The Iliad · Book XIX · The Rage Laid Down
Grade: B
Excellent: Agamemnon's Ate speech, Briseis's lament and Xanthus's prophecy all land. One overstated wound and a few biblical echoes to strip out.

## Issues
- `seg-0004` · fidelity · medium
  > Coon, son of Antenor, had run him through with a spear in the battle
  This overstates the wound. In Book XI, Coön stabbed Agamemnon in the forearm (Butler: "had wounded him").
  Fix: Coon, son of Antenor, had wounded him with a spear in the battle.
- `seg-0013` · register · low
  > I will not take the name of the gods in vain
  This is the wording of the Commandment, and it brings the Bible into Agamemnon's mouth. The source says only that he will not swear falsely before a god.
  Fix: and I will not swear falsely before the gods.
- `seg-0012` · register · low
  > his spirit may be willing, but his body fails him before he knows it
  This echoes the Gospel's "the spirit is willing". The thought is Homer's, but the phrasing points elsewhere.
  Fix: however much his heart wants to fight, his limbs grow heavy before he knows it.
- `seg-0022` · read-aloud · low
  > Sigh broke from him after sigh
  The word order is inverted, and it trips.
  Fix: Sigh after sigh broke from him.
- `seg-0027` · register · low
  > leapt into the car
  "Car" for chariot (recurring).
  Fix: leapt into the chariot.

## Best lines
- seg-0021: "the women wailed with her — for Patroclus to all seeming, but each of them in truth for her own griefs."
- seg-0025: "the sheen of it leapt up into the sky, and the whole earth laughed under the flash of bronze"
