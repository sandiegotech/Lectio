# Genesis · Chapter 2 · The Garden
Grade: B
Pilot text, and it reads beautifully; "side", "a partner who matches him", and the man's poem all follow the charter. One curt rendering weakens the warning the serpent will later contradict.

## Issues
- `seg-0005` · fidelity · medium
  > The day you eat from it, you die.
  Hebrew *mot tamut* is an emphatic future (KJV "thou shalt surely die"); bare present "you die" loses both the emphasis and the tense, and with them the echo of the serpent's *lo mot temutun* in 3:4 ("You will not die").
  Fix: "The day you eat from it, you will surely die."
- `seg-0007` · form · low
  > And the man said: “This one, at last — bone of my bones, flesh of my flesh. This one will be called Woman, for from Man she was taken.”
  The pilot sets this as two verse lines (the first poem in the Bible); the edition runs it into prose. Charter: Hebrew poetry is set as verse.
  Fix: break after "flesh of my flesh." so the couplet prints as two lines, as in the pilot.

## Best lines
- "And the LORD God built the side he had taken from the man into a woman, and brought her to the man."
- "And the two of them were naked, the man and his wife, and they felt no shame."
