Mechanical checks: every KJV verse in chapters 1–34 falls inside a Vernacular segment's spine range (no gaps or overlaps); every age and number in the genealogies of chapters 5 and 11 matches the base.

## Recurring patterns

1. **Hebrew poetry printed as prose.** No segment in the edition has a line break, so every embedded poem runs as a paragraph: the man's couplet (2:23), the judgments (3:14–19), Lamech's song (4:23–24), Noah's oracle (9:25–27), the twins oracle (25:23), and Isaac's two blessings (27:27–29, 39–40). The charter says verse is set as lines and parallelism is protected above all; the pilot itself sets 2:23 as verse. This is one fix applied in several places, and it may need a segment-kind or line-break convention in the pipeline, not only new wording.
2. **Glosses settled inside the sentence.** In a handful of places the text quietly picks one side of a known crux and prints it as if it were the plain text: "cypress" for unknown *gopher* (6:14), "Abram gave him a tenth" (14:20), "and lived" from an emendation (16:13), God's unfinished sentence in 3:22 smoothed into a rule. Each is small, but the charter keeps such choices in the notes, where this edition is otherwise scrupulous.
3. **Load-bearing words dropped or made smaller, mostly in the pilot chapters.** "signs" (1:14), "and your pregnancies" (3:16), "you will surely die" turned into "you die" (2:17), "the gate of heaven" / "God of heaven" made "the sky" (28:17, 24:3), *hesed* as "kept faith" against the charter's "kindness" (21:23). Five of the six medium issues are in chapters 1–3 and 28. Chapters 1–3 are the pilot text the charter names as the register standard, so fixes there should be made in the pilot too.

## Verdict

The translation of chapters 1–34 is very nearly ready to ship. It is faithful and plain, and it sounds like one writer throughout. The Hebrew narrative texture the charter asks for (the "And" chains, the verbatim refrains, the repeated key words) is kept with real discipline, and the hardest scenes (Sodom, the Binding, the stolen blessing, Jabbok, Dinah) are some of the best writing in it. There are no high-severity issues. The six medium fixes are each a phrase long. The book is not ready to ship as a whole, though, for two reasons: chapters 35–50 do not exist yet, and the embedded poetry needs to be set as verse before a reader sees it. After the medium fixes and the verse setting, chapters 1–34 can go to the founder's read-through. KJV should stay the default edition until that read is done and the book is complete.
