# Nicomachean Ethics · Book II · Virtue as a Mean
Grade: B
A clear, confident Book II that reads aloud well. The one serious fault is terminology: *sōphrosynē* becomes "self-control" and *kalon* becomes "fine", which breaks with Book I and blurs the temperance and continence distinction the work depends on.

## Issues
- `seg-0003`, `seg-0008`, `seg-0009`, `seg-0010`, `seg-0013`, `seg-0014`, `seg-0015`, `seg-0023`, `seg-0026`, `seg-0037`, `seg-0038` · fidelity · high
  > The man who abstains from bodily pleasures and is glad to do so is self-controlled; the man who abstains but resents it is not.
  This uses "self-controlled" for *sōphrōn*, but in Book I (seg-0036 and 0037) and Book III the edition uses "temperate" for *sōphrōn* and keeps "self-control" for *enkrateia*. As written, seg-0010 says the person who resents abstaining is not self-controlled, when that person is the very type Book VII calls self-controlled (*enkratēs*). Chase keeps them apart: "Self-Mastery" against "self-control".
  Fix: change every *sōphrosynē* and *sōphrōn* in this book to "temperance" and "temperate": "is temperate; the man who abstains but resents it is not"; "temperance and courage are destroyed by excess and by deficiency"; "the mean is temperance, and the excess is dissipation"; "the temperate man looks dissipated next to the insensible man", and so on. Leave "self-controlled and even-tempered" in seg-0005 only if it renders *sōphrones*, and change it to "temperate" there too.
- `seg-0010`, `seg-0012`, `seg-0039` · voice · medium
  > the fine, the advantageous, and the pleasant ... because of pain that we shrink from what is fine
  Book I uses "noble" for *kalon* throughout ("noble and just", "noble actions"), and Book II switches to "fine" without warning. A reader following "the noble" as a thread loses it.
  Fix: use "noble" here as in Book I: "the noble, the advantageous, and the pleasant"; "shrink from what is noble"; "rare, and praiseworthy, and noble".
- `seg-0016` · fidelity · low
  > envy, joy, love, hatred, longing, jealousy, pity
  *Zēlos* is emulation (Chase has "emulation"), the wish to match someone, not jealousy. Placed beside "envy" it reads as a repeat.
  Fix: "longing, emulation, pity".
- `seg-0021` · fidelity · low
  > "Good in one way only, bad in every way."
  *Pantodapōs* means "in all sorts of ways". "Bad in every way" reads as "wholly bad", which is a different claim.
  Fix: "Good in one way only, bad in all sorts of ways."
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · low
  > becomes a kind of insensible blank
  "Blank" is added color; the Greek is just "an insensible sort of person". The added word also gets in the way of the term "insensible", which seg-0026 coins.
  Fix: "becomes insensible, as it were."

## Best lines
- "Anyone can get angry — that is easy — and anyone can give away money or spend it; but to do these to the right person, in the right amount, at the right time, for the right reason, and in the right way — that is not something anyone can do, and it is not easy." (seg-0039)
- "the feeling for pleasure has grown up with us all since infancy, which makes it hard to scrape away, dyed as it is into our very lives." (seg-0012)
