# Nicomachean Ethics · Book X · Pleasure and the Contemplative Life
Grade: B
A fine close to the work: the pleasure argument is crisp, and the contemplative life is given the lift it deserves. This is the only book carrying stray Markdown emphasis, and *kalon* slides into "honor" again in the closing chapters.

## Issues
- `seg-0003`, `seg-0004`, `seg-0005`, `seg-0006`, `seg-0007`, `seg-0008`, `seg-0015`, `seg-0016`, `seg-0019`, `seg-0022`, `seg-0023`, `seg-0024`, `seg-0025`, `seg-0028` · form · medium
  > no one ever asks what a man is pleased *for*
  There are 21 Markdown emphasis spans (`*for*`, `*every*`, `*not*`, `*is so*`, `*quality*`, `*become*`, `*be*`, `*body*`, `*those*`, `*in a place*`, `*play*`, `*have*`, `*this*`, `*is*`, `*actions*`, `*wish*`, `*do*`, `*him*`, `*he*`, `*the*`). The app does not render Markdown, so readers will see literal asterisks. No other book of the edition, and no other edition in the bundle, uses them.
  Fix: remove the asterisks. Where the stress matters, carry it in the wording, e.g. "no one ever asks what a man is pleased for — pleasure is taken to be worth having in itself"; "and the thing that is so is what strikes the good man as being so"; "for a man to toil and suffer all his life in order to play".
- `seg-0028`, `seg-0030` · fidelity · medium
  > lawgivers should call on the sense of honor to urge men toward virtue ... the good man, who lives by a sense of honor, will heed reason
  The Greek is "for the sake of the noble" (*tou kalou charin*) and "living with a view to the noble" (*pros to kalon zōnta*). "Honor" is a different motive, and one the work ranks lower (Book III, citizen courage). This is the same substitution as Book III seg-0029.
  Fix: "lawgivers should urge men toward virtue for the sake of what is noble ... the good man, who lives with his eye on the noble, will heed reason". Seg-0028 may keep "a sense of shame" for *aidōs*: "not by nature obedient to a sense of shame but only to fear".
- `seg-0011` · fidelity · medium
  > Pleasure completes the activity not as a settled state already in it, but as a kind of bloom laid over the top
  Aristotle's phrase is "as a supervening end", *epiginomenon ti telos*, with the bloom as the comparison. Dropping "end" loses the claim, and "laid over the top" sounds like icing.
  Fix: "Pleasure completes the activity not as a settled state already in it, but as an end that supervenes on it, like the bloom of youth on those in their prime."
- `seg-0003`, `seg-0026`, `seg-0030` · fidelity · low
  > he was thought a model of self-command ... having lived with self-command ... a life of self-command and endurance
  This is *sōphrosynē* again, under a fourth English name (Book II "self-control", Book III "temperance", Book VI "self-mastery"), while seg-0021, 0024 and 0025 here say "temperate man".
  Fix: "he was thought a man of exceptional temperance"; "having lived temperately"; "a life of temperance and endurance".
- `seg-0020`, `seg-0022`, `seg-0023`, `seg-0027` · voice · low
  > Call that best thing intelligence
  *Nous* is "intuitive understanding" in Book VI and "intelligence" here. The shift can be defended for the divine element, but it should be signaled once.
  Fix: "Call that best thing intellect (the faculty we called intuitive understanding)", then "intellect" through to seg-0027.
