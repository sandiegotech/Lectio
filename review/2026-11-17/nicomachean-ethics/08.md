# Nicomachean Ethics · Book VIII · Friendship
Grade: B
Faithful and largely graceful, and the strongest book so far on keeping terms steady (*teleia* is "complete" here, as it should be in Book I). A few sentences go stiff or knotted, mostly in the closing chapters on complaints.

## Issues
- `seg-0015` · read-aloud · medium
  > People tied otherwise are friends in the way that something good and some likeness arises to them
  This does not parse aloud, and the point is lost: the lesser friendships count as friendships only so far as they hold something good and some likeness to the true kind.
  Fix: "The others are friends only so far as there is something good in their bond, and some likeness to the true kind."
- `seg-0045` · read-aloud · medium
  > One should act, that is, as having made a mistake at the outset in taking a kindness from one from whom one should not have taken it — he being no friend, and not acting disinterestedly — and should discharge oneself as though one had received a kindness on stated terms.
  It is a long, stacked "one ... one ... oneself" sentence in a stiffer register than the rest of the work, and it trips at "from one from whom one".
  Fix: "You should behave as if you had made a mistake at the start, by accepting a kindness from someone you should not have taken it from — someone who was no friend and was not acting for your sake — and settle the matter as if the kindness had been given on stated terms."
- `seg-0044`, `seg-0045` · read-aloud · low
  > when people contract and discharge obligations in different keys
  The musical image is not in the Greek ("not in the same spirit"), and it makes the reader stop to decode it.
  Fix: "when people take on an obligation in one spirit and settle it in another"; seg-0045 "because he does not get it settled in the same spirit in which he entered into it."
- `seg-0044` · register · low
  > those who have dealt on trust must abide the outcome.
  This is a leftover archaism.
  Fix: "those who have dealt on trust must live with the result."
- `seg-0012` · voice · low
  > for as the proverb says, men cannot know each other until they have eaten the proverbial salt together
  The proverb is flagged twice in one sentence.
  Fix: "until they have eaten the salt together".

## Best lines
- "It would be absurd to say a man wishes his wine well; if anything, he wishes it kept sound — for his own use." (seg-0007)
- "A wish for friendship comes quickly; friendship itself does not." (seg-0012)
