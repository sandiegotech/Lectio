# Nicomachean Ethics · Book VII · Self-Control and Pleasure
Grade: B
The argument comes through cleanly, from the practical syllogism to "unimpeded activity". But the book's central pair of terms is rendered so that the self-indulgent man and the man lacking self-control sound like the same person, which is exactly the distinction the book exists to draw.

## Issues
- `seg-0001` to `seg-0024` (throughout) · fidelity · high
  > Some people run together the man with no self-control at all and the merely weak-willed man
  In this book *akratēs* is variously "the man without self-control" (seg-0003), "the man who fails of self-control" (seg-0004) and "weak-willed" (most segments), while *akolastos* is "the man with no self-control at all" (seg-0003, 0012, 0016, 0017, 0020, 0022, 0029, 0032, 0034). "Without self-control" and "with no self-control at all" are nearly the same English for the two characters Aristotle is separating, and Book III has already named the *akolastos* "self-indulgent". Seg-0022 shows the damage: "temperance is treated as the opposite of the utter lack of self-control alone".
  Fix: *akolastos* = "self-indulgent", *akolasia* = "self-indulgence", in every segment (as in Book III). *Akratēs* = "the man who lacks self-control", *akrasia* = "lack of self-control", used consistently. "Weak-willed" may stand only as an occasional variant, and only after the term is fixed. For example, seg-0003: "Some people run together the self-indulgent man and the man who lacks self-control; others keep them apart."
- `seg-0002`, `seg-0018` · fidelity · medium
  > along with its near relations softness and self-indulgence ... is soft and self-indulgent
  This is *tryphē*, luxury or pampered softness, but "self-indulgence" is the edition's word for *akolasia* (Book III), so it collides with the term above.
  Fix: "softness and luxuriousness"; "is soft and pampered".
- `seg-0003` · form · medium
  > Those are about the common opinions.
  This is a garbled closing sentence.
  Fix: "Those, roughly, are the common opinions."
- `seg-0012` · read-aloud · medium
  > So we should count the man more wholly given over to his passions who chases excessive pleasures and flees moderate pains while feeling little or no real desire, than the man driven by overwhelming desire
  This does not parse. The Greek says such a man is *more* self-indulgent.
  Fix: "So we would sooner call self-indulgent the man who chases excessive pleasures and flees moderate pains with little or no desire, than the man who does so from violent desire".
- `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > pulling out one's hair, gnawing one's nails, eating coal or dirt. Where nature is truly the cause, no one would call a man weak-willed
  The Greek list continues with sexual relations between males, and the next clause compares "women, for being passive in intercourse". Chase silently cuts these (marked by an ellipsis), and the edition has inherited the cut without any mark. A gloss-free modern edition should restore them plainly or flag the omission.
  Fix: "pulling out one's hair, gnawing one's nails, eating coal or dirt, and sex between men. Where nature is the cause, no one would call such people lacking in self-control, any more than one would call women so because they are passive rather than active in intercourse".
- `seg-0019` · fidelity · low
  > like those who tickle themselves first to be ready for it
  The Greek is "as those who tickle others first are not tickled themselves".
  Fix: "just as people who tickle first are not tickled themselves".
- `seg-0002`, `seg-0003`, `seg-0007`, `seg-0008`, `seg-0009` · voice · low
  > We'll come back ... we'll have done enough ... isn't using it ... don't use it
  Contractions are scattered through a book otherwise written without them.
  Fix: spell them out.
- `seg-0035` · fidelity · low
  > This is why God enjoys a single, simple pleasure forever
  *Ho theos*: the capital brings in a monotheist reading (same issue as Book I seg-0012).
  Fix: "This is why the god enjoys a single, simple pleasure forever".

## Best lines
- "So he is like a city that passes all the right laws and has excellent statutes but acts on none of them" (seg-0023)
- "Better to say 'the unimpeded activity of a natural state' — 'activity' for 'process,' and 'unimpeded' for 'perceived.'" (seg-0028)
