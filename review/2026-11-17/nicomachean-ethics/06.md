# Nicomachean Ethics · Book VI · Practical Wisdom
Grade: B
Clear and well argued, with memorable phrasing ("knowledge, with its head on"; "this eye of the soul"). The intellectual-virtue vocabulary needs tightening, and the book's central term does not match the promise Book II made.

## Issues
- `seg-0001`, `seg-0018` · fidelity · medium
  > the mean is fixed by the right principle. It is time to spell out what that principle is.
  Book II (seg-0006) promised "later we will say what right reason is". This is that payoff, but *orthos logos* now reads "right principle", so the reader cannot see the promise being kept.
  Fix: use one phrase in both books. Here: "the mean is fixed by right reason. It is time to spell out what right reason is." Carry the same phrase through seg-0018 ("in accordance with right reason").
- `seg-0005` · fidelity · medium
  > craft and the reasoned state apt for making true things are the same.
  The truth is in the reasoning, not in the things made (*hexis meta logou alēthous poiētikē*). The same segment's later wording is right.
  Fix: "craft is the same as a state apt for making that involves true reasoning."
- `seg-0019` · fidelity · medium
  > no choice can be right without practical wisdom and goodness of character, since the one supplies the right end and the other makes us do what leads to it.
  Read in order, "the one" is practical wisdom, which reverses the division seg-0016 states: character sets the end, wisdom the means.
  Fix: "since goodness of character supplies the right end, and practical wisdom makes us do what leads to it."
- `seg-0013`, `seg-0014` · fidelity · medium
  > by which we call people understanding or not ... both the first terms and the last are grasped by understanding and not by reasoning.
  "Understanding" renders *synesis* in seg-0013, while *nous* is "intuitive understanding". Seg-0014 then uses bare "understanding" for *nous*, so the two faculties merge just where they are being distinguished. Calling a person "understanding" also now suggests "sympathetic", not "intelligent".
  Fix: seg-0013 "by which we call people men of understanding or not". Seg-0014 "both the first terms and the last are grasped by intuition, not by reasoning", and use "intuitive understanding" or "intuition" consistently for *nous* in the rest of the segment.
- `seg-0006`, `seg-0018` · fidelity · medium
  > It is also why we give self-mastery the name we do in Greek
  This is *sōphrosynē*, which is "temperance" in Book III, "self-control" in Book II, and now "self-mastery". The etymology joke only lands if the reader knows which virtue is meant.
  Fix: "It is also why temperance has the name it does in Greek, a word that means 'what preserves practical wisdom'"; seg-0018 "justice, temperance, and courage".
- `seg-0012` · voice · low
  > The weak-willed person, or the bad one
  *Akratēs* is "the man who lacks self-control" in Books III and V. "Weak-willed" is a third label.
  Fix: "The person who lacks self-control, or the bad person".
- `seg-0004`, `seg-0009` · voice · low
  > philosophic wisdom
  Book I (seg-0037) names *sophia* "theoretical wisdom".
  Fix: choose one term and use it in both books.
- `seg-0010` · read-aloud · low
  > or about anything that has no end, and an end that is some good attainable by action.
  The clause trails off and loses its structure.
  Fix: "or about anything that does not lead to an end — an end that is some good attainable by action."

## Best lines
- "So philosophic wisdom is intuitive understanding combined with scientific knowledge — knowledge, with its head on, of the highest things." (seg-0009)
- "We should therefore pay as much attention to the undemonstrated sayings and opinions of experienced and older people, and of the practically wise, as to demonstrations — for experience has given them an eye to see rightly." (seg-0014)
