# Nicomachean Ethics · Book IV · The Virtues of Character
Grade: B
Strong, readable, often vivid; the great-souled man in particular is a pleasure. What needs fixing is key terms that do not match the books around this one, plus a few slips of wording.

## Issues
- `seg-0001`, `seg-0008` · fidelity · medium
  > nor in the matters that show self-control ... most of them have no self-restraint at all
  Seg-0001 has *sōphrosynē*, which the edition renders "temperance" in Book III. Seg-0008 has *akolastoi*, which Book III renders "self-indulgent". "Self-control" belongs to *enkrateia*, as this book itself uses it correctly in seg-0027.
  Fix: "nor in the matters of temperance"; "That is why most of them are self-indulgent".
- `seg-0003`, `seg-0004`, `seg-0008`, `seg-0011`, `seg-0013`, `seg-0018`, `seg-0019`, `seg-0022`, `seg-0023` · voice · medium
  > Every action that accords with excellence is fine and done for the sake of what is fine.
  *Kalon* is "fine" throughout this book but "noble" in Books I and III, so a reader cannot follow the motive that Book III made the heart of courage. This book is at least consistent within itself, but the work is not.
  Fix: settle one word for the whole work ("noble" is what the edition mostly uses) and apply it here: "is noble and done for the sake of what is noble", and so on.
- `seg-0020`, `seg-0021` · voice · medium
  > Mildness is the mean concerning anger. ... we give the name mildness to the one in the mean
  Book II (seg-0031) has already named this mean "even temper", and its excess "hot temper". Here they become "mildness" and "irascibility", so the reader meets the same virtue under two names.
  Fix: "Even temper is the mean concerning anger ... we give the name even temper to the one in the mean"; "this person is even-tempered"; "sometimes we praise people deficient in anger and call them even-tempered".
- `seg-0026` · register · medium
  > the other with the rest of social intercourse.
  For a fifteen-year-old reader, "intercourse" now has one sense.
  Fix: "the other with the rest of social life."
- `seg-0013` · read-aloud · medium
  > he lavishes much and shows off out of taste
  "Out of taste" is not idiomatic and stops the reader. The Greek is "makes a show out of tune".
  Fix: "he lavishes much and makes a tasteless show".
- `seg-0009` · fidelity · low
  > The cheat at dice, the man who strips the dead, the highway robber
  *Lōpodytēs* is a clothes thief (at the baths), a petty thief, not a stripper of corpses. "Strips the dead" makes him a ghoul, which the argument about small gains does not support.
  Fix: "The cheat at dice, the petty thief who steals clothes, the highway robber".
- `seg-0015` · read-aloud · low
  > since honor is what the great think themselves chiefly worthy of, and at a certain rate.
  "At a certain rate" is opaque. The Greek is *kat' axian*, "in proportion to their worth".
  Fix: "and worthy of in proportion to their worth."
- `seg-0003` · fidelity · low
  > since people are likelier to hold tight to their own than to reach for what isn't theirs
  The comparison is off. Aristotle says people are more reluctant to give up what is theirs than to refrain from taking what is another's.
  Fix: "since people part with their own less readily than they refrain from taking what is another's."
- `seg-0013` · fidelity · low
  > dresses the actors in purple for the opening scene
  It is the comic chorus, at its entrance.
  Fix: "brings the chorus on in purple at their entrance".
- `seg-0024` · register · low
  > he comes off as a fool rather than a knave.
  "Knave" is leftover archaism.
  Fix: "he comes off as a fool rather than a scoundrel."
- `seg-0023`, `seg-0025` · voice · low
  > the false-modest person
  Book II (seg-0033) names this extreme "irony, or self-deprecation" and "self-deprecating", while seg-0017 here also says "self-deprecating".
  Fix: "the self-deprecating person" throughout.

## Best lines
- "standing tall over the great is not ill-bred, while doing it to humble folk would be crude, like flexing your strength against the weak." (seg-0017)
- "So the refined and gentlemanly man is, as it were, a law to himself." (seg-0026)
