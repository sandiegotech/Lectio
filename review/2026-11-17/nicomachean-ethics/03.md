# Nicomachean Ethics · Book III · Choice, Courage, and Temperance
Grade: B
Lively, concrete and complete, but courage is made to act "for honor" where the Greek says "for the noble", and the voice slips into a chattier, contraction-heavy register than Books I and II.

## Issues
- `seg-0029`, `seg-0033`, `seg-0035` · fidelity · high
  > Honor, then, is what moves the brave man to stand fast against terror
  The Greek is *tou kalou heneka*, "for the sake of the noble", as the same segment says two sentences earlier. Seg-0032 then defines citizen courage as courage *for honor* and ranks it below true courage, so rendering *kalon* as "honor" here collapses the very distinction the chapter draws. Seg-0033 ("brave from a sense of honor") and seg-0035 ("act from a sense of honor", twice) make the same substitution.
  Fix: seg-0029 "It is for the sake of the noble, then, that the brave man stands fast and does what courage demands." Seg-0033 "a man should be brave because it is noble, not under compulsion." Seg-0035 "the truly brave act because it is noble" and "not because it is noble and not as reason directs".
- whole section · voice · medium
  > it's ... don't ... we've ... here's a stronger point ... Well, ... on down the line ... flat out
  Books I and II use no contractions and a measured, even tone. Book III is full of contractions and spoken asides, so it sounds like a different writer. The register itself is not wrong, but the work has to sound like one writer.
  Fix: remove the contractions throughout and cut the conversational fillers ("Well,", "here's a stronger point" becomes "And there is a stronger point:", "on down the line" becomes "and so on", "flat out" becomes "outright").
- `seg-0018` · register · medium
  > Same with bitter and sweet, hot and heavy, and the rest.
  "Hot and heavy" is a current idiom with a sexual sense, and read aloud it will draw a laugh.
  Fix: "So too with what is bitter or sweet, heavy or hot, and the rest."
- `seg-0008` · fidelity · medium
  > We don't usually blame a drunk man's or an angry man's ignorance for what he does; we blame the drink or the anger.
  Aristotle is assigning the cause of the act, not blame. "Blame the drink" sounds like an excuse, and seg-0021 says the drunk pays a double penalty.
  Fix: "A man who is drunk or angry is thought to act not because of ignorance but because of his drink or his anger; yet he acts not knowingly but in ignorance."
- `seg-0022` · fidelity · medium
  > the unjust man and the man without self-control could have avoided becoming what they are
  The Greek has *akolastos*, the self-indulgent man (the same segment says "self-indulgent man" two sentences earlier), not the *akratēs*. This repeats the work-wide blur between temperance and self-control. In the same segment, "undisciplined" is a third word for *akolastos*.
  Fix: "the unjust man and the self-indulgent man could have avoided"; "make themselves unjust or self-indulgent by doing wrong".
- `seg-0029` · read-aloud · medium
  > The brave man isn't thrown off balance — only so far as he's human, of course.
  As punctuated, this sounds like a limit on his courage. The Greek means he is as undaunted as a human being can be.
  Fix: "The brave man is as undaunted as a human being can be."
- `seg-0046` · register · medium
  > no one craves to become an addict.
  "Addict" is modern clinical vocabulary, and it changes the claim. The Greek is "no one craves to be self-indulgent."
  Fix: "since no one craves to become self-indulgent."
- `seg-0010` · read-aloud · medium
  > Take it as true and, first, no animal but a human being would ever act voluntarily
  This trips; the conditional is buried.
  Fix: "If that were so, then first of all no animal but a human being would ever act voluntarily, and neither would children."
- `seg-0003`, `seg-0018`, `seg-0019` · voice · low
  > for no fine end ... fine and pleasant things ... doing the fine thing
  *Kalon* is "noble" everywhere else in this book, sometimes in the same segment (seg-0003 has "great and noble" and then "no fine end").
  Fix: "for no noble end"; "noble and pleasant things"; "doing the noble thing".
- `seg-0017` · fidelity · low
  > choice is a deliberate reaching for something in our power
  This is the textbook definition, *orexis bouleutikē*, "deliberate desire". "Reaching" loses the word readers will look for.
  Fix: "choice is a deliberate desire for something in our power: we deliberate, then judge, then desire in line with what we deliberated."
- `seg-0035` · fidelity · low
  > and dissolute men do plenty of bold things out of lust.
  The Greek has *moichoi*, "adulterers", which is concrete and sharper.
  Fix: "and adulterers do plenty of bold things out of lust."
- `seg-0040` · fidelity · low
  > Next, temperance — self-mastery — which seems
  This is a doubled gloss inside the sentence, and it adds a third name to a term the work already struggles to hold steady.
  Fix: "Next, temperance, which seems".

## Best lines
- "Once you've thrown a stone, you can't call it back — and yet the throwing was up to you" (seg-0022; without the contraction)
- "Foreseen dangers you can face by calculation; the sudden ones you face from the kind of person you've become." (seg-0036)
