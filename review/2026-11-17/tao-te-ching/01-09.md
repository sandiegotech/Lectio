# Tao Te Ching · Chapters 1–9
Edition: lectio-modern · compared against legge-1891 and the received Chinese text (no original bundled).

## Chapter 1 · The Way and the Name
Grade: A
Faithful, clear, and the famous opening is intact.

### Issues
(none)

### Best lines
> The Way that can be spoken is not the eternal Way. The name that can be named is not the eternal name.

## Chapter 2 · The Mutual Arising of Opposites
Grade: B
Good pairs passage, but one clause of the sage's conduct is dropped.

### Issues
- `seg-0004` · fidelity · high
  > They are brought to life, and the sage claims nothing as his own. The work is done
  為而不恃 is missing: Legge has "they go through their processes, and there is no expectation (of a reward)" between ownership and completion.
  Fix: "They are brought to life, and the sage claims nothing as his own. He acts, and does not lean on what he has done. The work is done, and he does not rest in it as an achievement."
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · low
  > This is how it always is.
  Not in Legge or the received text (it is the Mawangdui reading 恆也); a variant slipped in without the base's support.
  Fix: drop the sentence, or keep it only if the edition adopts Mawangdui readings consistently.
- `seg-0005` · fidelity · low
  > the credit never leaves him
  "Credit" explains 不去 ("it does not leave"); Legge keeps it bare ("the power not cease").
  Fix: "It is just because he does not rest in it that it never leaves him."

### Best lines
> the long and the short measure each other; the high and the low lean on each other

## Chapter 3 · Keeping the Heart Unstirred
Grade: B
Mostly sound, but a misused idiom and two softenings blur the political point.

### Issues
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · high
  > so that the clever ones never dare to force their hand
  "Force someone's hand" means to pressure another into acting; the text (使夫智者不敢為) says the clever dare not act. Legge: "to keep them from presuming to act."
  Fix: "so that the clever never dare to act."
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · medium
  > free of cunning and free of craving
  無知 is "without knowledge"; "cunning" moralizes it into something easier to accept. Legge: "without knowledge and without desire."
  Fix: "He keeps the people without knowledge and without craving,"
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · low
  > Do not make a show of honoring the worthy
  "Make a show of" is added; 不尚賢 is simply "do not exalt the worthy" (Legge: "Not to value and employ men of superior ability").
  Fix: "Do not exalt the worthy, and the people will not compete with one another."

### Best lines
> he empties their minds and fills their bellies, weakens their ambition and strengthens their bones.

## Chapter 4 · The Bottomless Well
Grade: B
Strong images, but the text's hedges ("seems," "as if") are removed, making the Way sound asserted rather than glimpsed.

### Issues
- `seg-0001` · fidelity · medium
  > It is so deep — the wellspring from which the ten thousand things flow.
  淵兮似萬物之宗: "deep, it seems the ancestor of all things." The "seems" is dropped and "ancestor" becomes a new metaphor. Legge: "as if it were the Honoured Ancestor of all things."
  Fix: "It is so deep — it seems to be the ancestor of the ten thousand things."
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · medium
  > and yet it is always there
  湛兮似或存: "it seems perhaps to be there." The text hedges; "always there" asserts. Legge: "as if it would ever so continue."
  Fix: "So still, so hidden, it only seems to be there."

### Best lines
> It blunts the sharp edges, unties the knots, softens the glare, settles down with the dust.

## Chapter 8 · Like Water
Grade: B
Faithful, but the list of seven opens on a phrase that trips.

### Issues
- `seg-0002` · read-aloud · medium
  > In where it dwells, it holds to the ground.
  "In where" is ungrammatical and stops the reader at the head of the list.
  Fix: "In dwelling, it holds to the ground."

### Best lines
> The highest good is like water.

## Chapter 9 · Knowing When to Stop
Grade: A
Faithful; one opening clause has awkward tense.

### Issues
- `seg-0001` · read-aloud · low
  > Keep filling a cup to the brim, and you would have done better to stop in time.
  The shift to "would have done" muddles the tense of a plain maxim (持而盈之不如其已).
  Fix: "Better to stop in time than to fill a cup to the brim."

### Best lines
> When the work is done and your name is rising, withdraw. This is the Way of heaven.
