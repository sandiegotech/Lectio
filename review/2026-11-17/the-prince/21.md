# The Prince · Chapter XXI · How a Prince Wins Esteem
Grade: A
Accurate where the comparison text goes wrong ("no example could be more wretched" for "miserabile"), with a lively Roman legate and a clean maxim on choosing among bad courses. Two polish points.

## Issues
- `seg-0003` · read-aloud · low
  > and you will have no defense to offer and no reason anyone should give you one, and no one will take you in.
  Three "and" clauses pile up, and the middle one is hard to parse; the source is a single phrase, nothing to defend you or take you in.
  Fix: "and you will have no claim, and nothing, to defend you or give you shelter."
- `seg-0007` · register · low
  > and no one afraid to open a business for fear of the taxman.
  "The taxman" is breezy and modern; "taglie" are levies imposed by the prince.
  Fix: "and no one afraid to open a trade for fear of taxes."

## Best lines
"Prudence consists in knowing how to weigh the troubles against each other, and to take the least bad as good."
"keep out, and without thanks and without dignity you will be the winner's prize."
