# The Prince · Chapter XVIII · How Princes Should Keep Their Word
Grade: A
Exact and memorable: the fox and the lion, "the eye rather than the hand," and the turn into evil "when he has to" all come through with their edge. Two word choices to tune.

## Issues
- `seg-0001` · register · low
  > This point was taught to princes under cover by the ancient writers
  "Under cover" now suggests spying; the source "copertamente" means in veiled, figurative form.
  Fix: "This point was taught to princes in veiled form by the ancient writers"
- `seg-0001` · register · low
  > but because they are wretched, and would not keep their word to you
  As in XVII, "tristi" is wicked or base, not miserable; "wretched" invites pity where Machiavelli means contempt.
  Fix: "but because they are wicked, and would not keep their word to you"

## Best lines
"So one must be a fox to recognize the snares, and a lion to frighten off the wolves."
"not to depart from good while he can, but to know how to enter into evil when he has to."
