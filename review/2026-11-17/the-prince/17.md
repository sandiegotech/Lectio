# The Prince · Chapter XVII · Cruelty and Mercy: Loved or Feared
Grade: A
The book's most famous chapter is rendered exactly and with force; the catalogue of men's faults and the bought-friendship line are as good as any English version. Polish only.

## Issues
- `seg-0003` · form · low
  > Res dura, et regni novitas me talia cogunt moliri, et late fines custode tueri.
  Virgil's two hexameters (Aeneid I.563–64) are run together on one line; verse should keep its break after "cogunt."
  Fix: break the line: "Res dura, et regni novitas me talia cogunt / moliri, et late fines custode tueri."
- `seg-0004` · fidelity · low
  > Hard necessity, and the newness of my kingdom, force me to such measures — to guard my borders far and wide with sentries.
  The English rendering is the editor's, not Machiavelli's, and sits in the text as if it were his. It is a help to the reader, but it should be marked as a translation (set apart or italicised as an editorial gloss) or moved to the margin, consistent with how other quotations are handled.
  Fix: keep the wording, but present it as an editorial translation of the Latin rather than as running text.
- `seg-0006` · register · low
  > for love is held by a chain of obligation which, men being wretched, is broken at every turn
  "Tristi" means wicked or base; to a modern ear "wretched" first means miserable, which softens the charge into pity.
  Fix: "which, men being wicked, is broken at every turn of their own advantage"

## Best lines
"for friendships bought at a price, and not with greatness and nobility of spirit, are paid for but not owned, and at the moment of need they cannot be spent."
"for men forget the death of a father more quickly than the loss of a patrimony."
