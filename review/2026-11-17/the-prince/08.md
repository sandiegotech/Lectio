# The Prince · Chapter VIII · Rising by Crime
Grade: B
Faithful and chilling, with the Fermo banquet told at full tension; one word ("militia") is used in a sense today's reader does not carry.

## Issues
- `seg-0002` · register · medium
  > after entering the militia, he rose through its ranks to be Praetor of Syracuse
  "Milizia" is soldiering, the military life; to a modern reader "the militia" is a citizen reserve or an armed irregular band, which is wrong for Agathocles' career. The same word recurs in `seg-0003` ("up through the ranks of the militia").
  Fix: "after taking up soldiering, he rose through its ranks" / "up through the ranks of the army"
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · low
  > he lived a criminal life at every stage of his career
  The source is "per li gradi della sua età," every stage of his life; "career" narrows it and anticipates the soldiering that follows.
  Fix: "he lived a criminal life at every stage of his life" or "from boyhood on, he lived a criminal life"
- `seg-0006` · read-aloud · low
  > converted into the greatest usefulness to the subjects that they can be
  The tail "that they can be" trails off and buries the point.
  Fix: "and turned, as far as possible, to the subjects' benefit"

## Best lines
"And yet it cannot be called virtù to kill your fellow citizens, betray your friends, and be without faith, without pity, without religion. Such methods can win empire, but not glory."
"For injuries should be done all together, so that, being tasted less, they offend less; benefits should be handed out little by little, so that they are savored longer."
