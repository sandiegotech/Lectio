# The Prince · Chapter XXVI · Exhortation to Free Italy
Grade: B
The exhortation rises as it should, and the closing questions and "this barbarian rule stinks" keep their heat; but "virtù" dissolves into four different English words just where the book calls back to Moses, Cyrus, and Theseus, and the Petrarch is run together as prose.

## Issues
- `seg-0002` · fidelity · medium
  > for the power in Moses to show itself ... the excellence of Theseus to appear — then now, for the worth of one Italian spirit to be known
  Each is "virtù" in the source, and Chapter VI rendered the same three examples with "virtù." The edition then gives "ability" (`seg-0004`, "con la fortuna e virtù sua"), "military virtue" (`seg-0006`), and "strength" (`seg-0007`, twice) for the same word. The callback to VI and the book's central term are lost in its final pages.
  Fix: "for the virtù of Moses to show itself ... the virtù of Theseus to appear — then now, for the virtù of one Italian spirit to be known"; likewise "with its fortune and its virtù" (`seg-0004`), "as if military virtù were spent" (`seg-0006`), "Here there is great virtù in the limbs" and "by virtù or by fortune" (`seg-0007`).
- `seg-0007` · fidelity · low
  > The first witness is Il Taro; then Allesandria, Capua, Genoa, Vaila, Bologna, Mestri.
  "Allesandria" is a misspelling carried over from the 1908 text (Machiavelli: "Alessandria"); "Vaila" also differs from "Vailà" in XX.
  Fix: "then Alessandria, Capua, Genoa, Vailà, Bologna, Mestri."
- `seg-0011` · form · low
  > Virtù contro al Furore prenderà l'arme, e fia il combatter corto: ché l'antico valore negli italici cuor non è ancor morto.
  Petrarch's four lines (Italia mia, 93–96) are run together as prose; the English rendering in `seg-0012` is likewise unlined and reads as Machiavelli's own text rather than the editor's translation.
  Fix: restore the line breaks ("Virtù contro al Furore / prenderà l'arme, e fia il combatter corto: / ché l'antico valore / negli italici cuor non è ancor morto.") and line the English the same way, set apart as an editorial translation.

## Best lines
"See how ready she is, how willing, to march behind a flag — if only someone would raise one."
"What doors would be shut against him? What people would refuse him obedience? What envy would set itself against him? What Italian would withhold his homage? To every one of us this barbarian rule stinks."
